## Why PREVIOUS V2 does not read like the reference

1. **It tells the lie with the wrong body parts.** V2 relies on yaw (trunk twist over the hips, head yaw) and a clavicle drop. From the front these are small rotations of an upright body. The reference tells it with **forward flexion plus a lateral lean of the whole trunk**, the head going down with it. That changes the silhouette.
2. **The arms never leave the running cycle.** The asymmetric counterweight shape, the clearest single cue in the reference, is missing.
3. **The free leg gives the exit away.** It swings forward toward the ball during the lie, where the reference's hangs behind, unloaded. At V2's strongest instant the free knee already points at the exit, so the freeze-frame test fails.
4. **The body never commits its momentum.** Forward speed barely falls (3.2 → 2.6 m/s), so the velocity still says "forward". In the reference it falls to about 0.8 m/s while the body moves toward the fake: for an instant the player could leave that way.
5. **The ball sits in the wrong place.** In V2 it sits in front of the body and is then knocked out. In the reference it rolls on the exit side and the body leaves it, then collects it. The ball's position is part of why the release is quick.
6. **The timing is late.** V2's keys put the maximum lie just before the touch, and the hips return with the touch. In the reference the lie peaks just after the plant, and the hips are already returning while the upper body still sells.

**PREVIOUS V2's timing skeleton is close to the reference's:** false-step plant → touch is 0.32 s against 0.28 s, and the exit is under way at about +0.35. So the rebuild keeps the support skeleton and the decisive touch. It moves the lie into flexion, lean, head and arms. It also changes:
- the free leg's path;
- the brake;
- where the ball is placed;
- the relative timing.
