In one paragraph. Your hypothesis holds. The tackling boot was SP1's kickstand and its brake: 71 % of SP1's braking came from it, as a heel strike followed by studs dragging. The reference shows a different entry: the trailing leg lands first, then the outer thigh and hip take over, and the tackling boot never plants. The force-plate literature agrees.

SP2 rebuilds the ground entry around that mechanism:

  • The folded leg lands first, on the side of the knee (1.3–2.6 kN over 29 ms; the literature gives 1.0–2.3).
  • The lateral thigh (3.2–4.4 kN) and the hip take over within about 0.1 s.
  • The tackling leg stays extended low. It only skims on its heel or upper (≈ 0.1 BW, 4–6 % of the support), and its studs never engage.
  • The body keeps organised without a kickstand. A pelvis-orientation objective works through the folded hip while it is on the turf.
  • It slides 1.0–1.8 m from first contact (SP1: 0.45 m), slowing progressively, deterministically, from 4.25 to 5.5 m/s.
  • Obstructions are handled by tactile, regional yielding, which escalates to a whole-body fallback only when regions are overpowered.

What is not solved:

  • The folded hip is pressed 4–8° past its adduction limit for about 1 s while the body sits on it.
  • The lateral knee still carries about half of the glide at 5 m/s.
  • SP2 fails at 5.75 m/s and above. The real tackle is a 9 m/s sprint: the clip is 0.4× slow motion.
  • Against the unchanged, standing C3 the responses are graded by geometry (passes, absorbed, displaced-recovered, slow fall), but there is no fast sliced-out fall, and the recipient looks rigid. The reference's dribbler was mid-stride on one foot, which the standing C3 cannot be.

1 · The reference, and what it actually shows

Contact sequence (manual annotation, ±0.04 s real; MediaPipe could not be used at this resolution):

  1. The last running plant (−0.12 s).
  2. The trailing leg's instep and lower shin reach the turf with the foot still on its plant spot (0 s).
  3. The knee follows (+0.04 s) and the shin lies flat.
  4. The outer thigh, then the hip / buttock (+0.10 to +0.24 s).
  5. The hand posts (+0.25 s).
  6. He is lying on his side by +0.30 s.

The tackling boot is in swing while the shin lands. It never plants: it skims within ≈ 0.1 m of the turf, reaches turf level at the ball, and its knee goes from 95° to straight in 0.2 s.

Speeds: ≈ 9 m/s through a 0.25 s knee-glide, then the side-lying slide decelerates at 2.5–3.3 m/s² (μ ≈ 0.3) for 7.5 m. He gets up while still moving.

The dribbler steps onto the slider's shin 40 ms after the ball is played. That foot is swept forward and up; he is seated 0.16–0.22 s and on his back 0.30–0.36 s after contact.

3 · Phase 2: the isolated slide (fixtures)

  • F1: pitches onto its face. Friction under a knee 0.55 m below the centre of mass is ≈ 200 N·m of forward torque.
  • F2: slams the pelvis first.
  • F3: slides at the Coulomb rate (4.8 m/s², 1.8 m) but rolls over onto its front.
  • F3R: 11 of 12 held postures roll over. Only the strongly reclined one with the hand posted stays organised (2.0 m at 5.0 m/s²).

Lesson: a sliding body needs its centre of pressure ≈ μ·h ahead of its centre of mass. A held posture is marginal, so the slide needs feedback, not a pose.

8 · What needs your decision

  1. Receiver. The decisive missing piece is a running, single-support recipient with protective stepping and arms. The reference's dribbler was swept mid-stride, while C3 is a standing controller. Building one needs a preregistered successor (stop condition B). I did not modify C3.
  2. Sprint slides. The real tackle is ≈ 9 m/s. SP2 is qualified for 4.25–5.5 m/s from a 5 m/s entry. Going faster needs a speed-indexed commit and a sprint plant state from RUN.
  3. Fold-hip adduction. Side-sitting on the folded thigh presses the hip 4–8° past V2's 35° adduction stop. Either the slide posture changes, or the hip's passive end-range is re-examined for this loading.
  4. Adoption. M1c and TCF-1 are both needed by SP2. Neither has been regression-tested on other domains, so I am not recommending canonical adoption yet.
