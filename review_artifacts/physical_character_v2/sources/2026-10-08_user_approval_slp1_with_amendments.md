# User approval 2026-10-08 — SLP-1 architecture approved with amendments 1–4; freeze, implement and run (verbatim)

Received in the Claude Code session of 8 Oct 2026, in reply to `slp1/SLP1_DESIGN_PREREGISTRATION_DRAFT.md`. Reproduced verbatim below.

---

Approve SLP-1 architecture with the following amendments, then freeze the preregistration and implement/run SLP-1.
The central architecture is approved: finite-force springy pelvis support, no kinematic/root lock, normal articulated rigid bodies and contacts, existing capacity-limited actuators/leg IK, autonomous balance/gait layers bypassed, and continuous support authority derived from recoverability rather than a binary collision/impulse threshold.
Preserve the proposed 21-case disturbance matrix and deterministic repeats. The genuine rigid-impactor case is particularly important: I want evidence that an actual collision propagates through the articulated body rather than merely reproducing the applied-push diagnostic.
Amendment 1 — do not block SLP-1 on external jog/run biomechanics research. SLP-1 is an architecture/physics feasibility test, not gait validation. Use the existing walking research for ~1.2 m/s where applicable. For 3 m/s and 6 m/s, use explicitly labelled provisional diagnostic cadence/step/contact timing values chosen only to produce plausible stress-test trajectories. Record their provenance/assumptions and do not treat them as production locomotion parameters. Proper evidence-based locomotion design belongs to LOC-1 after this architecture passes. If you can obtain reliable research cheaply, use it, but do not delay SLP-1 for it.
Amendment 2 — support must not cheat. Preserve the requirement that support never reads collision events, never teleports/overwrites body position or velocity, never applies forces outside its frozen caps, and is solved physically in the same step as contacts. Record support force/work throughout every run.
Amendment 3 — preserve momentum on support loss. When support authority reaches zero, do not reset or replace linear/angular velocity. The falling articulated body must inherit the actual physical state that produced loss of support.
Amendment 4 — don't optimise performance yet. The measured ~10–20% like-for-like saving is useful evidence, not a failure. Do not change Jolt's 150 solver iterations, physics frequency, passive-tissue model, IK frequency or other validated physics merely to improve the benchmark in SLP-1. Measure SLP-1 first. We will separately investigate production physics/LOD cost after the architecture is established.
For the 1.2, 3 and 6 m/s undisturbed cases, additionally report actual achieved speed, foot-contact pattern, support-force fraction versus leg-carried load, actuator saturation, joint margins, foot slip and whether the body visibly/physically remains a plausible articulated runner. Do not fail SLP-1 merely because provisional 3/6 m/s gait timing is not human-quality; distinguish architecture failure from locomotion-authoring deficiency.
For disturbances, report the complete progression rather than only pass/fail: retained support → displaced/recovering → substantially disrupted → support lost/fall, including the recoverability margin and support-authority time series. Specifically compare torso, swing-leg and stance-leg disturbances at matched impulse.
Stop immediately and report rather than tuning around the result if: the support has to become effectively rigid to track; collision response is substantially cancelled by support; ordinary undisturbed motion requires forces beyond the frozen caps; support loss produces discontinuous state/momentum; the body becomes numerically unstable; or the architecture requires changing V2 physical properties.
If SLP-1 passes its architectural purpose, do not continue into locomotion polishing, LOC-1, tackling, recovery, TD2C/E2, or any further character development. Stop and report the results to me first.
Freeze, implement and run SLP-1 now.
