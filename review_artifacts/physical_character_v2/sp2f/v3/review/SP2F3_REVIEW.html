1. What was wrong, and what the reference does

  • SP2F / SP2F2: after contact the posting hand is NOT the stable end of the chain. The turf drags it backward and outward relative to the pelvis by 0.68 / 0.76 / 0.86 m (SP2F2) while the elbow stays straight (-7…-2 / -6…10 / -7…23°) and the upper arm rises from pointing down to ≈ horizontal: a long straight lever swept back along the turf. The 120–135° "shoulder angle" was that sweep, not the torso lowering onto a planted hand.
  • The tall raised arm in the SP2F / SP2F2 rear and side views is the tackling-side arm (up / over the body since SP2D), not the posting arm.
  • REFERENCE3, 20 hand-annotated frames (evidence/REFERENCE3_arm_annotation.json): its player slides on his RIGHT side (we see his back; the posting arm is his right). His posting hand stays at a near-constant image offset beside / slightly behind the hip from the landing on; the shoulder descends toward the hand's height; the elbow bends progressively (≈ 9° → 79° in the image); the upper arm stays steep (≈ −65 … −87°) while the forearm flattens onto the turf (≈ −78° → −8°): a forearm post that the torso lowers onto.
  • The post built FROM THE HAND OUTWARD (new opt-in sp2_ctrl post.hold): at the first forearm contact the hand's horizontal offset from the pelvis is captured (translation only — the body may turn about it) and a compliant, capped spring-damper (600 N/m, 60 N·s/m, ≤ 120 N) keeps the hand there, so it slides with the player instead of being dragged toward the head. Its height is left to the turf contact and a light 60 N press.
  • The arm as an ECCENTRIC SUPPORT STRUT: an axial push (60 N) plus a damper on the shoulder's closing speed toward the hand (1600 N·s/m, ≤ 1000 N, only while the arm points upward from the hand) — it resists being compressed quickly (it manages the fall) but shortens as the torso lowers, and it does not resist the arm rotating about the hand. The elbow keeps 30 % of its stiffness toward its angle AT CONTACT (it yields progressively instead of locking straight and buckling); the shoulder has no angle target.
  • Reach: the hand goes DOWN beside / behind the hip with the elbow bent (abduction 45°, extension 35°, elbow 35°, by 0.62 s).
  • Torso: SP2F's (no in-air counter-lean release — so no folded-knee overrun) plus a gradual lean onto the planted arm after contact (lumbar −5°, thoracic −14° over 0.62 → 1.52 s); pelvis-roll objective 60° (75° rolled the trunk over the arm, onto it).
  • New: the hand-relative diagnostic (hand − pelvis, hand − shoulder, hand height, elbow, upper-arm / forearm orientation, shoulder / elbow angular velocity, hand speed, image-plane projection) and the REFERENCE3 annotation.
