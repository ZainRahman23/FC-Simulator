BALANCE-3: coordinated ankle–hip control for the P8 defect

B3 NOT QUALIFIED. Stop condition reached; C3 stays the 16 / 17 baseline.
  • Research: Alexandrov 2005's eigenmovement control (per-mode delayed PD; hip mode K_S 1.15, K_V 0.17 s, 66 ms), continuous blending, cross-coupled feedback, Zelei 2021's "free of overshoot". No joint-space gains, no integral action and no small-push sagittal overshoot data exist in the sources reached.
  • Body audit: the trunk-at-hips low-frequency stiffness is neutral (body 1.01 × gravity, controller ≈ 0). The rigid two-segment model mispredicts this body (hip torque moves the COM ≈ 7 × less), and ankle actions move the hip angle more than hip torque does.
  • B3 (frozen before any run): C3 unchanged as the ankle mode, plus a hip-mode objective on the hip angle with a COM-neutral ankle companion.
  • Result: B2F 14 / 17 (fails P6, P2, P8). P8 back small 0.34 (C3 0.287). Hip–ankle torque correlation −0.77 (fighting; C3 −0.43). The forward 12 N·s push now falls. L1 / L2 and causality pass; reruns are bit-identical.
  • Why: C3's ankle (COM) actions drive the hip angle through trunk lag, so the hip loop fights them, and it suppresses the hip flexion C3 used near its forward limit. A working modal controller would also need the COM pattern rebuilt from an identified multi-segment model, which means unsupported parameters. Per the instruction, I stopped and did not tune.
