The three biggest defects visible at 1×
  1. The tackled recipient still runs on when the tackle meets a freshly loaded boot (EARLY): the boot is not swept (friction holds it), he keeps running for ≈ 0.5 s and only goes down when the tackler's body reaches his feet. Even at the canonical phase he stumbles and pitches forward over the tackler rather than having the legs taken from under him.
  2. A pushed runner shows almost no visible reaction: BRUSH and BUMP drift 13–33 cm off line, but the stride, trunk and arms look the same as the twin's, with no visible stagger and no visible recovery step (the hip offsets are 3–7°). The recovery happens, but it is mostly the support's velocity regulation and is not readable as a recovery.
  3. The falls (STRONG push, tackles) are slow folds onto the hands and knees rather than a footballer's tumble: the body sinks and curls forward with the arms out (the protect intent) instead of carrying its running momentum into the fall.
In one paragraph. RUN-1 stays the intent and a collidable V2 body carries it. A continuous viability estimate (placement feasibility, height margin, trunk organisation; no collision-size term) with a hysteresis commitment decides how much of RUN is still executable, region by region; when RUN becomes impossible the running support and every angular term are withdrawn and an active protect intent takes over. Undisturbed running is untouched; prescribed shoulder pushes give the requested spectrum (30 N·s runs on, 60 N·s is knocked 27–33 cm off line and recovers within ≈ 1 s, 100 N·s falls after an emergency attempt, from either side); the frozen slide tackle now produces different outcomes at different support phases (unloading: absorbed; canonical: stumble and forward fall at ≈ +0.7 s; freshly loaded: the boot holds and he runs on). A fully physical runner (support only through joints and contact) was built and does not run: RUN-1 is not angular-momentum consistent and cannot turn, which makes physical support of RUN-1 a balance-controller problem outside this mission. The hybrid's angular support is therefore still the HYBRID-RUN-1 gait abstraction, now explicit and withdrawn whenever RUN is not viable.

How HAC-1 works

Continuous action viability V = min(placement feasibility of the next recovery step, height margin at that touchdown, trunk organisation), from physical state only. It falls fast and recovers slowly. A fall commitment C with a dead band (rises below 0.30, decays above 0.65) gives hysteresis. Locomotion weight = 1 − C. No term depends on the size of a collision: contact acts only through the state it creates.

Support integrity / lost support. A support foot that slips (0.8 → 2.0 m/s) or is displaced from its landing point (0.15 → 0.35 m) in the planted part of its stance stops carrying its share. The expected stance counts as lost if it was planted and then lost, or missed by more than 60 ms.

Recovery step. The free foot lands at RUN-1's own landing point + c·Δv (lateral, c = 0.15 s). It is applied as hip offsets in RUN-1's pelvis frame, ramped and rate-limited, and bounded by hip range, cross-over, the time left and reach. When support is lost, an emergency step re-times RUN-1's own touchdown pose (earliest = foot height ÷ 2.5 m/s + 60 ms). A swing-foot clearance response handles toe catches.
Regional authority. Each region (legs, trunk, arms) has a contact / error authority α and a RUN-intent weight w. Targets = slerp(PROTECT, RUN-1 + recovery, w); stiffness = max(α, tone × (1 − w)). Contacts win locally; the arms leave RUN earlier (balance / brace); a failing athlete keeps tone.

Active protect intent: chin tuck, mild trunk flexion, arms toward the fall direction, legs near-extended.

Support is HYBRID-RUN-1's gait support and pelvis moment, every term × the locomotion weight. When the fall commits, it vanishes, so nothing holds a falling athlete up. A fully physical alternative (support only through joints and contact) was built and is documented in section D; it does not yet run.

D · The unphysical pelvis force

Audit. HYBRID-RUN-1 had three non-physical angular sources: the external animation-consistency moment (≤ 450 N·m, in flight too), the external 'supported' moment (≤ 0.4 m × Fy; no foot is 0.4 m long) and the support pair's own hidden moment (+F spread over the body by mass, −F at the foot: lines of action that do not coincide, i.e. a virtual pivot at the COM — the reason HYBRID-RUN-1's foot placement could not affect balance).

Physical replacement, attempted (support.mode: "jt", HAC1_LOG §2): the stance leg's own joint torques realise the ground reaction (verified: commanded ≈ delivered); attitude through the ground reaction's direction, foot friction yaw, an angular-momentum-consistent entry, free-leg aiming, SIMBICON stance-hip control and compensation, chain transmission, a ground-limited stance-foot moment, a virtual leg, touchdown-aligned support profiles. Over 20 candidates, none ran longer than ≈ 2 s. Causes, in order of discovery: RUN-1's entry momentum spins the body; its limb motion swings yaw momentum ±4 kg·m²/s per stride and its pitch momentum averages +2.4 kg·m²/s; touchdowns come 30–100 ms early and cascade because RUN-1's clock lifts the leg before push-off ends; the ankle's inversion axis (≤ 79 N·m) rolls under the lateral ground reaction of a wide step; speed drifts to 5.5 m/s. Executing RUN-1 physically needs a SIMBICON-class balance layer or an angular-momentum-consistent intent: physics-generated locomotion, outside this mission, so I stopped rather than build it silently.

Shipped (PARTIAL). The angular terms and the support itself are multiplied by the locomotion weight (1 − C): the moment can no longer hold up a failing or swept athlete — the HYBRID-RUN-1 complaint ('after collision it becomes 3–5× more consequential') is removed for the failing case — but while RUN is viable it is still applied (logged and drawn every step).

G · Authoritative-simulation boundary

HAC-1 runs in its own Jolt world in review_artifacts/; nothing changes a match. If it decides outcomes, these must become authoritative simulation facts (not presentation): (1) the support state — which feet carry load, support integrity, lost support; (2) viability V and the fall commitment C, hence whether a player falls; (3) the resulting COM position and velocity and the fallen / protecting state; (4) the recovery step actually taken; (5) every non-contact force (the support pair and the pelvis moments are part of the gait abstraction); (6) the parameters, versioned with the simulation. Outcome neutrality: the RUN-1 intent, its clock and the physical response must be computed identically with rendering on or off — here renders only read records. The integration contract (how these facts reach the match simulation, at what rate, with what authority) is the next architectural decision and is not made tonight.

H · Remaining blockers

  1. RUN-1 is not physically executable as authored (angular momentum, yaw swing, mean pitch momentum): fully physical support of RUN-1 needs a SIMBICON-class balance layer or an angular-momentum-consistent intent. Owner decision: which, if either; until then the hybrid keeps an explicit, viability-gated gait abstraction.
  2. RUN-1 cannot turn or sidestep: a runner whose intent turns falls even without contact (also under HYBRID-RUN-1). This blocks two-runner shoulder fixtures and any change of direction under contact; turning is a locomotion (RUN-2) problem.
  3. This tackle geometry cannot take a loaded foot: boot-on-boot contact sweeps an unloading foot but a loaded one is held by friction (≈ 1.4–2 BW × 1.2). A sliced-out fall needs the contact on the leg above the foot or the tackler's body blocking the next step — tackle choices; F2/F3 were not modified.
  4. The recovery step is small and not readable: 3–7° hip offsets; the full capture-point gain destabilised normal running and was halved (tuned). A visible stagger / recovery stride needs a larger, phase-aware step and upper-body balance reactions.
  5. Fall / protect is minimal: one brace pose scaled by the fall direction; falls fold slowly instead of carrying momentum; no get-up.
  6. CPU: ≈ 0.6 ms per active character per step at 240 Hz; match scale needs fewer simultaneously active bodies, a lower physics rate or a cheaper passive layer.
