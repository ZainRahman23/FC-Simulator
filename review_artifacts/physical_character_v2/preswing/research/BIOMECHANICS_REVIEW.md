# Literature review: how a lightly loaded foot behaves in pre-swing

**What this is:** a literature review delegated to a research subagent (web search plus direct DOI / PMC retrieval), 2026-10-05, and recorded here unchanged in substance.
- Items marked "abstract" rest on abstracts only.
- Items marked **[Inference]** are the reviewer's reasoning, not reported measurements.
- The web-search budget ran out early. After that, sources were retrieved only through DOIs, PMIDs or citation graphs.

## Key findings

### 1. Trailing-foot ground reaction forces

- **Double support is short and unloading is fast.** Double support lasts about 240 ms at 2.0 km/h and about 100 ms at 5.5 km/h. The trailing limb's vertical force falls at 2.9–8.2 BW/s [11].
- **[Inference] Humans pass through the 0.1–0.5 % BW regime in about 1 ms.**
- **The horizontal force is roughly proportional to the vertical force.** Its direction is limb-aligned (CoP at the metatarsal heads → COM). Horizontal force ≈ vertical force × tan(limb angle), with a ratio of about 0.3–0.5; R² = 0.81 at peak propulsion [12].

### 2. Required friction at push-off

- **Peak values:** 0.38–0.54 when turning; 0.21–0.35 at heel contact in straight walking [1].
- **Near-zero load:** the raw horizontal / vertical ratio spikes as vertical force approaches zero, but this is treated as an artefact. Fino & Lockhart discard samples below 50 N (about 7 % BW): "a slip when there is negligible vertical GRF will not result in a noticeable movement of the COM" [1].
- **Slip risk** scales with required minus available friction [5, 6] (Redfern's review [4]).

### 3. Does the foot slide?

- **On dry ground, pre-swing is rolling about the metatarsal heads and toes (the rockers), not sliding** [31, 12].
- **Macro-slip thresholds** are > 3 cm and > 10 cm [9]. Backward toe sliding is documented only on slippery floors [10].
- **Gap:** no millimetre-scale measurement of toe translation in dry pre-swing exists.

### 4. Football studded-boot traction

- **Pivots are tested and performed on a loaded forefoot** [18, 28].
- **Rotational traction** is tens to more than 100 N·m under 450–1321 N of load [18, 20, 22, 23].
- **Translational "traction coefficients" of 1.9–2.5** at 300 N reflect stud interlock, not Coulomb friction [23]. Full stud penetration is reached at about 146 N [24].
- **Gap:** no traction data exist for a nearly unloaded foot.

### 5. Control during pre-swing

- **The limb mainly prepares swing:** gastrocnemius push-off, and hip and knee flexion [13–15].
- **There is no evidence of foot-position servoing** on the planted foot. **[Inference]** It is held by contact.
- **Friction regulation is anticipatory and whole-gait** [7]. There is no evidence of millisecond horizontal-force regulation.

## Reviewer's conclusions

**(a) Emerge vs control:**
- **Should emerge from contact physics:**
  - horizontal force and its ratio to vertical force (given the limb direction);
  - stick or slip;
  - CoP moving to the forefoot;
  - heel rise as rolling;
  - pivot resistance;
  - toe-off.
- **Should be controlled:**
  - ankle push-off;
  - hip and knee flexion for swing;
  - limb angle;
  - transfer timing.
- **[Inference]** A stiff foot-position target on a lightly loaded foot demands forces outside the friction cone.

**(b) Plausible tangential force at 0.1–0.5 % BW** (75 kg player) **[Inference]**:
- Vertical force is 0.7–3.7 N, so the tangential force is about 0.3–4 N; with a limb-aligned ratio it is about 0.2–2 N.
- The foot itself weighs about 10 N, so the leg is mostly holding it up.
- About 1 mm of slip is consistent with human data. It is below measurement resolution and 30–100× below macro-slip thresholds.
- The non-human feature is a foot *staying* at 0.1–0.5 % BW for a long time during fast body motion.

**(c) Pivots on a lightly loaded foot [Inference]:**
- Torsional friction ≈ (2/3)·μ·N·R ≈ 0.02–0.1 N·m, so such a foot swivels almost freely and cannot anchor body rotation. Turning torque must come from the loaded foot.
- Caveat: embedded studs may resist briefly through interlock. This is unmeasured and outside Coulomb friction.
