PVP-1: SP2F2 and SP2F3 against the same running recipient

14–15 Oct 2026 · local, stopped for your visual review · instruction: sources/2026-10-14_user_instruction_pvp1_running_recipient_f2_vs_f3.md · report pvp1_running/PVP1_REPORT.md · log PVP1_LOG.md · I cannot play video in real time: everything below was reviewed frame by frame.

  • Both frozen tacklers met the same running RUN-1 recipient, from the same geometry and speed. In every run the first contact is tackling boot against the loaded right boot, and the recipient's struck foot is swept toward its other foot (16–18 cm by +0.1 s, about 0.3–0.4 m by +0.2 s; its twin's foot moves 1–3 cm).
  • The recipient cannot keep running physically. Its TWIN, the identical recipient with no tackle, sinks through the stance, fails to push off, lands early on the other foot and dives forward to the turf: first non-foot contact +0.42 s after the contact time, pelvis below 0.5 m by +0.44 s. It does so with the canonical M1b muscles too.
  • So the up-and-over fall that looks like REFERENCE3 is mostly the recipient's own collapse. In all ten runs the fall starts within ±0.05 s of the twin's. The tackle changes its direction (pelvis yaw, leftward COM velocity), not whether or when it happens.

Verdicts

RUNNING RECIPIENT STATE VALIDNOThe incoming state is genuine RUN-1 running: 4.5 m/s, mid-stride, right foot loaded. The physical body takes it over 50 ms before contact with RUN-1's own pose, joint velocities, COM velocity and momentum, and its muscles start at the run's inverse dynamics. At first contact: pelvis within 0.8°, COM height within 5 mm, horizontal COM velocity within 0.1 m/s, joint RMS 6.8°. But the struck-foot load is 1.7 BW against RUN-1's 2.7, the upward COM velocity is +0.04 against +0.35 m/s, and the body cannot continue the run (its twin falls at +0.42 s). The per-foot load and the run's continuation are NOT preserved.
F2 CONTACT PHYSICS PLAUSIBLEPARTIALContacts are authoritative: no ghosting, exactly equal-and-opposite impulses, penetration ≤ 9 mm (16 mm once, when the falling recipient lands on it in LIGHT), no explosion. It keeps sliding through in every condition and loses 6–49 % of its momentum. But the recipient half of the interaction is confounded by the recipient's own collapse, and its authored raised arm reaches up into the recipient's body.
F3 CONTACT PHYSICS PLAUSIBLEPARTIALAs F2 (penetration ≤ 6 mm), with 9–43 % momentum loss. It delivers more impulse to the recipient in the central case (85 against 47 N·s over 0.6 s). Same raised-arm and recipient caveats.
F2 REMAINS RECOGNIZABLE AFTER CONTACTPARTIALIts slide and sliding post survive: post gaps ≤ 0.09 s. But its folded-leg tuck is knocked open by 29–59° in LATE / EARLY / LIGHT, its posting arm has a regional failure in LATE, and by +1 s its pelvis is 28–109° from its solo slide.
F3 REMAINS RECOGNIZABLE AFTER CONTACTPARTIALIts folded-leg tuck survives (≤ 7° in every condition). But its later, held hand post is interrupted: gaps 0.17–0.41 s in CANON / LATE / STRONG, and a regional failure of the posting arm in CANON at +0.42 s. It sits up more at contact (thorax 5° against 12° solo at +0.1 s), and by +1 s its pelvis is 10–98° from its solo slide (LATE closest).
RECIPIENT RESPONSE EMERGES WITHOUT SCRIPTINGYESNothing is authored. There is no trigger, no reaction clip and no ragdoll switch; regional failure and fallback are disabled for the recipient. Its controller is only its own run plus generic contact-driven support. What emerges, though, is mostly its own collapse.
SLICED-OUT RESPONSE OBSERVEDNOThe struck foot is swept and the recipient goes over the tackler, but its fall starts at the twin's time. REFERENCE3's attacker keeps running over the slider for about 0.5 s before he is lifted; ours goes over within 0.2–0.3 s because it was already falling.
LIGHTER RECOVERABLE RESPONSE OBSERVEDNONo condition is recoverable. The recipient cannot keep running even without contact.
FOOT PINNING PRESENTNEITHERNo sustained pin. The downward force on the recipient's feet / shanks exceeds 0.3 BW for at most 0.062 s (F3 EARLY; F2 LIGHT 0.054 s), and the struck foot moves sideways in every run: 11–21 cm by +0.1 s (EARLY F2 4 cm, then 10 cm by +0.2 s). The PVP-0 failure (tackler on top of the feet, bodies locked) does not occur.

6. What F2 does better

  • Its posting arm survives the collision. The early sliding post is already on the turf when the recipient arrives (0.04 s after first contact), and it keeps contact with ≤ 0.09 s of gaps in every condition. F3's later, held post (0.23–0.30 s after contact) is interrupted in CANON (0.21 s), LATE (0.17 s) and STRONG (0.41 s), and fails regionally in CANON.
  • It goes through more cleanly in the central, late and heel-line cases. Momentum lost: 13 / 6 / 8 % (F3: 24 / 9 / 15 %); horizontal speed at +0.3 s: 3.23 / 3.55 / 3.46 m/s (F3: 3.06 / 3.43 / 3.24; solo 3.82).
  • Its trunk keeps closer to its solo lean through the contact (21° against 25° solo at +0.1 s). F3 sits up (5° against 12°).

What F3 does better

  • Its folded-leg tuck survives being hit. Folded-knee change from the solo slide is ≤ 7° in every condition; F2's opens 29° (LATE), 46° (EARLY) and 59° (LIGHT). Its fold-hip abduction / rotation also changes less in EARLY / LATE / LIGHT (5–13° against 12–28°).
  • It removes the support more decisively. The struck foot unloads 21–29 ms earlier than the twin in CANON, EARLY and LIGHT (F2: the same as the twin, or 34 ms later in EARLY). It delivers more impulse to the recipient in CANON (85 against 47 N·s) and lifts the struck foot higher at +0.1 s (0.22 against 0.11 m).

What both get wrong

  • The authored raised tackling-side arm (up and over the body since SP2D) reaches up into the recipient's falling body; see the diagnostic view at about +0.2 s. It reads as a blocking arm, not a slide.
  • When the recipient lands on them (EARLY, LIGHT) both lose 38–49 % of their momentum, their slides end 1.4–1.8 m short of their solo by +1 s, and neither reorganises after being landed on.
  • By +1 s, in most conditions, both are far from their designed slide: pelvis 28–109° (F2) and 10–98° (F3) from solo, joint RMS 7–33°. The late slide is not the one reviewed on empty turf.
  • Neither can show a sweep followed by a recipient who keeps running over it. The recipient fails first, so 'sweep vs barrier' can only be judged in the first ≈ 0.15 s, where both sweep.

  • Placement: the tackling boot's position 0.12 s after its first fold-side turf contact (the mean of the two solo runs) is the target. The recipient is moved so its struck sole centre (+λ along the foot) is there. Its clock is aligned on the measured first contact (one iteration).

Notes

  • The recipient's body is the tacklers' V1-matched V2 body (1.90 m, 78.9 kg). The RUN-1 mapping is built on it, and both characters use the same controllers and anatomy.
  • The recipient uses M1c, the labelled fast-motion muscle candidate both tacklers use; it is not canonical and not adopted.
