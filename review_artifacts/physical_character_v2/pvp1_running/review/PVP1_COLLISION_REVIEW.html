7. The five things you asked to see (read from the frames and the tables above)

  1. What part hits what. The first contact is the tackler's tackling right boot against the recipient's loaded right boot (mid-stance), in both. The same tackling boot then also touches the recipient's swinging left boot (F2 +29…+79 ms, F3 +29…+54 ms). Later, F2's head meets the recipient's left forearm (+108…+121 ms). In F3 the recipient's left shin and boot come down onto F3's posting arm (+271…+367 ms, up to 1.2 kN).
  2. How the struck leg moves. For about 25 ms the struck foot stays planted while its load falls (1.7 → 1.0 BW in F2 and 1.7 → 0.8 BW in F3; the twin's falls 1.5 → 1.0). By +50 ms it is being pushed toward the recipient's other foot at 2.1 m/s (F2), and at 2.6 m/s and also 2.0 m/s backward (F3); the twin's moves at 0.2 m/s. It leaves the turf at about +75 ms and is carried sideways and up while the recipient's body passes over the tackler (orange trail in the diagnostic).
  3. Sweep or catch. At the contact the tackling boot slows from about 5.6 m/s to 2.9 m/s (F2) and 1.5 m/s (F3) at +25 ms: a brief catch, stronger in F3. It then speeds up again (+50 ms: 3.1 / 3.7 m/s; +75 ms: 3.6 / 4.8 m/s) and continues past the struck foot: the cyan trail runs on beyond the contact point. F2's boot rides up about 6 cm off the turf after contact; F3's stays on the turf (about 5 cm, its rest height).
  4. How the support changes. The right-foot load falls 1.7 → 1.0 (F3 0.8) → 0.4 → 0 BW over +0 → +25 → +50 → +75…+100 ms, almost the same timing as the twin's own unloading (1.5 → 1.0 → 0.4 → 0.07). The left foot is off the turf until +150 ms (F3, 1.1 BW) or +200 ms (F2, 0.1 BW); the twin's left foot is down from about +90 ms. Between about +75 and +150 ms (F3) or +200 ms (F2) the recipient has no turf support.
  5. How the recipient then meets the tackler. Between about +100 and +250 ms the recipient's body passes over the tackler's legs and thighs, then goes down beyond it (+400 ms: diving forward past the tackler). F2's head touches the recipient's falling arm at about +110 ms. The recipient's left leg lands on F3's posting arm at +270…+370 ms.
