**IMPORTANT CORRECTION TO THE CURRENT DRIBBLING WORK.**

Continue the broader TECHNICAL-CONTROL assignment, but change the fundamental **basic dribbling contact model** before polishing it further.

The current dribbling behavior is wrong in an important way:

> **The player currently looks like they are repeatedly kicking the ball left-to-right using the inside of each boot.**

That is not the elite dribbling reference we want.

Use this Messi video as a major visual reference:

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CuEF6bnoacY

Also use the football research already available.

Published observational analysis of Messi specifically identifies use of:

```text
inside
outside
instep
sole
tip
```

and finds characteristic sequences where he carries with the **outside of his preferred left foot**, continues using that foot, and takes running steps between touches.

The research also describes an important pattern:

> **inside of foot to control → outside of foot to move forward**

and associates outside-foot use with faster ball carrying.

So first fix the **fundamental carry technique**.

# 1. STOP THE LEFT-RIGHT INSIDE-FOOT PING-PONG

Normal dribbling should NOT default to:

```text
left inside
→ ball moves right

right inside
→ ball moves left

left inside
→ ball moves right

right inside
→ ball moves left
```

That creates the robotic zig-zag we are currently seeing.

Inside-foot contacts remain useful.

But they are not the universal locomotion touch.

---

# 2. FORWARD CARRY SHOULD OFTEN USE THE OUTSIDE / INSTEP REGION

For a player carrying generally forward, especially with their preferred foot, use actions more like:

```text
preferred foot
→ outside / outer-instep touch
→ ball remains slightly ahead and on preferred-foot side
→ running steps
→ same preferred foot prepares again
→ another small outside/instep touch
```

The ball does NOT need to cross the player's centre line every touch.

This is critical.

---

# 3. SAME-FOOT DRIBBLING MUST BE NORMAL

A player should be able to make:

```text
LEFT
→ LEFT
→ LEFT
→ LEFT
```

contacts naturally while carrying.

Likewise:

```text
RIGHT
→ RIGHT
→ RIGHT
```

depending on player style and geometry.

Do NOT force alternating feet merely because the other foot becomes technically available.

# 5. INSIDE AND OUTSIDE HAVE DIFFERENT FUNCTIONS

Build this into the action vocabulary.

Conceptually:

### OUTSIDE / OUTER INSTEP

Useful for:

```text
forward carry
slight outward redirection
accelerating with ball
preferred-foot repeated touches
small direction changes
```

### INSIDE

Useful for:

```text
bringing ball across body
inward correction
larger lateral redirection
setting up opposite direction
control before outside-foot acceleration
```

### INSTEP

Useful for:

```text
straighter forward push
larger stride touch
higher-speed carry
```

### SOLE

Useful for:

```text
stop
drag
pull
tight repositioning
```

Do not treat all contact surfaces as interchangeable animations.

---

# 6. BALL SHOULD HAVE A CARRY LANE

Introduce the concept of a preferred **ball carry lane** relative to the player's body.

For a left-foot dominant carrier, normal forward dribbling might keep the ball:

```text
slightly forward
+
slightly left
```

of the player's centre.

For a right-foot carrier:

```text
slightly forward
+
slightly right
```

The ball should not oscillate across the player's entire body width on every touch.

---

# 7. FOOT SELECTION SHOULD FOLLOW THE BALL LANE

If the ball is already in the player's preferred-foot carry lane:

```text
same preferred foot
```

should usually remain the cheapest/natural next action.

Do not choose the opposite foot merely because alternating feet appears symmetrical.

Human football movement is not symmetrical like that.

---

# 8. RUNNING STEPS BETWEEN TOUCHES

Do not require a ball contact on every footfall.

The research specifically observes running-step sequences between Messi's touches, including characteristic three-step patterns.

Therefore allow:

```text
touch
→ running step
→ running step
→ touch
```

or:

```text
touch
→ step
→ touch
```

depending on:

```text
speed
space
turn requirement
Dribbling
style
```

High Dribbling means the player **can** touch rapidly.

It does NOT mean they must touch on every possible frame.

---

# 9. TOUCH FREQUENCY IS CONTEXTUAL

At 99 Dribbling:

### Tight space / direction changes

```text
very frequent small touches
```

### Open straight sprint

```text
fewer touches
larger stride carry
```

### Moderate carry

```text
small preferred-foot touches
with natural running steps between them
```

This should eliminate the mechanical metronome effect.

---

# 10. DO NOT MAKE ELITE DRIBBLING A SERIES OF KICKS

The visual should feel like:

> **the player is running with the ball**

not:

> **the player repeatedly stops their running gait to kick the ball.**

Technical touches should emerge inside the locomotion cycle.

---

# 11. PREPARE CONTACT DURING THE RUNNING STRIDE

For an outside-left carry touch:

```text
right/support step
↓
left leg naturally swings through
↓
outside/outer-instep brushes/pushes ball
↓
left leg continues into running recovery
↓
next stride
```

Do not:

```text
running animation
→ freeze
→ special kick
→ running animation
```

---

# 12. CONTACT VELOCITY SHOULD BE SMALL RELATIVE TO A KICK

Most close dribble contacts are not miniature passes.

They are controlled pushes/brushes.

Author the resulting ball velocity accordingly.

Again, exact foot collision physics do not need to generate it.

The authoritative action can say:

```text
outside-left carry touch
→ ball receives small forward/right/left velocity adjustment
```

and animation explains it.

This creates actual technical logic.

# CENTRAL CORRECTION

> **Normal dribbling is not repeated left-right inside-foot kicking. The ball should usually occupy a useful carry lane relative to the player's body, with outside/outer-instep/instep touches used heavily for forward carrying and the same foot often contacting the ball repeatedly. Inside-foot touches are important for control and across-body redirection, not the universal locomotion touch.**

For elite dominant-foot dribblers:

> **Frequent small preferred-foot touches integrated into the running stride should create the "ball glued to the foot" appearance, while the support foot and body continue normal locomotion.**
