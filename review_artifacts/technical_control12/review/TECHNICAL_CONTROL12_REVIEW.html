1 · Why the old model ping-pongs, what changed

TECHNICAL-CONTROL-1 aimed every carry touch at a point on the next touching foot's side (±10 cm), took nearly every footfall and alternated feet, so most touches had to send the ball across the body — an inward impulse, which is the inside's, and the inside was the cheapest surface. The closed loop could also hand the next touch to the other foot for almost nothing.

TECHNICAL-CONTROL-1.2 (tc12_core.js, on TECHNICAL-CONTROL-1's simulation — the receive, gait, actions, Ball Control's precision, the closed loop are unchanged): a ball lane relative to the body (a dominant-foot carrier: 15 cm toward the preferred side, outside the preferred foot's own line; otherwise each foot's 10 cm lane); the foot follows the lane (the same foot repeatedly is normal; crossing the centre line costs 1.5 / 1.1 / 0.9 for a dominant-foot / preferred-foot / two-footed player, less when the intent bends toward that side, half while turning); running steps between touches by context (speed, open space, turning, style, Dribbling's cadence — a moderate carry touches every stride, an open sprint every two strides, a tight turn every footfall); surfaces by function (outside / outer instep for the forward carry, slight outward redirection, acceleration and the preferred foot's repeated touches; instep for the straighter, larger push at speed; inside for inward corrections and across the body; the sole for the actions); the actions on the lane (the outside cut toward the ball's side and the inside across the body away from it, both with the lane foot, are the plain answers; the sophisticated families must be worth their sophistication — the double touch is no longer the tight sequence's default). A new style profile: dominant-foot close-control carrier (preferred foot 0.95, outside 1.45, rhythm 0.8) — a choice layer, no attribute modifier. Ball Control → precision, Dribbling → cadence / coordination / vocabulary, Agility → body reorientation: unchanged.

The Messi footage: I cannot play video, so I could not watch it; the model follows the principles you listed and the observational research you cited (Messi's use of inside, outside, instep, sole and tip; outside-of-the-left-foot carrying sequences with running steps between touches; inside to control → outside to move forward).
