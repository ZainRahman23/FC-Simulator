Slide Contact Geometry V1.2

Touchline · branch prototype/slide-contact-v1.2 (worktree FC Simulator worktrees/slide-contact-v1.2) · built on baseline/tackled-player-v1 (f5f6076) · frozen controls baseline/outfield-runtime-v1, baseline/possession-v1.1-handoff, baseline/tackled-player-v1 untouched · local only, nothing pushed

A focused contact-geometry pass on the slide tackle and the tackled-player reactions. There are three changes. The side-on slide now uses the researched technique: he drops onto the near hip and tucks the near leg, and the far leg sweeps across toward the ball. The defender's body therefore travels beside the attacker, not through him. Every body contact during the slide's travel is now tracked in a short, bounded contact history, not only the first one. The ball's response follows from the contact the rendered boot actually makes. The tackle-quality law (WON / POKE / GLANCE / MISS) is unchanged, and so are the standing tackle, the jockey and everything outside a slide.

0 · Stop conditions

  • MET 1. The researched sweep geometry and the correct leg for the side. The near (ball-side) leg tucks and he drops on that hip; the far leg sweeps across (SWEEP 0 → 75°, BLOCK 0 → 17°). Defender on the attacker's right → tackling R, tucked L. On his left → tackling L, tucked R. Verified in world coordinates on the rendered rig (§1, §4).
  • PARTLY 2. The body travels beside the attacker. For the side-on slide along the run it does, with zero core-body overlap. Before → after, identical requests: the aimed side-on 22.7 cm → 0, sl_left 20.2 → 0, sl_win 21.4 → 7.9 cm (1 tick). Closest roots are 0.9–1.2 m instead of 0.1–0.2 m. Not yet for slides whose line converges through the carrier: the counterfactual pair 17–22 cm, sl_loose 16 cm, the head-on block 13.5 cm. There the attacker now reacts to every contact and steps around or falls over the slider, but trunk and legs still overlap for some ticks (§7, §8).
  • MET 3. The attacker responds continuously to meaningful follow-through contact. One challenge now keeps a contact history. A second leg or the seat met re-enters the balance model; failing steps that meet the slider are cut short; he goes over the slider and is caught on it; his corrective steps go around it; the slider is stopped by a body on the pitch. Examples: rx_free_leg shin → STUMBLE then planted foot → FALL; blk_front foot → ball → next step cut short → over → caught.
  • MET 4. The ball contact corresponds to the final rendered tackling foot. Side-on sweeps: the rendered boot face is −2.0 to −0.2 cm from the ball surface at the contact instant, and the normal agrees with the simulation's to within 6–8°. The patch (boot / shin) matches the simulated region. Shin contacts on the converging slides leave a 4.6–5.2 cm gap: the boot is tracked, not the shin.
  • MET 5. The deflection follows the contact geometry. A sweep drives the ball across and back (the hook), a block along the line, a poke harder along the normal. A glance keeps V1's 1 m/s, now along the normal (§5).
  • MET 6. Misses remain misses; clean ball-only tackles remain possible. Wide (1.3 m) and early slides miss with no contact at all. The left-side sweep and the aimed side-on win the ball with no body contact. The quality law and its thresholds are unchanged.
  • MET 7. Left and right are symmetric. The simulation's geometry is an exact mirror: same tick, same region, normal and ball velocity mirrored, spin sign flipped. Where the two sides differ, it is the attacker's own stride (his right foot planted on one side), which is recorded.
  • MET 8. No unrelated football outcome changes. The 60 frozen single-player scenarios, 27 squad fixtures and auto pass / receive are identical. All standing-tackle and jockey fixtures are bit-identical. Every slide fixture is identical up to its request. Games and demos diverge only after a slide request. intercept is identical when run on its own. Animation ON/OFF and determinism pass everywhere, and the tunnelling test found 0 misses (§9, §10).

1 · The leg selection: what the technique is

Sources: Human Kinetics, Soccer has three basic tackles (figure 11.17); Soccer Coach Weekly, How to coach the slide tackle; B. Christensen, Teaching the Slide Tackle (2004). All three describe the same side-on chase → drop → sweep:

  • "The tackling player chases the ball until confident that his lead foot will overtake the ball. The near leg leads, allowing the player to sink down on the left leg, which curls beneath the seat of the defender. The far leg swings around in a wide sweep, with the foot hooked, towards the ball. The tackling leg sweeps through the ball and either traps it with the hooked foot or, more usually, plays the ball away." (Human Kinetics)
  • "Approach from the side and tackle across the path of the opponent. Tackle using the leg furthest from the ball and keep it slightly bent. Tuck the leg nearest the ball underneath the backside and ideally slide on the outer thigh." Contact through the centre-to-top half of the ball. (Soccer Coach Weekly)
  • "Slide onto your hip area … on one hip." "Tackling leg even with the belly of the ball." "HOOK the ball, don't poke it away. If you have to poke it away, you are too far behind." "Gain a tiny bit of space between you and the opponent right before your slide, NOT shoulder to shoulder." (Christensen)

Your reading was right. With the defender on the attacker's right, the ball and the attacker are on the defender's left: he tucks his left (near) leg, drops onto his left hip, and his right (far) leg sweeps from his right across toward the ball.

What the game did (measured, not assumed). V1 chose foot = the ball's side, i.e. the near leg, and extended it straight along the slide from the far hip. In the rendered V1 pose he lay on his far hip with the far leg tucked. To reach a ball at the carrier's feet with a straight leg, the slide had to be aimed at the ball, and that line runs through the carrier.

World coordinates, verified on the rendered rig (the known Dribbling ofBootPlan mirror is not involved): +y is toward the camera (the near touchline draws lower on screen). A player facing +x has thigh_R at +y, which is his right as the camera sees it. The GL view matrix has determinant −1, the reflection a left-handed pitch frame needs, so _R bones render on the character's anatomical right. The simulation's right = (−fy, fx) is therefore correct, and every left/right below is in world terms.

2 · The rule, from the geometry

  • Which leg. The ball's side of the slide line, where the ball will be in 0.3 s, is the near side. That leg tucks and he drops on that hip. The far leg tackles. If the ball is within 5 cm of the line, the nearest opponent's side decides. There is no alternation and no preferred-foot rule. Recorded at the request: foot / tuck / tech, the ball's lateral offset, which side of the carrier he is on, and the approach angle.
  • Which technique. SWEEP when the ball is genuinely beside the line (≥ 0.35 m): the far leg comes across 0° → 75°. BLOCK when the ball is on the line: the same top leg comes barely across (0° → 17°) and drives the ball along the slide. Both are the researched slide (lie on the bottom hip, top leg extended). They differ only in how far the leg has to come across.
  • The sweep is a kinematic commitment, not an aim. It starts 0.04 s after the drop and takes 0.20 s. The pelvis turns with it (30% of the sweep angle), since hip rotation drives a sweep. The foot is never steered to the ball; the player's skill is the timing of the request (and the AI's aiming aid picks the direction).
  • The leg as the simulation holds it. The top hip is 0.08 m behind the root and 0.05 m to the tackling side (lying on the near hip puts it nearly over the pelvis). The leg runs knee → toe tip to 1.25 × leg length (1.16 m for a 0.865 m leg, measured on the rendered Astra leg + boot swept out on the pitch). The contact radius is 0.05 m about the leg's axis, the rendered boot's half-thickness. V1's 1.12 × leg reach and 0.08 m radius matched V1's straight near-leg pose.
  • The AI aiming aid rolls the same law forward. It wants the lower leg (knee → boot) on the ball first, and among ball-first lines prefers one whose body passes beside the carrier ("a tiny bit of space … not shoulder to shoulder"). It is an input, never part of the contact law.

3 · Diagnosis, measured before anything changed

On the V1 build (baseline/tackled-player-v1), every side-on chase with the slide along the run MISSED: the straight near leg can't reach a ball 0.6–1.05 m to the side. With the AI aiming aid, the slide was angled at the ball. It won the ball, but the defender's root passed within 0.2 m of the attacker's, and the rendered bodies overlapped for 39 ticks (head into torso 22.6 cm, torso through his thighs 20 cm). Measured on the final rendered skeletons (of_slide_geo.js), the phasing came from three causes:

  1. The slide's root trajectory. A straight near leg forces an aim at the ball, and at a carrier's feet that line goes through his legs.
  2. Body collision evaluated once. The first contact of a challenge (often foot on foot, a mild correction) was the only one. The sliding seat and trunk then ploughed through both legs with nothing re-evaluated.
  3. No collision in the slow end of the slide. A slide still moving was excluded from disc occupancy but not yet a lying capsule, so a player going back for the ball walked through the defender. During a fall's failing steps (the steps before he leaves his feet) the victim counted as "falling" and was not tested, so he ran through the defender too.

It was not the reaction starting late (reactions start on the contact tick) and not sub-stepping (see §8).

5 · Ball physics: the contact decides where the ball goes

At the first touching sub-step, the ball's new velocity follows from the contact itself:

  • the contact point and horizontal normal (leg → ball centre);
  • the leg's own velocity there: the slide's, plus the sweep's ω × distance from the hip;
  • restitution set by the authoritative outcome: WON is a hook / sweep-through that carries the ball with the boot (e 0.15); POKE is a knock away (e 0.50);
  • the leg's effective mass (the shin + foot share of his recorded weight) against the ball's 0.43 kg;
  • Coulomb-limited tangential drag (μ 0.3).

A GLANCE keeps V1's 1 m/s touch, now along the contact normal instead of the slide direction; the carrier rides it. The ball is then placed so the rest of the tick runs from the contact point with its new velocity (the keeper-contact convention). A sweep drives the ball across and back, "toward the defender's starting direction" as the coaching note says. A block drives it along the line. Spin: a ground ball rolls before (ω = v / r). The tangential impulse's spin about the vertical is recorded but not simulated, because the playtest ball has no spin state.

The rendered boot follows the leg. Its inside face is tracked, every tick of the sweep, onto the simulation leg's leading surface: the axis plus 0.05 m toward where the leg is moving. The thigh, knee and foot are solved per sweep angle so the whole boot continues the leg's line (a locked, hooked ankle). The contact-tick measurement reads the final rendered boot after every layer, including the slide pose, the alignment, the reach IK and the ground drop.

6 · Body contact through the follow-through: one challenge, a short contact history

  • First contact, exactly as V1: segment + support state + relative velocity + impulse + capture point → correction / stumble / fall.
  • Every tick of the slide's travel, the slider's parts (sweeping leg, its thigh, tucked leg, seat, reclined trunk; measured on the rendered pose) are tested against each opponent in reach. A new part of him met, or the same contact still driving into him at ≥ 1 m/s 0.1 s later, is resolved again through the same law from his state now. The stronger reaction wins; a weaker one is absorbed by the stepping already under way. A fall still in its failing steps whose next step meets the slider is cut short and starts now. The slider pays every impulse. At most 4 body resolutions per victim.
  • Still on his feet (correcting / stumbling): his corrective steps go around or over the slider. The part of his step velocity driving into the slider's seat or leg is removed (STEPS_AROUND); the capture-point steps otherwise go on as planned.
  • Falling (in the air), he goes over the slider: the slider's seat and trunk are stopped against his legs and hips, and the part of his fall travel driving into the slider is removed, so he comes down on him. On the ground, he cannot slide through the slider's body (a velocity constraint), and a slide still moving cannot pass through a body already on the pitch. Never a displacement.
  • The presentation lays a faller on the slider's rendered body where he lands on it: the pitch or the slider's seat / trunk / legs, whichever is higher, is his ground.
  • Every contact, ball and bodies, enters the challenge's history in order (≤ 12 entries: BALL, BODY with segment / planted / impulse / reaction / how it was taken, OVER, FALLER_CAUGHT, SLIDER_BLOCKED…, STEPS_AROUND, LANDED_ON, GROUND_BLOCK). Each entry carries its ball-first / simultaneous / man-first / no-ball order: the facts a future referee reads. No foul is called.

What the counterfactual says. At an identical request and identical outcome, the far-leg geometry by itself does not remove the body overlap when the slide line converges through the carrier (here 10° in from 0.6 m, a BLOCK-classified slide). Both geometries put the defender's trunk into the attacker. What B changes is the ball contact and the contact history. The rendered boot meets the ball on the leg, and the normal is known. 9 · Continuous collision: can a fast foot or a fast ball cross unseen?

Ball: V1.2 slides against loose balls crossing at 0–40 m/s at many offsets and times. Every tick is re-checked with the simulation's own within-tick kinematics (root and sweep angle linear, ball b → b + v·dt), 400 samples per tick. The simulation's sub-steps are adaptive, so neither leg nor ball moves more than 3.5 cm relative per sub-step (4 to 32, event-driven: only while a slide is live).

12 · Limitations and next

  • Limb-level overlap when bodies pile up. A faller's protective arms and the slider's bracing arm are posed, not collided. When one player lands on or beside the other, a forearm can still pass into a trunk by 5–15 cm for some ticks (the "limb" column). The gross cases are handled, with no ragdoll by design: legs through the slider, pelvis through the sliding trunk, the sweep leg through both legs with no reaction, a faller sliding through the man underneath. The next step, if you want it, is an arm-placement pass (hands avoid a body under them).
  • The terrain the faller is laid on is the slider's pose one tick earlier (the attacker is solved before the tackler in the same tick). At slide speed that is ≈ 5 cm of lag in where a leg lying on the slider rests.
  • The far-leg sweep reaches further to the side than V1's straight leg. A parallel chase at 0.6–0.9 m now wins the ball where V1 missed, and a slide angled steeply at the ball from close can now meet the man first. This is the physical consequence of the correct leg. The quality law and its thresholds are untouched. Chases wider than ≈ 1.2 m still miss.
  • The attacker's stride is not mirror-symmetric (his right foot is planted on one side at a given moment), so a mirrored chase can meet a planted foot on one side and the ball clean on the other. The tackle geometry itself is exactly mirrored (§4).
  • Ball spin is recorded, not simulated. The playtest ball carries no spin state.
  • BLOCK vs SWEEP is a threshold (ball ≥ 0.35 m beside the line at the request).
  • The V1 path is kept behind PT_DEF.slide.rule = "near" for the counterfactual only.
  • Recorded, not fixed. The Dribbling V1 ofBootPlan lateral mirror (not used anywhere in this pass).
  • Foul facts (the contact history with ball / man order) are recorded for the future referee. No call is made.
