## Part A — The receive → dribble boundary

### 1. The old exit-touch problem
RECEIVE-ELITE-1 ended every turn with an **exit touch**: a launch that sent the ball 1.6–2.0 m ahead so that the frozen DRIBBLE-V1 could take it inside its takeover contract. On RECEIVE-ELITE-1's own receive set (stationary / running / sprinting turns × 3 seeds, `evidence/tc_vs_re1.json`):

| | runs | max separation, receive → continuation (median · worst) | exit-touch launch speed (median) | CLEAN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RECEIVE-ELITE-1 · 99/99/99 | 60 | 1.70 · 2.37 m | 8.34 m/s | 60 |
| RECEIVE-ELITE-1 · Agility 50 | 60 | 1.96 · **5.67 m** | 7.84 m/s | 56 |
| RECEIVE-ELITE-1 · Agility 25 | 60 | 1.96 · 5.67 m | 7.84 m/s | 54 |

The boundary was visible: a tight receive, then a kick into space. At low Agility it was worse. A turn the body could not make with the first touch was "declined" by the dry run, and the ball went into space (5.67 m).

### 2. The receive → dribble bridge solution
There is no hand-over anymore. **One controller runs from the receive through the dribble.**
- `TechEpisode` extends RECEIVE-ELITE-1's `EliteEpisode`. RECEIVE-ELITE-1's approved receive (its first touch and its turn) is kept unchanged.
- From the first contact played on the exit onward, the same episode continues. Its next contact is a carry touch or a technical action on the gait (§13), never an exit touch.
- `TechSim.promote()` copies the elite episode into a technical one at its first action.

The result on the same 60-run set:

| | max separation (median · worst) | exit touches | CLEAN | tCont (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NEW · 99/99/99 | **0.85 · 0.87 m** | **0** | 60/60 | 0.69 s (RECEIVE-ELITE-1 0.57) |
| NEW · Agility 50 | **0.86 · 1.00 m** | 0 | 60/60 | 0.90 s |
| NEW · Agility 25 | **0.82 · 0.97 m** | 0 | 60/60 | 0.96 s |

tCont uses RECEIVE-ELITE-1's definition: the first contact played facing the exit, running along it at ≥ 2.5 m/s, with the ball ≤ 1.5 m away. It is about 0.1 s later because the continuation is now a real carry touch on the gait rather than the launch of an exit touch.

A **bridge to DRIBBLE-V1** still exists as an option (`bridge=sprint`, §26). The default is off: TECHNICAL-CONTROL-1 carries at every speed.

### 3. Removing the low-Agility fallback
The dry-run decline ("this body cannot make the turn → release the ball toward the exit") is gone:
- While TechSim runs, `TechSim.withoutDecline` temporarily patches `EliteEpisode.dryCheck`; it is restored after every call. Isolation is proven: RECEIVE-ELITE-1 is bit-identical before and after, 78/78.
- When the moving dry run fails or would need a recovery, the first touch becomes a **cushion**: the ball is kept near the body, with no direction imposed (`tech.firstExit = true`, `tech.dir0 = null`).
- The **turn is then made with the ball**: a technical action (Cruyff-like turn / drag-back) after the body has braked, at the body's own Agility.
- A ball beyond reach is **chased**, never released. The run is re-planned toward where the ball will be at a set of arrival times (0.35–1.6 s), at the speed that arrives then. A shooting correction re-aims by the miss, because the run's momentum bends it wide. The first arrival with a swing that plays the ball within reach wins.

Result: **0 failures in 2,826 sweep runs + 396 chain runs + 180 RECEIVE-ELITE-1-set runs**, including BC 1, DRI 1 and AGI 1. Lower Agility now means a slower body, more steps and a later exit (§11), with the separation unchanged (median 0.86 m at every Agility from 1 to 99).

## Part B — Human fast dribbling

### 4. Cause of the robotic fast dribbling
Measured on the receive chain at 99/99/99, with each body on its own simulation (`evidence/tc_body_ab.json`, 10 cases, posed skeleton at 240 Hz):

| | ankle acceleration in swings, p95 (median) · worst frame | IK reach error p99 (median) · worst | planted-foot slip, worst | legs fully extended |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RECEIVE-ELITE-1's body | 490 · 1386 m/s² | **32 cm** · 85 cm | 30 mm | **25 %** of frames |
| TECHNICAL-CONTROL-1's body | **243 · 826 m/s²** | **4.1 cm** · 19 cm | **1.3 mm** | **7.6 %** |

The reference is RUN-1 / TURN-1.2 running: ankle acceleration p50 84, p95 279, max 724 m/s².

The causes, each found and measured in the diagnostics:

**(a) Targets out of the leg's reach.**
- The landing lead grew with speed: 0.44 m ahead at a sprint, where this skeleton (leg 0.834 m, hip ≈ 0.87 m) reaches about 0.29 m.
- The stance foot stayed down while the body ran past it: 0.33 s at 5 m/s left it 1.2 m behind.
- The touch preparation key was fixed in world space, so 75 ms before a sprint touch it sat 0.6 m ahead of the body.
- Together these give the "locked straight leg reaching for its foot" look: a 30 cm IK error, with legs fully extended a quarter of the time.

**(b) The pelvis pulled toward the "stance" foot even in flight.**
- With both feet in the air, the weights fell back to the step cycle, and the pelvis was pulled 16 cm backward toward the trailing foot every stride.
- Along the run, this weight shift made the pelvis surge forward and back by about 1.5 m/s.

**(c) Contacts without preparation.**
- A contact swing had no stride keys between toe-off and the touch, so the foot lagged and then rushed: 0.22 s gaps between keys and 2 m of travel.

**(d) Crammed and re-timed swings.**
- Swings were shortened to meet a forced contact.
- A forced contact already inside a swing was re-applied to that foot's next swing; this produced a contact before the lift-off and a 60 ms swing with a single key.

**(e) Discontinuities at re-plans.**
- A swing the new plan had already started was popped in "in the past" while the previous body still had that foot planted (a 0.17 m jump).
- The moving receive's run-in steps were missing, so a stale "air" foot pulled the body.

### 5. The human-motion solution
`TechBody` (`tc_body.js`) reads the authoritative contract only — the motion plan (root, facing), the gait (swing windows) and the contacts (time, foot, surface, ball). It never writes the ball.
- **The stride is RUN-1's.** Each swing is keyframed (C¹ Hermite) from the frozen running gait's own swing at the running speed (`runTemplate`: r1Pose / r1FK sampled per 0.25 m/s), root-relative, with its ends corrected onto this plan's toe-off and landing.
- **A touch lives inside the swing:** toe-off → the stride's own path → preparation → contact → follow-through → landing.
  - The preparation is a backswing *relative to the body* (≤ 0.16 m against the impulse) plus the body's own travel to the contact, so at a sprint the foot comes from under the hip.
- **Landing within reach.**
  - A plain landing is at most 0.27 m ahead (RUN-1 lands 0.23–0.25 m ahead).
  - After a touch, the foot lands where its motion takes it: its follow-through plus the body's travel. It stays clear of the ball's new path and never lands behind the running stride.
- **Stance ≤ the ground the body covers over the foot (0.78 m).** The foot lifts once the body has passed it; the swing becomes longer instead.
- **Weight:** the pelvis shifts only toward a loaded foot. Sideways it shifts at every speed (the sway); along the run only slowly (`(1 − v/6)²`). There is no shift in flight.
- **Continuity.** Every re-plan inherits the previous body's foot state at that tick: position and velocity, C¹. A swing the re-plan would have started earlier starts *now*, from where the foot is.
- **The moving receive's run-in is built.** RECEIVE-CONTROL-1.1's steps keep the leg's reach: the plant lands when the body is within 0.34 m of the standing spot.
- **The rest of the body:** pelvis bob, twist and lean; trunk roll into the turn; arm counter-swing; the head on the ball in a technical sequence; per-family choreography (cut, Cruyff pivot, drag, feint dip); RUN-1 leg IK after all blends; a pelvis reach correction for planted / touching legs only.

The diagnostics cover the course and chain at 99/80/50, the styles at 90 and the lab sections (80 runs, `evidence/tc_neutral.json`):
- contact-pose error ≤ 3.9 mm;
- planted-foot slip ≤ 5.3 mm (a slow 135° pivot);
- ankle acceleration p95 median **215 m/s²**, below RUN-1's own 279; the worst single frame is 1388 m/s² (isolated, in the stop);
- IK reach error p99 median 4.0 cm, worst p99 7.0 cm;
- legs fully extended in 6.8 % of frames.

Visual checks: strips for the 90° cut, the 180° Cruyff, the feint, the drag across, the double touch, the stop, the sprint and straight carries, and the receives, in three views, plus slow-motion clips (`strips/`, `clips/slow*`). They show a plant, a crouch into the cut and a lean into the exit, with no visible snapping. The swing leg's high heel recovery at 5.5 m/s is RUN-1's own.

### 6. Technical contact cadence
A contact happens at the end of a swing: 0.075 s before that foot lands (`TC.landAfter`), or at a forced time inside it (an action).

**Rhythm** — how many steps between touches:
- close control touches every step (alternating feet) or every other step (one foot);
- a stride carry in open space touches every 3–4 steps at speed.

The style's rhythm and directness choose this, inside Dribbling's cadence limits:
- the shortest interval between alternating feet: 0.15 s (DRI 99) … 0.34 s (50) … 0.46 s (1);
- the same foot twice: 0.26 … 0.46 … 0.56 s;
- the two contacts of one action: 0.13 … 0.26 … 0.36 s.

Measured at 99/99/99 on the course:
- **1.73 contacts/s**; interval p50 0.63 s (the stride carry at 5.5 m/s); minimum 0.31 s;
- the tight left-right sequence: 0.34 s intervals, with clusters down to 0.13 s;
- the close-control technician at 90: **3.25 contacts/s**, intervals 0.31 s.

### 7. Locomotion / contact integration
- **Running speed:** the gait is RUN-1's own (`r1GaitAt`: step period Ts and duty factor) at ≥ 3 m/s when the body is not turning.
- **Turning or braking:** Agility's support-step period (`stepT` 0.18–0.30 s) with a turning duty factor of 0.46.
- **Procedural stride adjustment.** An action's contact needs a given foot at a given time:
  - that foot's swing ends at the contact, keeping the swing's natural duration (it lifts earlier or later);
  - or, when the other foot is due, that one is cut short (a stutter step), so that the foot that must play the ball is free.
  - A contact already inside a swing in progress belongs to that swing.
- **Stance is capped by the ground covered (0.78 m).** The body path is authored kinematics (`tc_motion.js`): a fixed-step integrator with the programs RUN (bend at Agility's lateral acceleration), PLANT (brake to the entry speed → redirect at Agility's rate → drive) and STOP (brake → pivot → drive), plus a feint dip, and a second-order facing follower (Agility's hip yaw rate).
- **Mid-interval intent watch.** The intent is watched every 6 ticks between contacts. When it moves, the body reacts *now* (the run bends or brakes into the plant), and the coming contact becomes the action's first one when the foot matches.

## Part C — The attributes

### 9. Ball Control scaling
Ball Control is the precision of each contact. The model is RECEIVE-KILL-1.2 B's curve (`relErr`, protected, unchanged) times the bounded keyed draws of RECEIVE-TURN-1. The error scales with the touch's difficulty, by design: easy balls are controlled by everyone.

**What was missing.** The receive model rates a dribble touch as easy (D ≈ 0.15), because the ball's speed relative to the foot is small. So at first BC changed nothing on the course: a relative error of 0.1 % at BC 99 and 0.9 % at BC 1.

**The fix.** TECHNICAL-CONTROL-1's own dribble touches get a dribble difficulty: + 0.05 per m/s of running speed, and + 0.04 per m/s of impulse imparted (`TC.ddiff`). The receive's first touch keeps RECEIVE-ELITE-1's difficulty. The relative execution error per dribble touch becomes:

## Part D — The technical vocabulary

### 13. The technical action vocabulary
Nine mechanically distinct actions. Each solves a football problem; none is chosen for being fancy (`tc_actions.js`).

| action | sophistication | range | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| carry touch | 0 | 0–30° | inside / laces / outside along the run: close, one-footed or a stride carry |
| outside touch / cut | 0.10 | 12–70° | the turn-side foot redirects with its outside, without breaking stride |
| inside cut | 0.15 | 30–115° | plant; the other foot cuts the ball across the body |
| sole stop | 0.15 | — | the sole stops the ball as the body brakes |
| sole drag-back | 0.45 | 115–180° | the sole pulls the ball back as the body stops and turns; the other foot plays it out |
| Cruyff-like turn | 0.55 | 105–180° | the foot away from the turn plays the ball behind the standing leg; the body pivots |
| drag across | 0.50 | 35–95° | the sole rolls the ball across to the other foot (the ball on the wrong side of the stance) |
| double touch | 0.55 | 25–90° | inside → inside / outside: a quick lateral shift in a tight left-right sequence |
| feint + outside exit | 0.65 | 25–100° | a body feint (the centre of mass dips the other way), then the outside takes the ball away — **only when the space calls for disguise** |

### 14. Action-selection logic
For an intent change beyond 28° (or a stop), the controller builds a **small feasible set**: family × the next ≤ 5 swings, with the foot fixed by the family's rule (turn-side / away / any). It drops what the body cannot brake for in time. Each candidate is scored:

`fit + time to the contact + speed lost + sophistication · (3 · (1 − soph(DRI)) + 2 · simplicity prior) − 1.6 · style.technical · soph(DRI) · family.soph + preferred-foot cost + surface preference + functional bonuses + contest term`

The functional bonuses are:
- the drag across when the ball is on the wrong side at < 4.5 m/s;
- the double touch in a left-right sequence;
- the natural bonus for the plain answers (the inside cut at 40–105°, the outside touch at ≤ 50°).

The lowest cost wins: a fixed order and a strict comparison, with **no randomness**. The chosen body program comes from the family and Agility (TURN-1's entry speed × entryK).

### 15. Automatic skill usage
There is no button. Sophisticated actions (sophistication ≥ 0.5: Cruyff, drag across, double touch, feint) appear only where they are the answer:
- reversals for the Cruyff;
- the contested change of direction for the feint;
- the left-right sequence for the drag across and the double touch.

Straight running is always plain carry touches.

**Over a spread of situations** — 35–180° × 3 / 4.5 / 5.5 / 7 m/s × 3 seeds, 132 actions per preset (`evidence/tc_vocab_spread.json`):

| preset | 99 | 90 | 80 | 70 | 60 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sophisticated share | 30 % | 14 % | 14 % | 11 % | 2 % | 2 % |
| Cruyff turns | 39 | 19 | 18 | 15 | 3 | 3 |

Their place is taken by drag-backs (9 → 29 → 30 → 33 → 45 → 45); inside cuts and outside touches stay at ≈ 84 throughout.

**On the course itself**, with only six reversal situations per preset, the uniform presets read 34 % (99) → 26 % (90) → 0 % (80 and below). The reversal there is a near-tie: at 80/80/80, Cruyff 3.35 vs drag-back 3.27. Agility 80 cannot brake in time for the Cruyff at the first swing, so it costs one swing more. Dribbling alone gives 35 % (D99) → 35 % (D90) → 12 % (D80) → 10 % (D60) → 0 % (D50).

### 16. Why skills are selected — worked examples (seed 1, 99/99/99, candidate costs from the episode's log)
- **90° turn:** inside cut L (1.19) · inside cut L one swing later (1.25) · drag across R (1.28) · double touch L (1.87). The plain inside cut wins: natural, at the next swing.
- **180° reversal:** Cruyff R (1.70) · Cruyff two swings later (2.52) · drag-back L (2.59). At 99 the pivot is affordable and is the fastest way round.
- **Contested 45°:** feint + exit R (0.59) · outside touch (1.24) · inside cut (1.40). The feint wins only because the space ahead is closed (contest 1). With the space open it is not offered at all.
- **Close-control technician, left-right:** inside cut R (0.29) first; then drag across (the ball on the wrong side, sole preference 1.7).

## Part E — Styles

### 18. The dribbling-style architecture
**Ability ≠ style.** The attributes say how well the player can execute. The style (`tc_attrs.js STYLES`) says how he tends to execute. It has six dimensions, each 0–1:
- **foot** — preferred-foot bias;
- **surface** — inside / outside / sole preference;
- **rhythm** — stride touches → touching at almost every step;
- **technical** — readiness for drag / Cruyff / double when functional;
- **feint** — readiness for disguise when the space calls for it;
- **direct** — straightest progression, larger touches into space.

Style weights the choices only:
- the action cost (§14);
- the carry rhythm;
- the surface among those that fit;
- the foot.

It never changes an attribute, a limit or an execution draw. The keyed BC draws are identical across styles: 2093/2093 contact pairs (§31).

Turns are asked at three phases of the rhythm to keep this fair.

## Part G — Animation, determinism, performance, regressions

### 27. Animation architecture
**Simulation → contract → presentation, one way.**
- **The simulation** (TechSim / TechEpisode, 240 Hz) owns the ball (BallWorld kernel, unchanged), the body path (MotionPlan), the gait (swings) and the contacts (time, foot, surface, ideal and executed launch).
- **The presentation** (TechBody) is rebuilt when the episode re-plans. It is handed the previous body for C¹ continuity, and is posed per frame:
  - feet: keyframed swings; planted with heel rise / toe-off pivot;
  - pelvis: bob, twist, lean and roll, weight shift onto loaded feet;
  - per-family choreography; arms; head;
  - blends with TURN-1.2's poses at a moving receive's start and at the bridge switch;
  - pelvis reach correction for planted / touching legs; RUN-1 leg IK.
- **The ball never follows the animation.** It is rendered from the simulation record.

### 28. IK / foot locking
- Planted feet are locked: slip ≤ 5.3 mm worst over 80 runs, 1.3 mm on the receive chain.
- Contact pose (the posed ankle vs the authored contact pose at the contact tick) ≤ 3.9 mm.

- The toe-off pivots about the ball of the foot; the reach correction moves the pelvis, never the foot or the ball.

## Part I — Limitations and recommendation

### 38. Remaining limitations
1. **I cannot play video in real time.** The motion at normal speed has been judged only from stills, strips and per-frame metrics. The owner's eye at 1× and 0.5× is the acceptance test that remains.
2. **The body is kinematic.** It uses keyframed swings from RUN-1 plus IK, not physics. A few isolated frames still reach 1.1–1.4k m/s² ankle acceleration (in the stop and at action re-plans), although p95 is below RUN-1's own.
3. **Legs near full extension.** About 7 % of frames are at late swing / toe-off; the IK p99 is 4 cm (worst 7 cm, at sprint).
4. **Ball Control's effect on separation is modest by design.** At BC 50 the median is 0.86 → 0.89 m; low BC shows mainly as corrections and recovery touches. If the owner wants a visibly looser low-BC ball, the dribble-touch difficulty (`TC.ddiff`) is the single knob; the protected BC curve stays.
5. **Sophisticated is not always faster.** 90/90/90 orients slightly slower than 80/80/80 on average (1.11 vs 1.05 s): at 90 it picks drag-backs / drag across for some reversals where 80 plays plain cuts.
6. **The contest is a proxy.** It is a space-closed scalar in the course, with no defenders. Feints therefore appear only in the contested section.
7. **Two-footed with a one-footed rhythm keeps the starting foot.** A two-footed player using a dominant-foot rhythm still touches with the right foot 80 % of the time: there is no bias, but a one-footed stride carry keeps the foot it started with.
8. **After a stop** the player keeps giving a nearly stationary ball small touches. There is no "standing on the ball" state yet.
9. **Weak-foot quality** (precision on the weaker foot) is not modelled. Only the preference is.
10. **The bridge is optional.** With it, DRIBBLE-V1's frozen sprint carry takes over (2.1 m median; up to 4.3 m at 60/50, its knock-on).
11. **Course timing.** The intent change was 1.2 s for every seed in the first runs; it is now 1.2 / 1.4 / 1.6 s per seed. Sweep numbers before that change are not comparable.
12. **Sandbox only.** Not integrated into the match engine; not frozen.
13. **A low-BC measurement artefact.** At BC 1 the turn metrics *improve* (orientation 0.78 s vs 1.02 s at 99; exit 5.09 vs 4.65 m/s). Imprecise touches trigger chases and re-plans, which start the turn earlier than the course's touch rhythm would. It is not a body capability; the body limits are Agility's alone.

### 39. Recommendation for the eventual RECEIVE / DRIBBLE foundation
Adopt the **TECHNICAL-CONTROL-1 architecture** as the candidate foundation after the owner's visual review. Its parts:
- **one controller from the first touch through the dribble:** RECEIVE-ELITE-1's approved receive, then the technical carry and actions;
- **authoritative contacts on the gait**, through procedural stride adjustment;
- **the compact automatic vocabulary**, with Dribbling's smooth sophistication weight;
- **style as a choice layer**;
- **the contract-reading body** (TechBody).

Keep DRIBBLE-V1 frozen. Use it behind the optional sprint bridge, or retire it to a reference; it should not stay the default carrier, because its takeover contract is what forced the exit touch.

Suggested order:
1. Owner review at 99 / 80 / 50 and of the styles. Agree the visual bar, then lock the presets.
2. Decide the bridge default (off recommended) and the low-BC looseness (`TC.ddiff`).
3. Replace the contest proxy with the engine's real space / defender geometry.
4. Add weak-foot precision and a standing-with-the-ball state.
5. Only then freeze, as RECEIVE-V1 + DRIBBLE-V2 (or one TECHNICAL-CONTROL-V1), after approval.
