v4 · MOMENTUM-CONTINUOUS DIVE — one airborne arc through CONTACT, landing where the arc comes down, deceleration only on the ground

What was wrong (measured before any change, §v4·1 left column): the presentation root's lateral velocity was 3.1 m/s in the push-off and 2.1 m/s at toe-off, then decayed to −0.1 m/s by the contact tick — the flight pelvis was a fixed solved offset in the frame of the simulation root, and the simulation eases its own root to a stop at execEnd (its dive is a 45 % root move with a smoothstep), so the body inherited that stop. At execEnd a separate landing plan then restarted the lateral velocity at a constant 0.71 m/s (a velocity step, unrelated to the flight) and killed it 0.12 s after touchdown with no slide. Code: gkGraphRedirect (pelvis blend to axis.pelvisOff), gkLandingPlan (vLat = travel/execTime·sinθ + 0.25), gkLandingState (constant decel to zero).

What v4 does (presentation only): at the PLANT the graph builds one launch plan in world space: a constant-acceleration push (plant → toe-off) that arrives at the toe-off with the launch velocity, then a ballistic arc — constant horizontal velocity, gravity only — that passes through the solved full-extension pelvis at execEnd (the simulation's root end point is closed-form, so the arc is known at the plant). Ball contact lies on that arc and does not change it; the same arc continues past execEnd until it reaches the touch height. Touchdown is where the arc comes down (tick 91 on this ball, 0.36 m further along the dive than before), horizontal velocity there is the flight's (1.57 m/s), and deceleration is progressive on the ground: 1.5 m/s² while the feet skid (IMPACT), 3.0 m/s² as the body hits and folds (ABSORB), then the slide's friction takes the remaining 0.6 m/s to zero by the end of the settle. Vertical velocity is absorbed with velocity-continuous (Hermite) height segments, zero at the ground. The get-up recovers the 1.47 m stop → simulation-root offset over its support points only (hips over the tucked feet, front-foot step, crouch step, rise; re-plants longer than a shuffle lift the foot as a step; recovery stages slow with the distance) — the simulation root is never touched. Foot locks are now released by leg reach (a planted foot stays planted while the leg can reach its point; toe-off = full extension), and a leg poking through the pitch bends at the knee instead of lifting the whole body — both were displacing the arc. Presentation facing frozen as before.
