# Research note R2 — cuts, sole roll, double touch / La Croqueta, body feint, protected carry

**Provenance:** this is a research subagent's report, produced by web search and fetch on 2026-10-20. It is kept as provenance for the TECHNICAL-CONTROL-1.5 spec.

**Labels:**
- **E** = a source says it explicitly (the codes refer to the source table at the end).
- **I** = the researcher's own inference.

**The evidence base.**
- **Technique detail** comes from coaching material: the FIFA Training Centre, the FA, the Special Olympics coaching guide, René Meulensteen's academy demo, and academy session plans on SportSessionPlanner (SSP).
- **Numbers** come from biomechanics papers, mostly about changes of direction (COD) without a ball.
- **Gap:** no laboratory study gives an angle or speed for any of these six moves performed with the ball.

## Cross-cutting evidence (numbers)

**The angle–velocity trade-off, without a ball (F1)**
- (E) A faster approach compromises the intended cut.
- (E) The penultimate step brakes little for cuts of 45° or less, and plays a prominent role for 60° or more.
- (E) Approach speeds:
  - about 5.8 m/s at 45°, 4.4 m/s at 90° and 4.0 m/s at 180°;
  - in one study, averages of 4.7 / 3.8 / 3.5 / 3.4 m/s at 45 / 90 / 135 / 180°.
- (E) As approach speed rose from 2 to 5 m/s, the angle actually achieved fell from about 35° to about 17.5°.

**Deceleration needed, without a ball, 4 m approach (F2)**
- (E) Deceleration steps: 0.4 at 45°, 2.7 at 90°, 3.3 at 135° and 3.3 at 180°.
- (E) Plant contact time: 0.15 s at 45°, 0.18 s at 90°, 0.30 s at 180°.
- (E) Centre-of-mass (CoM) displacement at the plant step grows from 0.18 m at 45° to 0.33 m at 180°.

**Speed with the ball (E9, 52 professionals)**
- (E) The 5 m sprint is about 16% slower and the 20 m about 10% slower.
- (E) Maximum velocity is 7.6–8.2 m/s with the ball versus 8.3–8.7 m/s without.

**What fast dribblers do**
- (E10, E) In a youth slalom, faster dribblers touched the ball about 30% more often, moved their CoM less sideways and vertically, and took a shorter path.
- (B5, E) Fast 180° sole half-turns: short steps, minimal sideways and forward body travel, a lowered CoM.

## 1. Outside-foot cut / outside hook

"Outside cut" means either a 90° cut with the outside of the foot on the ball's far side (A2), or a stop-and-turn back, "turning back on their goal" (A1). The "outside hook" is the 180° version, finishing facing the opposite way (A3, A4).

**Incoming speed**
- (E) The winger version is done at speed, starting the cut "a few steps away" (A5).
- (I, from F1/F2) The hook needs near-stop braking.

**Direction-change range**
- (E) 90° (A2); about 180° for the hook (A1, A4).
- (I) The winger's down-the-line cut is about 30–60°.

**Ball position before the move**
- (E) The outside of the foot goes "over and behind the ball" (A3), on its "far side" (A2).

**Support foot**
- (E) The foot opposite the cutting foot. "Place plant foot past the ball" (A1).
- (E) For the hook, the standing foot goes beside the ball, a little wider than for a drag-back, rotated to about 2 o'clock (right foot) or 10 o'clock (left), knee bent (A3, A4).

**Contact foot and surface**
- (E) The foot on the side you turn toward: "outside of foot same side" (A8).
- (E) The outside, the "pinky toe" (A1).

**Ball path relative to the legs**
- (I) It stays on the cutting-foot side and never crosses the standing leg (contrast "inside… across standing leg", A8).
- (E) In the hook, the cutting foot passes "through the gap" between the standing foot and the ball, then pulls the ball back (A4).

**Body and CoM**
- (E) Low centre of gravity, knees bent, arms out; the body turns with the ball (A4).

**Shielding**
- (I, from E1) The cutting foot is the far foot from a defender on the other side, so the torso stays between defender and ball.

**Number of ball contacts**
- (E) One cutting touch plus a "big touch to exit" (A1).

**Preparation**
- (E) "Take the defender inside" first (A5). Swing the leg and delay the touch (E12).

**Exit direction and acceleration**
- Exit: toward the cutting-foot side; for the hook, back along the original line (E, A1).
- (E) A big touch past the defender, then accelerate over about 5–6 steps (A5). "Accelerate away" (A3).

**When it is inappropriate**
- (E) "Don't do your cut when you are right on top of a defender" (A5).
- (I) When the defender is on the cutting-foot side.
- (E) Cue to use it: "Defender in front to cut off dribble" (A1).

**Confidence:** medium. The coaching sources are consistent; the angles are coaches' conventions.

## 2. Inside-foot cut (compared with the Ronaldo chop and the Cruyff)

**Incoming speed**
- (E) A "controlled approach" (A6); "small touches on approach" (SSP GlGnb).

**Direction-change range**
- (E) 90° (A2). "Cut the ball back" (A6), which suggests more than 90° (I).
- (E) Used in 90–135° turns after receiving (A8).

**Ball position before the move**
- (E) The inside of the foot "on far side of ball" (A2).

**Support foot**
- (E) "Step to the side of the ball with the non kicking foot. Pivot on that foot" (A6).

**Contact foot and surface**
- (E) "Inside of the opposite foot": the foot away from the new direction (A6, A10).
- The inside.

**Ball path relative to the legs**
- (E) "Across standing leg": across the front of the body (A8).

**Body and CoM**
- (E) "Lower your body to give a better platform to accelerate" (A7); bend the knees (A6).

**Shielding**
- (I) The ball crosses toward the cut side during the cut. That is safe only if the defender there has overrun or committed the other way; the body then rotates between defender and ball.

**Number of ball contacts**
- (E) One cut plus an exit touch, using the "nearest foot and surface" afterwards (A6).

**Preparation**
- (E) "Use your body to sell the inside-cut as a shot or pass" (A7).

**Exit direction and acceleration**
- (I) Exit across the body, toward the standing-foot side.
- (E) "Change of speed after turn" (A6); "Accelerate after the turn" (A7).

**When it is inappropriate**
- (I) When an uncommitted defender is on the side you cut to.
- (E) At full sprint, the chop is the variant used instead (A12).

**How the three moves differ**
- **Inside cut:** the ball passes in front of the standing leg; about 90° (A2, A8).
- **Ronaldo chop** (A9–A12):
  - done at speed with a hop ("lift both feet"), using the inside of the foot;
  - the ball goes behind the other foot, "diagonally forward" (A9), about 60° (A10);
  - MOJO describes it as "slicing… slightly backward and to the side" (A11);
  - one source (A12) disagrees: it puts the plant foot in front of the ball as a shield and pushes forward.
- **Cruyff:** fake a shot, widen the standing foot, and sweep the ball behind the standing leg, "more backwards", about 180° (A2, A8, A10).

**Confidence:** medium-high on the mechanics, low on the angles.

## 3. Sole roll / sole drag across

**Incoming speed**
- (E) Low: "Small touches when close" (B2); "Messi touches to approach defender" (B1).

**Direction-change range**
- (E) The roll goes laterally, across the body (B4).
- (I) The roll itself is about 90° to the direction of travel, with a forward or diagonal exit.
- (E) The sole half-turn is 180° (B5). No source gives degrees for the roll.

**Ball position before the move**
- (E) Slightly in front of the rolling foot (B4). The sole starts "on the outside of the ball" (B1).

**Support foot**
- (E) "Move standing foot at the same time": it shuffles or hops sideways with the ball (B1).

**Contact foot and surface**
- (E) The foot already carrying the ball: "Carry the ball on the foot you're performing the roll with" (B1).
- The sole.

**Ball path relative to the legs**
- (E) Across the front of the body toward the other foot (B4).
- (I) In front of the standing leg.

**Body and CoM**
- (E) "Opening up hips to fake the pass" (B1).
- (E, half-turn) Short steps and a lowered CoM (B5).

**Shielding**
- (E) "Using body to protect ball" (B2). The exit touch with the opposite foot "keep[s] the ball further away from the defender" (B1).

**Number of ball contacts**
- (E) The roll (a continuous contact) plus one exit touch (B1).

**Preparation**
- (E) Fake a pass; judge the distance with eyes up (B1).

**Exit direction and acceleration**
- (E) "Finish the move on the same side as the ball is traveling" (B3); "close the door by running behind the defender" (B1).
- (E) "Accelerate away, Ronaldo touch"; "bigger touch to beat the defender" (B1).

**When to use it**
- (E) Cue: "Defender in front with opposite momentum" (B2).
- (I) Not suited to a high entry speed.

**Confidence:** medium-low.

## 4. Double touch / La Croqueta

**Incoming speed**
- (E) "Medium to slow running pace", then accelerate (C2). "Slow down to draw the defender in" (C3). Used when driving at a player (C4).

**Direction-change range**
- (E) Touch 1 is lateral and touch 2 forward: an "L-shaped" path (C3).
- (I) The net change of travel is about 20–45°.

**Ball position before the move**
- (E) At the dominant foot (C2); feet shoulder-width apart, the ball toward the dominant side (C3).

**Support foot**
- (E) The weight shifts sideways onto the receiving foot's side (C1).
- (I) The foot-placement detail is inferred.

**Contact feet and surface**
- (E) Both feet: the inside of one, then the inside of the other (C1, C6). "Side-foot to side-foot" (C1); toe up (C2).

**Ball path relative to the legs**
- (E) Across the front of the body, between the feet, "not toward the defender" (C2).
- (I) It never goes behind a leg.

**Body and CoM**
- (E) "Body weight shifting accordingly to slalom past" (C1).

**Shielding**
- (E) Little true shielding. It takes the ball out of reach of a defender's committed leg and can split a double-team, "bisecting them" (C1).

**Number of ball contacts**
- (E) Two (C4, C2).

**Preparation**
- (E) Draw the defender "so they dive in" (C6), with the defender about a step away (C2); watch his feet (C3).

**Exit direction and acceleration**
- (E) Forward past the defender, the ball 2–3 steps ahead (1–2 if defenders are close, 3–5 in space) (C2).
- (E) A change of pace (C2, C6).

**When it is inappropriate**
- (E) Right on top of the defender, or with a slow push (C2).
- (I) Against a defender who isn't committing.
- (E) Laudrup's view: it exists "for the next pass" (C1).

**Confidence:** medium.

## 5. Body feint + exit (Matthews, shoulder drop, step-over)

**Incoming speed — the sources disagree**
- (E) Youth coaching says slow down: "little touches when close to slow down" (D2). The step-over "usually isn't done at full speed" (D6).
- (E) A study of skilled dribblers' scissors feints found the opposite (D8): they "maintain[ed] a high body speed" while closing distance, kept the speed difference to the defender small, then accelerated sharply.

**Direction-change range**
- (E) Opposite to the fake (D1, D2). Matthews: "dummy inside, scoot past on the outside" (D5).
- (I) About 30–60°.

**Ball position before the move**
- (E) Not moved during a pure feint (D1).
- (E) Matthews: a small inside touch, then the outside of the same foot (D4).

**Support foot**
- (E) Feint: the faking foot steps about 30 cm beside the ball and takes the weight; the player pushes off it (D1, D3).
- (E) Matthews: plant the opposite foot and hop on it (D4).

**Contact foot and surface**
- (E) Feint: the outside of the other foot (D1, D2). Matthews: the same foot, inside then outside (D4).
- (E) The outside for the exit (D1, D2, D4, D6).

**Ball path relative to the legs**
- (I) It stays in front of the body and does not cross the standing leg.

**Body and CoM**
- (E) Drop the shoulder, exaggerate the upper body, eyes toward the fake (D1–D3).
- (E) Rugby data: deceivers exaggerate foot displacement and head and upper-trunk yaw, and minimise CoM displacement and lower-trunk yaw (D7). This partly conflicts with "genuinely load your weight" (D3).

**Shielding**
- (E) "Put body between defender and ball" (D4).

**Number of ball contacts**
- (E) Feint: 1 (the exit only). Matthews: 2. Step-over: 1 (D1, D4, D6).

**Preparation — the sources disagree on distance**
- (E) About 1 m from the defender (D1); the defender should be "about to make a move" (D3).
- (E) For the step-over: "do the move early", and don't get "too close" (D6).

**Exit direction and acceleration**
- Away from the fake, past the defender.
- (E) Push off the ball of the foot (D1); "accelerate immediately" (D3); knee flexion then powerful extension (D8).

**When it is inappropriate**
- (E) Too far from the defender (D3); too close for a step-over (D6).

**Confidence:** medium.

## 6. Protected forward carry under pressure

**Incoming speed**
- (E) Up to near sprint: the elite maximum with the ball is about 7.6–8.2 m/s (E9).

**Direction-change range**
- (E) Close to straight: a "straight line" (E6).
- (E11, I) Cutting across a chasing defender's path is about 10–30°.

**Ball position**
- (E) "Out of feet into space" when there is room (E7). When pressed: "touch on every step" (E5, E6).

**Support and contact foot**
- (E) Carry with "the foot furthest away from your opponent" (E1); the "ball… on outside foot" (E4).

**Contact surface**
- (E) The laces (E7); the "pinkie toe" (E6); "pigeon-toed" (E5); the sole to move the ball away when shielding (E3).

**Ball path relative to the legs**
- (I) Outside the far foot's line, never across toward the defender.

**Body and CoM**
- (E) Partly sideways, a wide stance, knees bent (E2); "low centre of gravity and a strong core" (E1).

**Shielding**
- (E) "Whole body is between your opponent and the ball" (E1).
- (E) The arm nearest the defender is up (E2, E3).
- (E) Cutting across the defender's path means he can only tackle by fouling (E11).
- (E) Common errors: facing the defender, or having your back to him (E2).

**Number of ball contacts**
- (E) Every step when tight (E5). Big touches only in space; too big loses control (E8).

**Exit**
- (E) "Drive the ball into the space as soon as it appears" (E12); change of pace (E7).

**When it is inappropriate**
- (E) Long touches with a defender close (E8, E1).
- (I) A defender square in front calls for a move instead.

**Confidence:** medium-high on the principles, low on the numbers.

## Which technique for which angle or situation

1. **The far-foot rule.**
   - (E) "When performing a turn, use the foot furthest away from your opponent, so you have your whole body between them and the ball" (E1).
   - (E) "Turn away from defender to protect the ball" (E5).
2. **The surface sets the direction** (A8, E):
   - the outside of the foot → the same side;
   - the inside → across the standing leg;
   - the inside, Cruyff-style → behind the standing leg, more backwards.
   - (I) Combined with rule 1: the far foot's outside turns away from pressure; its inside turns across the body, which needs a committed or overrun defender.
3. **Situation cues (E):**
   - the outside cut when a defender in front cuts off the dribble (A1);
   - the sole roll when the defender in front has opposite momentum (B2);
   - the hook turn when boxed in on the wing or with a defender alongside (F3);
   - the croqueta in tight areas or against a double-team (C1, C5);
   - the chop at speed on the wing (A11, A12).
4. **Angle bands (I, from F1/F2 and the techniques above):**

   | Change of direction | Likely technique | Speed |
   |---|---|---|
   | ≤ 30° | Carry with the outside or laces of the far foot | Speed kept |
   | ~45–60° | Chop, or outside cut away | Little braking |
   | ~90° | Inside or outside cut | Plant step plus 2–3 deceleration steps |
   | 135–180° | Hook, Cruyff, drag / sole half-turn | Near stop, CoM lowered, short steps |
   | Lateral shift against a lunging defender | Croqueta or sole roll | Low to medium speed |

5. **After every move, the sources consistently say:** a bigger exit touch, then acceleration from a lowered body (A1, A5, A6, A7, B1, C2, D3).
