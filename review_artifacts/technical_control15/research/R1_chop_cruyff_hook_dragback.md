# Research note R1 — Ronaldo chop, Cruyff turn, hook turns, drag-back

**Provenance:** this is a research subagent's report, kept as provenance for the TECHNICAL-CONTROL-1.5 spec. It was produced by web search and fetch on 2026-10-20. It is not the owner's text.

**Legend:**
- [E] = a source states it (the source ID is in brackets).
- [I] = the researcher's own inference.

The examples are for a right-footed player; mirror them for the left. "Forward" means the original direction of travel.

**Source quality.**
- **Official bodies.** No FA, UEFA, FIFA or US Soccer page describes these moves mechanically. England Football only names them (S43), and UEFA links a video (S44).
- **Where the detail comes from.** Most explicit detail is from:
  - club session plans on sportsessionplanner.com (SSP), which are amateur-level;
  - Mojo, Goal and 90min articles;
  - one coach's handout (S17).
- **Research literature.** It covers only the sole half-turn (S39), cuts without a ball (S40) and dribbling speed (S41).
- **Slow-motion breakdowns.** No credible frame-by-frame breakdown of the chop was found. Goal reports a video of Ronaldo teaching his son the chop: he corrected "timing, body positioning, and reading the opponent", but there is no transcript (S45).

## 1. Ronaldo chop

**Confidence:** medium on the mechanics, low on the exit angle.

**Incoming speed**
- [E] It "works best when the player with the ball is at top speed"; "players don't have to slow down" (S1).
- [E] Conflicting: S7 says "Slowing down on approach to draw defender in" and also asks "Is the momentum high enough that you can Ronaldo Chop?". So momentum is a prerequisite, and the approach speed can be adjusted to bait the defender.
- No measured speed exists for the chop itself.
- [I] For reference, ball-less cutting approaches are about 4.7 m/s at 45° and about 3.8 m/s at 90° (S40). Straight-line dribbling is about 5.2 m/s, and about 2.1 m/s on tight curves (S41). A full-speed chop fits the 45–60° exits that coaches report; a true 90° chop implies losing some speed.

**Direction-change range**
- [E] The sources give:
  - "almost 60 degrees" (S3);
  - "Angle of movement 12 o'clock to 10 o'clock (or 2 o'clock)", which is 60° (S5);
  - "cut the ball … diagonally forward" (S4) and "chop the ball diagonally" (S9);
  - "slice the ball slightly backward and to the side", which means 90° or more (S1, S2);
  - "sharp 90-degree", from a PES video-game guide (S14);
  - "Angle of hips effects angle of exit touch" (S7).
- **Spread:** about 45° to about 100°. Coaching sheets cluster at 45–60°; the popular "90°" figure comes mostly from game guides.

**Ball position before the move**
- [E] "Push the ball out in front" (S9); "Slightly bigger last touch to sell move to defender" (S5); "Final touch before move towards 11 o'clock (right foot chop)" (S8).
- [I] The 11-o'clock touch brings the ball onto the support foot's line, so the striking foot, crossing behind the support leg, can reach it.

**Support (standing) foot**
- [E] It is on the side of the new direction. To cut left, the player "takes a little hop onto the plant foot — in this case, the left foot" (S1).
- [E] It lands in front of (past) the ball: "Leap over the ball" (S6); "slice the ball once your other leg is in front of it" (S3); "jump and put your right foot in front of the ball" (S9's left-foot version).
- [E] The entry is airborne: "lift both feet off the ground" (S4); "Legs moving outwards and airborn before execution" (S7); "both feet off ground, swivel hips, snap ball out of feet" (S10).
- **Timing conflict:** S1 puts the contact "right before landing"; S6 says "Land on both feet" and then hit the ball.
- Expert Beacon (a search snippet only; the page returned 403, so unverified) says to plant about one foot-length from the defender, angled slightly outward, knee bent.

**Contact foot**
- [E] The dribbling or dominant foot: the foot on the side you cut away from (S1, S3, S6).

**Contact surface**
- [E] The inside of the foot (S1, S4, S5). S7: "Contact with the inside of the foot. Ankle locked and toe up."
- One ESL site and EA FC call it a "heel chop". Coaching sources do not support that.

**Ball path relative to the legs**
- [E] Behind the support leg, in five sources:
  - "inside of one foot behind the other foot" (S4);
  - "chop ball back in other direction behind the opposite foot" (S5);
  - "behind your weaker foot" (S6);
  - "from behind you" (S9);
  - "poking the ball around the back of himself" (S13).
- S1 says only "across … slightly backward". No source puts the ball in front of the support leg.
- [I] The striking leg crosses behind the support leg; the contact is near or behind the support heel.

**Body and centre of mass**
- [E] "Body position low", "swivel hips" (S10); "Use of body to fake" (S8).
- [E] "As the player comes down on the left foot, he or she pushes off to follow the ball" (S1). The player doesn't "change their body position" (S1).
- [I] There is a short flight phase. The pelvis turns toward the exit by roughly the exit angle, while the trunk stays largely facing forward. The support-foot landing is both the brake and the push-off.

**Shielding**
- [E] "Position body between ball and defender" (S5). "Time the chop to just before the defender would reach the ball and use body to shield defender away" (S4).
- [E] Defender cues:
  - "Alongside you (space behind you) = Move (Ronaldo Chop)" (S8);
  - "If defender gets ahead of player, look to perform Ronaldo Chop", "in behind the defender" (S11; S5 has the same idea);
  - "When defender is about to cut you off" (S12);
  - "guarding them head-on" (S1);
  - wingers cutting inside past a full-back (S3).
- [I] The defender is level or slightly ahead and committed forward. The ball goes behind his line, and his own momentum shuts him out (S7: "increase defender's momentum").

**Number of ball contacts**
- [E] One redirecting touch: "snap ball out of feet" (S10); "Dribble, dribble, hop, CHOP — and go" (S1).
- [E] It sits between a bigger set-up touch (S5) and a bigger exit touch (S10).

**Preparation**
- [E] A set-up touch (S5, S8). "Recognition of the defenders momentum and angle of recovery" (S7).

**Exit direction**
- Diagonally forward, through sideways, to slightly back (see the direction-change range above).

**Exit acceleration**
- [E] "add a burst of speed" (S1); "Acceleration out of Ronaldo chop" (S8); "Bigger touch out of move, head up, change speed" (S10); "Distance to this movement effects distance out of the move" (S7).

**When it is inappropriate**
- [E] It is "not the best move to dodge out of a crowd, especially in a player's own zone" (S1).
- [E] If the defender is behind you, accelerate instead (S8: "Behind you = accelerate").
- [E] "First option is beat defender with speed, 2nd change of direction" (S12). Insufficient momentum also rules it out (S7).
- [I] It is the wrong tool for reversals beyond about 100°, or when the space on the cut side is occupied.

**Chop vs Cruyff**
- [E] 90min calls it the Cruyff turn's "younger brother", done "with more aggression and speed" (S13). The player has no need to slow down or reorient the body (S1).
- [I] The chop: a hop, the support foot landing past the ball, a 45–90° exit at speed, the trunk facing forward. The Cruyff: a decelerated fake from a grounded plant, then a turn of about 180°.

**Chop vs inside cut**
- [E] In the inside cut you "cut the ball with the instep or inside of right foot, Must cut ball back toward left plant leg and then move left plant leg before the ball hits" (S17). The ball crosses in front of the standing leg, which has to step out of the way; the turn is "up to 90-degree" (S42).
- [I] In the chop the ball passes behind the support leg, so that leg can stay planted as the push-off leg.

## 2. Cruyff turn

**Confidence:** medium-high.

**Incoming speed**
- [E] "Slowing down on approach" (S19). S17: "slow yourself down as you approach … and really sell the turn".
- [I] Low to moderate. Ball-less 180° turns approach at about 3.4 m/s, with ground contacts of 0.42–0.52 s (S40).

**Direction-change range**
- [E] "turning 180 degrees" (S15); "turn your behind from 6 o'clock to 12 o'clock" (S20); "Turn to face the opposite direction" (S9).
- [E] But also: "You can tap sideways or back" (S2, S23).
- **Spread:** 90–180°, most often 180°.

**Ball position before the move**
- [E] "Bring the ball out of your feet so it is in front of you, within striking distance" (S16). "Push ball forward and prepare to shoot" (S9).

**Support foot (the non-kicking foot)**
- The sources disagree on where it goes:
  - [E] next to or alongside the ball (S23, S3);
  - [E] "wide of the ball", on its left side for a right-foot turn, leaving "space between the plant foot and the ball so the ball does not hit the plant foot" (S20);
  - [E] "slightly ahead of ball" (S21); "in front of the ball" (S18, S19); "a step past the ball" (S17).
- S25, a student analysis of low credibility, says the planted foot is turned 90° to the direction of travel, so it doesn't need re-planting.

**Contact foot**
- [E] The faking (kicking) foot.

**Contact surface**
- [E] The inside of the foot (S16, S17, S18, S19).
- [E] "Bring the big toe the kicking foot behind the ball … little flick with the big toe" (S20). "wrap the foot around the ball" (S24).

**Ball path relative to the legs**
- [E] Behind the standing leg (S15, S16, S18). "cut the ball back underneath the body and behind the plant leg" (S9). "push ball with kicking foot through legs" (S21).
- [E] The OED definition: the player feints a pass, then drags the ball "behind and across" the standing leg (S26).

**Body and centre of mass**
- [E] A fake shot or pass, with "Exaggeration of movements for pass/shot" (S22). "Turn your shoulders at the same time as the drag" (S16).
- [E] "Bending knees on turn"; "Push off front foot" (S19). "Bend the knees to create a platform to accelerate"; "hips must work hard" (S21).
- [E] S25 (low credibility) gives the order: upper body, then hips, then leg, and warns not to start too early.
- [I] The player pivots on the forefoot of the plant foot, turns toward the plant-foot side, and lowers the centre of mass.

**Shielding**
- [E] "Using body to protect ball"; "Use of arms to feel for defender" (S18).
- [E] Cues: "When defender is on the side about to cut you off"; "Defender in front to cut off dribble" (S18). The original 1974 move was made under close marking (S15).
- [I] At the drag, the legs sit between the ball and a defender who is blocking the faked shot. After the turn, the attacker's hip and back face him.

**Number of ball contacts**
- [E] One drag touch, then an exit touch.
- **Conflict over the exit foot:** "Take the ball away with the non-kicking [foot]" (S19, S21) vs "push away with the same foot" (S24).
- [E] "Big touch to get out of turn quickly" (S18); "Bigger touch on execution of turn" (S22). The drag itself can be the travelling touch.

**Preparation**
- [E] A shooting or passing shape, plus "check over shoulder to assess space and pressure" (S20).

**Exit direction**
- Roughly reversed (about 180°) or sideways.

**Exit acceleration**
- [E] "Quick acceleration after turn is made" (S21). "Change of speed after turn to get onto the ball quickly" (S19).

**When it is inappropriate**
- [I] No source states this. It doesn't suit full speed, because it needs deceleration and a believable shooting shape. It also doesn't suit a situation where a defender or a crowd is behind, since the turn goes back toward them.

## 3. Hook turns

**Confidence:** medium. The terminology is inconsistent: S30 calls the Cruyff the "Outside Hook Turn", and S35 merges the outside hook with the "step over touch".

### Inside hook

**Incoming speed**
- [E] "Decelerate, slow down on approach", and shorten the stride (S29). "slow down before turning" (S33). Turns other than the stop-turn suit play "when not going very fast" (S31).
- [E] Conflicting: "much easier to do at speed", on the toes (S28).

**Direction-change range**
- [E] "pivoting 180 degrees" (S30); finish "facing the opposite direction" (S28).
- [I] About 135–180°.

**Ball position before the move**
- [E] "Stop the ball and place the inside of the foot over and behind the ball" (S27): the foot goes to the ball's far side.

**Support foot**
- [E] "directly behind the ball, leaning forward and staying low" (S28). "Weight transfer to standing foot is crucial" (S32).

**Contact foot and surface**
- [E] The other foot "comes round" and hooks the ball with the inside (S27, S28).

**Ball path relative to the legs**
- [E] Pulled "back behind the standing foot while turning" (S32); "Drag the ball … in the other direction" (S27).

**Body and centre of mass**
- [E] "rotating and lowering the body" (S27). The player pivots in the same direction as the ball (S28).

**Shielding**
- [E] "protect the ball with the turning shoulder" (S30). Keep "your body between the ball and the defender" (S29).
- [I] The player turns toward the standing-foot side, rotating the back across the defender.

**Number of ball contacts**
- [E] Two: the inside of one foot, then the outside of the other (S29).

**Exit direction and acceleration**
- [E] Reversed direction. "Move away quickly into space created after the turn" (S29).

**Use**
- [E] Full-backs or wide players facing the sideline under pressure (S30).

### Outside hook

**Support foot**
- [E] Beside the ball, with a bigger gap than for the drag-back (S28). "bending the knee of your standing leg to remain in balance" (S27).

**Contact foot and surface**
- [E] The kicking foot passes through the gap, then turns so its outside is parallel to the ball (S28). The outside goes "over and behind the ball" (S27). The toes are pulled up (S31).

**Ball path relative to the legs**
- [E] Pulled back "behind the standing foot while turning" (S32), the opposite way from where the player is facing (S28).

**Body and centre of mass**
- [E] "Knees are bent, arms are out, and the centre of gravity is low", and the body turns the same way as the ball (S28).

**Direction-change range**
- [E] A reversal, about 180° (S28).
- [I] The player turns toward the kicking-foot side; the kicking leg and hip shield the ball from a defender on the standing-foot side.

**When it is inappropriate**
- [I] For both hooks: at high speed (S31 recommends the stop-turn "when going really fast"), and when a defender is close behind.

## 4. Drag-back (pull-back, V and L variants)

**Confidence:** high on the mechanics, medium on which way the body turns.

**Incoming speed**
- [E] "begin to slow down" (S34).
- [E] The research task was a 5 m dribble followed by a half-turn with the sole (S39).
- [I] Low to moderate speed.

**Direction-change range**
- [E] About 180° (S31, S35, S42, S39).
- [E] Variants:
  - **V:** the ball is pulled back, then the inside of the same foot pushes it diagonally; "the path of the ball makes a V".
  - **L (roll-back):** the ball is pulled back, then pushed "behind your left standing leg" with the inside of the same foot (S17).
- [E] Goal: once the ball is behind the standing leg, "knock it in any direction" (S3).
- **Spread:** 90–180°.

**Ball position before the move**
- [E] "in front of the body" (S42). The standing leg goes "beside the ball (similar to shooting technique)" (S34).

**Support foot**
- [E] Next to the ball (S3, S28, S34), with "weight through standing leg" (S34).

**Contact foot and surface**
- [E] The sole or studs on top of the ball (S31, S34, S36). "Toe part of the sole" (S38). "do not step over it" (S28).
- S28's "heel" is an outlier.

**Ball path relative to the legs**
- [E] Pulled straight back "under the body" (S42), "in the direction you came from" (S31), not more than about 1 m (S34).

**Body and centre of mass**
- [E] "Quickly turn your body" (S34).
- [E] Conflicting turn side: "Turn to the side of which foot is being used" (S36) and "opening body to the same side as the foot being used" (S37), vs "Turn body opposite to foot you use" (S38).
- [E] "do not turn your back to the ball" (S37).
- [E] Zago et al. (S39; under-13 players, motion capture): faster turners used short steps, kept sideways and forward body movement small, lowered the centre of mass, kept the arms out to the side and limited forward trunk lean. Faster and slower trials also differed in hip rotation on the support side.

**Shielding**
- [E] Used with the back to goal by strikers under tight marking (S30).
- [I] The pull takes the ball away from a tackle coming from the front, and the turning body then sits between that defender and the ball.

**Number of ball contacts**
- [E] Two: the pull, then "Take the ball away using the opposite foot" (S36; S37 says the same).
- [E] Alternatively, the top of the foot pushes the ball out (S42).
- [E] The V and L use the same foot twice (S17).

**Exit direction and acceleration**
- [E] "Quick turns and burst of speed immediately following turn" (S37). Continue dribbling in the new direction (S34).

**When it is inappropriate**
- [I] At high speed (implied by S31's speed rule), and when there is no space behind the player.
