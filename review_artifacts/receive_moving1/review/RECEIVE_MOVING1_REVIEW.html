What was found first

On the integrated pipeline as it stood, a moving receive looked like universal braking. Most of that was the test: RECEIVE-CONTROL-1.1's pass solver cannot lead a fast runner (a sprinter outran the pass, so he had to brake). With passes weighted for the runner (solved through the ball kernel to arrive at his projected position at the named speed), the remaining problems were real:
  • large turns at speed found no action (the planner required the body to shed the turn's speed before the contact);
  • many moving receives were lost at the DRIBBLE-V1 takeover (the ball off his line);
  • the approach left its speed correction to the last 0.8 s.
A first redesign of the speed law (curve-then-cut) was tried and rejected: it lowered both CLEAN and the pace kept (moving CLEAN 331/504). It stays switched off behind RM.curveCut, with its evidence in evidence/rm1_tried_curvecut_*.

The moving-receive model

the run (TURN-1.2): position, velocity, facing, stride phase
→
PREPARATION: the approach follows the meeting's speed need from the strike (no ±6 % keep band); the run-in curves within the lateral and rotation limits
→
THE ACTION (RECEIVE-CONTROL-1.1's moving vocabulary): touch into stride · open-body turn (inside / outside of the foot) · through-stance turn — started at a TURN-1.2 touchdown, the footwork fitted to the stride
→
CONTACT: velocity-first meeting; a turn may be taken at up to the run speed (late redirect): ball touch ≠ body redirection
→
Ball Control execution (RECEIVE-CONTROL-2's unified model, keyed draws): touch quality only
→
EXIT: the turn's cost paid after the contact — the path from the contact velocity to the new line (≤ 9 m/s²): overrun, plant, the new direction
→
TURN-1.2 at the handoff touchdown (no speed reset)
→
TAKEOVER WINDOW: DRIBBLE-V1 takes the ball once it is on his line, its takeover distance ahead (± 0.6 m), ≤ 0.25 m off the line and not running away; meanwhile the run-out steers onto the ball's line

Regression and determinism

  • Standing receives: RECEIVE-CONTROL-2's integrated cases and standing matrix rows through RECEIVE-MOVING-1 — 130/130 bit-identical.
  • RECEIVE-CONTROL-1.1's matrix of record (714 runs): standing CLEAN 108 (RECEIVE-CONTROL-2: CLEAN 108); moving CLEAN 531 · TAKEN 48 · RECEIVED 5 · MISSED 22 (RECEIVE-CONTROL-2: CLEAN 500 · TAKEN 59 · RECEIVED 8 · MISSED 39).
  • DRIBBLE-V1 60/60 (its evidence 48/48), RECEIVE-CONTROL-1.1 11/11, RECEIVE-TURN-1 18/18, RECEIVE-KILL 30/30 — before and after 45 moving receives in the same process; D1 / RX / RXE deeply unchanged: true.
  • Animation on / off 104/104, replay 104/104; Chrome = Node 26/26; different seeds draw differently 11/11.
  • The draw key is RECEIVE-CONTROL-2's (seed | RECEIVE | receiver | R<event> | c<contact> | receive-control-v2 | dimension): the receiver's speed changes the geometry and the difficulty, never the key.

Known limitations

  • The moving body is RECEIVE-CONTROL-1.1's placeholder body on the action's stride-fitted footwork (stride-aware: plant, contact, exit stride), not RECEIVE-TURN-1.1's human body; TURN-1.2's real locomotion before the action and after the handoff.
  • A pass aimed at the runner's body, from the side, makes him check his stride: the stride touch meets the ball 0.42 m ahead of him (case B: 0.83 of the pace kept; with the pass into his stride 0.91).
  • DRIBBLE-V1's takeover still fails in 50 of 504 moving receives (27 of them after 120° – 150° turns). This is the frozen dribbler's pocket.
  • 17 moving receives of the movement matrix find no action. They are firm / hard passes with a 60° turn from behind-left, and 150° – 180° turns at a sprint.
  • No Agility (the extension point: the exit acceleration, the rotation limit and TURN-1's entry speeds are the body-redirection capability a rating would scale), no Dribbling attribute, no pressure.
  • The moving actions are RECEIVE-CONTROL-1.1's moving vocabulary (adapted), not RECEIVE-TURN-1's stationary techniques re-authored for running.
