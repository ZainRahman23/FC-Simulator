01 · Diagnosis before implementation

The RUN hypothesis was substantially correct.

The old system already forecast future root positions. The failure was how it turned those forecasts into feet: ordinary forward RUN swing shapes were rotated into changing travel frames, rebuilt with each movement plan, then repaired through endpoint blending and IK. Prediction alone did not preserve a committed support sequence.

Two distinct mechanisms: movement replans replaced swing anchors and changed the RUN phase; the existing 0.12 s blend was bypassed across support changes and when corrections exceeded 0.6 m. Separately, reach correction engaged on planted/contact legs, so touchdown could abruptly move the rendered root and pelvis.

At fast-circle +0.550 s, the root advances only 3.33 cm and the intended ankle target changes about 0.06 cm, yet the rendered toe jumps 25.2 cm. The leg’s reach shortfall changes from roughly 26 cm in swing to 0.4 cm at plant. That event is a touchdown/reach discontinuity, not a large authoritative movement step. In repeated ±30° at +0.900 s, the rewritten landing target moves about 1.10 m and the toe jumps 1.22 m.

02 · Minimal locomotion vocabulary

Three families, with a replacement-step option.

Running

STRAIGHT_RUN blends into CURVED_RUN. Ordinary cadence is retained. Curves sustain one sequence with inside/outside placement, compact recovery, body lean and landings forecast along the arc.

Redirecting support

CUT_SUPPORT uses outside placement, shorter support timing, knee/hip flexion, torso participation and an outgoing opposite step. It reacts to the active trajectory and support phase.

Braking / reversal

BRAKE_REPLACE compresses and reorganizes support through C’s existing braking. REPLACEMENT_STEP is a transfer variant when a support becomes unsuitable.

Football studies support asymmetric curved sprinting, distinct 45°/90° cutting demands, coordinated trunk–pelvis motion and braking across preceding contacts. These inform the vocabulary, not physical caps on C. See the research memo, including Filter et al. on curved sprinting, Havens & Sigward on cutting mechanics and Dos’Santos et al. on reversal braking.

Crossover was investigated. This pass uses compact lateral replacement and separate swing routes; it does not claim a complete authored crossover system. The anatomical stance width is not globally expanded to conceal collisions.

Commitment survives input changes.

Every foot keeps its world position, velocity, identity, landing time and current support phase. Early swing can adapt to the forecast. Late swing progressively preserves the endpoint; an unsuitable target can shorten into a reachable landing or retime into a replacement. Retiming preserves the height phase. The next landing uses only the current authoritative plan plus recent coherent curvature, never a later recorded input.

The recovery boot follows the pelvis through mid-swing and admits more landing rotation late. This avoids sweeping a prematurely turned toe through the opposite calf. A planted ankle stays in place while the boot can pivot; an overstretched stance releases early. Reachable swing routes are planned before IK. A continuous, bounded vertical pelvis compression handles both approaching and planted feet, so touchdown cannot switch on the old rendered-root pull.

Presentation lag: the body heading frame uses a 25 ms exponential follower, capped at 12° behind authoritative facing. The measured fast-circle right-entry peak is 7.13°—about 26 ms at 270°/s. This is orientation lag, not a delayed root path. Root translation is exact. The pelvis also has its ordinary cyclic twist of up to 6°; this is separate from the measured heading-frame lag. Torso counter-rotation is capped at 12°, curve lean at 26°, with smooth transitions.

03 · Result and diagnostic coverage

The discontinuities disappear in the tested set.

272 BALL OFF scenarios cover jog/run/sprint, both directions, sustained 0/15/30/45/60/90/120/150/180°, gradual and fast ramps, repeated ±30°, rapid ±60°, 90° sequences, both circles and release. Sprint has four entry offsets (0 / .12 / .24 / .36 s); jog/run have two (0 / .24 s). Each body has 264,192 measured frames.

Large foot jump: rendered toe displacement over 12 cm in one 1/240 s tick. Intersection flag: existing limb-capsule clearance below −2 cm, not full mesh collision. The new minimum clearance is −1.11 cm, so “zero flags” does not mean perfect separation. Maximum new toe displacement is 7.92 cm per tick.

05 · Unchanged TC diagnostic

Better legs do not yet mean valid touches.

The new support body removes the diagnosed jumps in all 24 BALL ON scenarios as well, but it does not honor authored baseline touch choreography. This is a deliberate diagnostic boundary: the same ball, contact schedule, technique selection and physics run under both bodies. The ball is never attached to the new foot.

Where contacts conflict · phase 0, right first

Of 144 successful simulation contacts in these trials, the original body is over 10 cm from the ball at 3; the new body is over 10 cm away at 51. The worst new gap is 53.6 cm in rapid steering. Mean positive boot-surface/ball gap rises from 1.05 cm to 10.40 cm. Some individual contacts improve, but the new body is not ready to combine with Technical Control.

In particular, repeated ±30° and the fast circle produce visible detached touches; reversal and named-technique contacts can occur while the new foot is in a different recovery phase. No touch, dribbling, Ball Control, B4 effort turn, B5 exit or named technique was changed to conceal this. The pending integration needs its own review.

06 · What remains provisional

Coherent feet are a step forward, not final approval.

  • Airborne turning remains. In the phase-zero right fast circle, flight falls from 66.3% to 50.2% of measured frames, but about 510° of heading change still occurs during flight over four seconds. In repeated ±30°, flight instead rises from 55.1% to 66.3%, and airborne heading change from 261° to 381°. That exact case still needs a stronger grounded explanation even though the limbs are coherent.
  • Late landing compromises are visible in the data. The final endpoint can differ from the achieved target by up to 24.6 cm across the broad audit (fast ramp, sprint, offset .24 s, right first); rendered landing error peaks at 24.8 cm. First forecasts can be metres wrong after a later input—worst 3.82 m in the left fast ramp. Those are changed/shortened landings, not precise forecasts or teleportation.
  • Foot pivot is an ankle-centred presentation cheat. In the right fast circle, total planted ankle travel is 3.7 cm across all supports, while toes travel 1.72 m as the boots rotate. This is accumulated pivot travel, not a 1.72 m single slide. It is not a fully articulated forefoot-pressure model.
  • Ordinary running stays simple, but looks more compact. The phase-zero straight sprint keeps 32 lift/land switches over four seconds in both bodies, with no added facing lag. The new pelvis is lower, its foot arcs differ and its flight share is 60.1% versus 53.5%. Judge that posture and rhythm in the straight control clip.
  • No claim of universal biomechanical validity. This is a causal support presentation, not a force-driven balance simulation. The audit does not test every arbitrary stick history, body shape, surface or collision. No complete crossover, foot-pressure or contact-integrated locomotion system has been approved.
Owner decision preserved: C remains the provisional Agility-99 ceiling. No trajectory was slowed to accommodate the body. The implementation is ready for your visual/playable review. Nothing is frozen, merged into production or calibrated below 99.
07 · Verification and review package

Same movement. Isolated presentation change.

All 564 C runs—282 request/speed/phase/direction cases with BALL OFF and ON—have exact equality of recorded tick arrays (position, velocity, heading, facing and ball state), contact records and simulation hashes against V1. No tolerance was used. Eight additional live runs cover all four profiles and both ball modes: posing leaves simulation hashes/contacts unchanged, and online versus replay joint coordinates match exactly.

The source comparison finds 1,604 V1 files unchanged, two viewer files modified and two new presentation files. Movement parameters, acceleration, sprint ceiling, the simulation adapter, original body and baseline TC modules are byte-identical to V1. The prior snapshot/provenance is retained. Production files were not written.
