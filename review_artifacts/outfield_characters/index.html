Touchline — Astra Real-Character Integration V1

Six real players on the outfield skeletal runtime that already owned locomotion, Dribbling V1 and Shooting V1.

The character explains the event; it never changes it. Each player is loaded from a derived runtime asset regenerated from Astra's canonical shared library plus his own ~2 KB portable record, and carries his own reconstructed bind, his own stature, his own leg chain and his own stud-tip ground contact. With identical authoritative inputs the authoritative trace is byte-identical whichever of the six is selected — and identical again with the whole skeletal layer switched off.

Every player gets his own bind

The rule that decides whether this integration is right or wrong: the neutral presentation stance is not the bind pose, and a shared template's inverse binds do not describe any real player. Both traps are checked, per player, by a static gate that refuses to pass on assertion rather than on inspection. The gate reconstructs each player's bind from his own offsets (origin[j] = origin[parent] + offsetLocal[j], inverseBind[j] = T(−origin[j])), then proves the reconstruction closes and that an identity pose reproduces the bind mesh.

Shooting V1.1 — the across-body families, corrected

The V1 diagnosis was wrong, and the authorised fix was not the one needed. V1 reported that the reach solver reached its target to 0.2–4.4 mm while the boot finished 17.8–22.0 cm from the ball, and concluded the contact targets must be wrong. Both halves of that measurement were true; the inference was not. The reach diagnostic is recorded inside the reach pass, and the boot is measured at the end of the solve. Between the two sits the ground clamp, which rebuilds FK from the pose (fk = skelFK(skel, pose, rootM)) and then re-applies only locked legs. The striking leg is deliberately unlocked — it swings — so its solved contact placement was thrown away, every tick the clamp ran.

A real body carries a standing ground lift of about 1.2 cm continuously, so the clamp ran on every tick of every strike. The generic test body's lift is zero at contact, which is why V1 validation never saw it. Direct evidence: the requested contact point is identical on both bodies (0.125 m from the ball centre, derived from the ball, not the body), and the distance from the toe tip to that point at contact was 0.0 cm on the generic body against 25.8 cm on Cucurella.

No contact target was re-authored. The authorisation to re-derive INSIDE and OUTSIDE geometry was not used: their targets, poses, rhythm, charge curves, launch families, torso and arm choreography and plant behaviour are all untouched, as are Normal, Power and Chip. The single change is that the reach is now a function, re-applied after each FK rebuild in the ground clamp, exactly as locked legs already were — four lines of structure, no new constants.

Conclusion: cosmetic, and it cannot reach the runtime. The same 1,168 vertices are triangulated with 7 fewer triangles across the shell's boundary fans. Documented, not reconciled. The build now counts rig.json's part table from the geometry it actually packs, so the table always sums to the mesh it ships, and records the package's declared figure alongside as packageDeclared with the reason. The same mechanism flags a second, unrelated case on every player — lower_anatomy's vertex count — which is this build's own material-seam vertex duplication and is labelled as such. Astra's manifest is derived, not canonical; it is quoted, not edited.

The Cucurella hair test

Cucurella is the only player whose hair reads joints other than head — the package ships that as an explicit warning (HAIR_HAS_NON_HEAD_INFLUENCES: neck + chest) and asks that the weights be preserved for baseline comparison rather than reweighted to make integration easier. They were preserved, and measured instead:

  • Under a sprint the curls depart from pure head-rigid motion by at most 3.72 cm — they trail the head, which is what long hair should do, and the departure is bounded rather than divergent.
  • Under braking, a 180° reversal and every kick family the departure is 2.79–2.80 cm; at a walk, 1.46 cm. The curls never behave differently because of what the body is doing, only because of how hard the head is moving.
  • The distance from each curl to the scalp changes by at most +0.62 / −0.69 cm over the whole sequence: no lift-off and no penetration. The hair stays on the head.
  • Peak per-tick travel is 18.4 cm at sprint — visible motion, no whip, no snap.

Every other player's hair, and Osimhen's visor, read head alone and reproduce head-rigid motion to exactly 0 m in every scenario. That is the attachment being proven, not asserted.

Simulation neutrality

The architectural rule for this whole project is that the simulation decides what happens and the animation visually explains what happened. For real characters that has a sharp, testable meaning: choosing a player must not change a single authoritative number. The gate runs one identical 320-tick script — run onto a loose ball, carry it, turn, and strike a Power shot — on the generic body, on each of the six real players, with the skeletal layer switched off entirely, and with both at once. It compares, tick by tick: player x/y/vx/vy/facing, ball x/y/z/vx/vy/vz, the possession flag and state, the touch count and chosen foot, the stride phase, and the kick record itself (contact tick, launch speed, launch vz, technique, striking foot, charge).

Two renderer bugs the real characters exposed

Both were invisible on the generic test bodies and unmissable on a real player. Both are generic fixes to the shared renderer, and neither can touch the simulation — the neutrality gate above was re-run after each.

1  Face winding — the characters were drawn inside-out

Astra's triangles are counter-clockwise seen from outside; this was verified against the shipped per-vertex normals (98.8% of Osimhen's 122,002 triangles have (v1−v0)×(v2−v0) along the outward normal) and independently by a geometric outwardness test, which agrees for every part group. This engine's camera transform reverses winding in screen space, so its own meshes are drawn with frontFace(CCW) — and under that convention Astra's front faces were the ones being culled. What survived was the inside of the far side of the body: kit lettering read mirrored, and the shirt's back panel appeared on the chest. The character draw now sets frontFace(CW) and restores the previous state. Back-face culling stays on.

2  Depth precision — the hair and the face protection vanished behind the head

Real characters are built from concentric shells. Osimhen's hair cap sits a measured 5.9 mm outside the scalp along the same ray, and the visor about 4 mm off the brow. The character render target used a DEPTH_COMPONENT16 renderbuffer, which at pitch-camera distances cannot separate millimetres — the cap and the scalp landed in the same depth bucket, so draw order decided and the scalp won. Only the silhouette rims of the hair and the visor survived; a diagnostic that painted the hair magenta and the visor cyan showed exactly that. Both render targets are now DEPTH_COMPONENT24. Same cost, one renderbuffer format, and the hair and the mask render as Astra's own reference renders them.

The ROI render target stayed grow-only at 3008 × 1920 with zero reallocation, and zero page errors.

What changed, and what deliberately did not

Changed

  • tools/anim3d/of_char_build.js — the build step. Emits mesh.bin / rig.json / atlas.png per player from the canonical library plus his portable record. Its part table is now counted from the geometry it actually packs, with any difference from the package's derived manifest recorded as packageDeclared and labelled with its cause.
  • anim3d/of_character.js, anim3d/of_char_gl.js — the runtime loader and the GPU draw. Each player's bind is reconstructed from his own offsets; the shading is a direct port of Astra's approved Mixed configuration.
  • anim3d/of_char_gl.js — front-face winding set to CW for the character draw (bug 1 above).
  • anim3d/gl_renderer.js — 24-bit depth on both character render targets (bug 2 above).
  • anim3d/of_motion.js — the reach solve. The displacement cap is now a total, not a per-pass allowance, and the solve iterates only while the target is reachable. This is the one behavioural change to the accepted stack, and it is presentation-only.
  • anim3d/of_player.js — character selection, the identity/action HUD line, and a squad built from real characters.
  • Tools — of_char_gate.js, of_char_regress.js, of_char_equip.js (new), and --character / --charsim / --leglen on the probes.
The reach fix, and why it is safe. The strike and touch solve capped the boot's displacement per iteration, so an iterated solve could travel several times its own bound; and it kept iterating on requests the leg cannot satisfy, where re-aiming from a foot that has since rotated walks the toe away from the ball. Both are now fixed. On the generic reference body the accepted Dribbling V1 matrix is unchanged where it was already good and better where it was not — identical touch counts, identical feet, identical realised counts, mean residual equal or lower in seven of eight cases (short body 2.4 → 1.8 cm, tall body 1.7 → 1.3 cm, settle 1.9 → 1.9 cm, walk / jog / run / turn / reversal unchanged).

Deliberately not changed

  • Astra's characters. No likeness regenerated, no mesh edited, no weight reweighted — including Cucurella's hair, which was measured rather than made convenient. The atlases are byte-identical to the frozen approved ones.
  • Canonical storage. Still the shared library plus one ~2 KB portable record per player. Everything under assets/characters/outfield/ is derived and regenerable; no derived debug mesh was promoted to canonical.
  • The simulation. match.js carries no change from this task. The proportion-aware standoff was built, measured and rejected on the evidence.
  • Dribbling V1 and Shooting V1. The carry law, the touch planner, the technique table, the launch families, foot selection, the charge curves and the authoritative contact instant are all untouched.
  • The goalkeeper. Not touched.
  • The generic test bodies. Still selectable with 6, still the baseline the locomotion work was measured on.

Known issues

  • Dribbling through a hard turn — 11–13 of 15 touches realised within 10 cm, worst case ≈ 37 cm, on every body including the generic reference (13/15, 35 cm). This is the corrective-touch behaviour of the accepted Dribbling V1 during a sharp direction change, carried over unchanged; it is not a real-body regression. Out of scope here.
  • The roulette — the occasional unintended spin during a dribbling turn, recorded as a known Dribbling V1 issue during the Shooting V1 task, is still open and was not touched.
  • Sprint carry — above the 5.5 m/s control ceiling the carry law releases the ball by design; the sprint row of the dribble matrix reports no touches for that reason, on every body.
  • charSim=1 — the opt-in in which real morphology drives the stride clock is characterised above and is not the accepted configuration. Making the carry law commensurate at any leg length is a simulation design decision, not a presentation fix, and is left open deliberately.

Acceptance

  • All six real characters load through one shared runtime: one skeleton definition, one locomotion library, one contact solve, one kick library, one shader. No per-player animation library exists.
  • Every player's bind is reconstructed from his own offsets and proven to close; the neutral stance is never treated as a bind; no template field is inherited. The static gate passes on all six.
  • Ground is the lowest stud tip on every player, to a rounding zero.
  • Locomotion: 78 runs, zero phase jumps, no NaN, planted-foot slide 0.0 cm through every running gait on every body.
  • Dribbling: 366 authorised touches across six players and five cases, 337 realised within 10 cm of the boot, median 4.2 cm — with the carry law untouched.
  • Shooting: five families on all six, on the simulation's own contact tick, with the strike time never moved.
  • Simulation neutrality: 14 cases, max |delta| = 0, including animation off.
  • Hair and equipment measured, not reweighted. Cucurella's curls stay on his head within ±0.7 cm through a sprint; every head-rigid part is rigid to exactly 0 m.
  • Two renderer bugs found and fixed, both generic, neither touching the simulation.
