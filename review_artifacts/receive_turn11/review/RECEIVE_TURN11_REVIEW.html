Architecture

RECEIVE-TURN-1 simulation
imported unchanged: technique, contact foot + surface, contact tick, ball at contact, impulse, exit line, root path, meeting, follow-up
→ read only →
authored reference REF[family](angle): lead time, plant time, swing start, support-toe angle, shoulder lead, counter-rotation, exit lead, hip pre-turn, crouch, lean, landing share, pivot range
→
footstep plan: support step (toe into the turn) · contact foot opens on its forefoot · contact swing through the CONTACT pose · lands as the first exit step · support foot pivots on its forefoot (heel up) · run steps on the path with toe-off · touches as exact via-points
→
rotation channels: gaze (scan toward the exit, then the ball) → shoulders (lead before contact, counter at contact) → hips (the root heading) → feet
→
procedural root: the simulation's path, pulled toward the loaded foot (weight ramps); pelvis moves — never a planted foot — when a leg would over-reach
→
IK + grounding: leg IK to each foot target, pivots exact about the rig's MTP joint, contact-pose ground clearance → FK → skin

Nothing flows back: the body is built from the episode after the simulation has run; the ball never follows the animation (neutrality 750/750). Ball Control never chooses the family or the technique — it changes the touch, and the body adapts where it goes (root correction, extra control, which foot rejoins).

Known limitations

  • The body is a procedural placeholder on the real rig (no authored mocap clips): the reference motion is authored as parameters per family and angle.
  • Shorts-mesh deformation where the legs cross (through-stance turn, ~+0.2 s) — a skinning artefact of the character mesh, not of the motion.
  • The run-out after the rejoin is a generic stride (quintic swing, low lift): fast foot swing (~18 m/s peak) and IK reach kinks near full extension. It is outside this milestone's focus (the turn) — RUN-1 is the real locomotion.
  • No Agility scaling, no running receives, no physical body simulation (as instructed).
  • The outside-of-foot turn is only feasible at slow passes (≤ ~6 m/s) — RECEIVE-TURN-1's simulation decides that; its rejoin pose is 14 mm off (the leg is near full reach).
  • Rotation timing does not change with Ball Control (it is the technique's); only the touch and the body's path to the ball change.
