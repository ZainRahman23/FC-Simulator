How it works

  • Authority: the simulation owns intent (direction / speed, the cut gate, pursuit); TURN-1.2 resolves locomotion, unchanged; the resolver decides when, which foot, which boot region, which contact tick and the launch; BallWorld owns the ball from that tick on. No tether: the dribble code never writes the ball's state — it sets collider frames and schedules one launch per touch through the kernel's validated schedule().
  • The touch is a bounded adjustment of the free leg's swing on top of TURN-1.2's pose: a swing time-warp (the boot meets the ball at an earlier point of its own path, at a controlled speed), a bounded offset, a boot-region rotation (yaw carried by the hip), a follow-through hold, pinned to zero at touchdown — the plant never moves (0 mm over 26,076 adjusted ticks).
  • Candidates come from real foot opportunities in TURN-1.2's own prediction (contact tick × path phase × region × contact elevation); each must pass reach, closing speed, the approach and follow-through checks, the launch solve against the next feasible window (exact BallWorld refinement), and must not send the ball into the other leg.
  • Touch distance scales with speed (aimed separation 0.7 m at JOG → 2.8 m at SPRINT, longest interval 1.4 → 2.9 s); minimum intervals stop rapid fire; one deliberate touch per swing.
  • Foot selection: both feet's opportunities compete; cost includes the preferred / weak foot (weak-foot rating), region, reach and continuity with the window the last launch aimed at; the weak foot also raises the execution error.
  • Execution error (seeded, keyed per touch): σ from Dribbling 0.5 / Ball Control 0.3 / Agility 0.2, weak foot, boot region, speed and turn (Balance); Reactions set the reaction time; Agility the reach.
  • Cuts: a requested change of more than 8° is held until a touch can take the ball into it (at most the speed's longest touch interval + 0.3 s, then the body turns without it); staged cuts and set-up touches; the carrier's pursuit bends the run toward where the free ball will be — the ball is never corrected.
  • Control state is a classification only (CONTROLLED / LOOSE / CONTESTED).

No tether — the free-ball audit

The live ball was re-simulated in a fresh BallWorld with no colliders from its own state after every launch and every body contact; between those events the two must agree. Over 128650 free-ball ticks (every scenario × 4 seeds): 0 unexplained deviating ticks (maximum |Δ| 0); 2 ticks deviate only on the tick right after a body contact (the ball still resting on that boot / leg surface, resolved by the kernel without an impulse event — physics, not a correction). The dribble code never writes the ball's state: it only sets collider frames and schedules one authoritative launch per touch through the kernel's validated schedule().

Plants unchanged — the swing adjustment never moves a stance foot

At every tick where a touch adjustment acts (26076 ticks, every scenario × 2 seeds), the stance foot and toe of the adjusted pose against TURN-1.2's plain pose of the same state: max deviation 0.0000 mm; the other (unadjusted) swing foot: 0.0000 mm.

Determinism

Node replay: 20 / 20 scenarios bit-identical (ball + COM every tick, touch ticks, feet, regions, launches). Node v22.19.0 vs Chrome/155.0.8059.80 (the lab page): 18 / 20 identical — differing: CURVE_JOG, REV180_SPRINT.
The cross-engine differences start at the last bits: REV180_SPRINT in the ball's state inside the ball kernel (~6e-15 at 3.26 s), CURVE_JOG in the player's heading ψ (4.4e-16 at 2.25 s — locomotion / intent math), while the other state is still identical; a sample of common Math functions gives identical results in both engines, so the difference is in a transcendental for specific inputs between V8 versions. Same-engine replay is exact.

Defects and next step

Three biggest V1 visual defects

  1. Touch-family monotony: almost every touch is a FRONT (toe) push — 75 of 75 at JOG, 45 of 45 at RUN; inside / outside / laces appear only in cuts. At gameplay scale the rhythm reads well, close up the touches look alike.
  2. Failed cuts end in a firm recovery push: when no touch can take the ball into the cut, the swing that meets it pushes it 9–10 m/s straight on while the body turns (k07); the cut also starts late (about 1.2 s after the request) because the player must first reach a ball he pushed 1–1.5 m ahead.
  3. SPRINT knock-ons look like chasing a loose ball: 2.5 s between touches, the ball 2–3 m ahead; at the 1× camera player and ball are small, so contacts read only through the ball's change of speed.

Three biggest V1 system defects

  1. Cut reliability (the stop-condition gap): turns taken with the ball, 8 seeds — 45° 5/8, 60° 5/8, 60° left 3/8, 90° 1/8, 90° left 2/8, 90° jog 2/8, 135° 0/8, 180° 0/8. Cause: a contact early in the swing has a fast follow-through (the time-warp must catch up to TURN-1.2's unchanged plant), so only a firm launch clears it without a second contact — the soft redirecting touch a cut needs is often infeasible. Tried tonight, measured, kept or rejected: interval consistency, gate on user changes only, speed-dependent hold, blockers, partial / staged cuts, set-up touches, pursuit on/off.
  2. CPU: 496.6 ms per simulated second for one dribbler (Apple M4, one thread) — locomotion 28.1, ball 2.8, resolver 465.7 (predictions 270.1, launch solve 117.9); 2.01× real time on average, slower than real time in the worst cases (135°: 0.53×).
  3. Foot preference is weak and the kernel can fail closed: the right-footed elite player makes only 31–59 % of RUN / FAST touches with his right foot (continuity with the aimed window outweighs preference); the ball kernel throws on a leg–ball–turf pinch (0 in the final 160 runs, seen during development — the run ends as a recorded fault); Node vs Chrome replay differs in 2 / 20 scenarios (ball kernel, 1e-17 level).

Exact next shipping step

DRIBBLE-1.1 — a soft-touch follow-through: after the contact tick, let the touching swing decelerate / lift around the ball and re-converge to TURN-1.2's unchanged plant (instead of the time-warp's catch-up), add it to the follow-through model, and re-run the cut matrix with the taken-with-the-ball metric (target 45–60° ≥ 7/8, 90° ≥ 5/8); in the same pass, share one locomotion prediction per input change between planning, clearance and the post-cut branches to cut the resolver's CPU.
