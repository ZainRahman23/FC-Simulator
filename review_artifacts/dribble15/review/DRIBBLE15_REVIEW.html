A — the 90° root cause: a second stage measured from the lagging velocity

Traced first: over the 90° right runs, DRIBBLE-1.4 made its first stage earlier than DRIBBLE-1.2 (median 0.50 vs 0.61 s after the request) but released the rest of the cut later (1.70 vs 1.33 s) and needed one more touch to get the ball onto the new line (3 vs 2). The extra touch was a second STAGE: 12 of 68 taken runs staged twice (1.2: 6 of 64). In seed 83 the release search 55 ticks after the first stage (35°) saw the cut as 75° away — the velocity was still turning — and staged to 49°; the ball reached the new line at 2.15 s. Measured from the commanded stage line (45° remaining, not 'big') the only release option is the full cut: inside of the left foot to 74° at 3.14 s, clean in 0.91 s. With the fix no 90° run stages twice.

B — recovered clean 90° cuts

90° right seed 37 (lost → clean in 0.92 s) and 90° left seed 48 (lost → clean in 1.23 s). In the zoomed view the 1.4 ball drifts out to the side after the second stage; the 1.5 ball stays in front of the player down the new line — no sliding, no emergency toe contact. Suspicious-case check: 90° left seed 11 counts as clean with a FRONT decisive touch — the frames show an inside-right set-up taking the ball across while the body turns, then an ordinary toe push along the new line (as in straight dribbling), not a rescue.

C — the known 90° right seed 16 case

The known 90° right seed 16 case keeps DRIBBLE-1.4's fix: the stage contact at tick 640 (6.87 m/s), the cut taken (not clean).

D — a D1.4 → D1.5 regression

Regressions are physical, not systematic. 90° right seed 58: 1.4 staged twice and took the cut late; 1.5 makes the full 79° cut at once, the faster ball later runs away from the player (loose at 4.33 s). 60° left seed 13: the 12 ms budget lets the search finish sooner and pick a direct outside-left cut to −30° instead of 1.4's set-up; ball and body diverge and the ball is lost. On matched seeds 60° cuts change in 53 of 192 runs: 7 better, 13 worse, 33 neutral.

E — the best recovered 60° cut (DRIBBLE-1.4) and straight RUN

60° cuts never stage (stagedMin 75°): the stage-line fix leaves them bit-identical; only the 12 ms budget changes their timing. Straight RUN is bit-identical to D1.4.

What changed

  • stageLine (plan15.stageLine). When a 90° cut is staged, the planner releases part of the turn (stageFrac of it) and holds the rest. At the next release search it must decide whether the rest is still 'big' (≥ stagedMin 75°: stage again) or not (release the full cut). DRIBBLE-1.2 – 1.4 measured it from the player's velocity heading, which lags the commanded stage heading for several hundred milliseconds while TURN-1.2 turns. It is now measured from the line of the cut so far — the stage heading — exactly as the intent layer's hold already measured it. One line of the planner; the staged direction and everything downstream are unchanged.
  • Decision-tick budget 8 → 12 ms (plan13.budgetHi). The incremental planner's architecture is unchanged — work units, the 2 ms ordinary budget, invalidation, caches. Ticks in which a decision is due (a meaningful cut held, or no plan in force) may now do 12 000 units of work instead of 8 000, so the release decision after a stage completes sooner. Average CPU unchanged (143.8 vs 141.8 ms / s); worst step 34 ms.
  • Flag fix (no behaviour change). rStop1 now alone controls whether the release search stops at its first valid plan (DRIBBLE-1.3 / 1.4 tied it to rFirst, so it could not be tested); defaults unchanged.
  • Unchanged. TURN-1.2, the every-tick release window and contact-timed release of DRIBBLE-1.4, the graded set-up validator, contact geometry and costs, the resolved touch, one authoritative impulse, the free physical ball, interruption, control loss, keyed randomness.

Limitations and next step

Three limitations to leave to attributes / defenders

  1. How sharp a 90° turn can be. Clean 90° cuts are back at DRIBBLE-1.2's level (47 vs 48 of 192), and 90° right still needs ≈ 2 s to the line in its slower half. How tight and how quick a 90° turn is now belongs to Agility, Ball Control and pressure, not to the generic search.
  2. Preferred / weak foot. The geometry picks the foot.
  3. Bad-touch severity. Faster cuts lose the ball sometimes (seeds 58, 13); Ball Control should scale the execution error through exec13, not the planner avoid quick cuts.

Recommended next step

FREEZE DRIBBLE-V1 (tag the DRIBBLE-1.5 core) and move to the attribute layer: Dribbling / Ball Control / Agility through the exec13 execution-error interface and the turn-sharpness limits, then preferred / weak foot and pressure / context.
