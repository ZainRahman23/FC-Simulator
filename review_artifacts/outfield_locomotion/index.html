Touchline — Outfield Locomotion V1

Idle, walk, jog, run, sprint on the real pitch, driven by the simulation's own player law. No ball, no dribbling.

Simulation decides what happens; animation visually explains what happened. The authoritative player law is untouched: the same ptStep acceleration, braking, lateral and facing limits the playtest always used. The locomotion reads that state and produces a pose. It never writes position, velocity, facing, ball state or timing, and it contains no randomness. Animation ON and animation OFF produce bit-identical authoritative traces (gate below).

Architecture

One continuous parametric cycle, not a clip library. Five parameter sets (idle, walk, jog, run, sprint) are anchored at speeds and blended piecewise-linearly by the authoritative speed, so there is a single pose generator and no blend tree of canned animations.

  • Stride from the body, not the clip. Step length is a multiple of the player's own leg length, so cadence v / (step x legLen) falls out of the authoritative speed. A tall player takes longer, slower strides at the same speed.
  • Phase is continuous. One phase accumulator advances by cadence and is never reset by a gait change; the right heel strikes at phase 0, the left at 0.5. Changing gait changes the blend weights, not the phase, so legs never swap or restart.
  • Pelvis height is geometric. It comes from a forward-kinematics pass on the stance leg's own sole, not from an authored bob curve; in flight it interpolates to the computed landing height. A leg entering stance takes the pelvis over gradually.
  • Contact solve. Planted feet are locked in world space by two-bone leg IK with a forward knee pole, a late-stance toe pivot, a heel-strike forefoot roll, and a bounded pelvis drop. Every metre-valued quantity scales with leg length.
  • Direction basis. The legs play along the movement direction with the trunk twisting toward the facing up to 55 degrees; beyond 110 degrees the cycle back-pedals along the facing, with hysteresis at 88 degrees and a slewed leg basis. Lean and roll come from filtered authoritative acceleration. There are not eight directional libraries.
  • The presentation root is the simulation root. Sliding is never hidden by moving the player. The red cross (authoritative) and violet ring (presentation pelvis) are drawn together in the harness so the two can be compared directly.

All values in centimetres. Slide is the planted foot's ground-relative displacement. Pop is the per-tick change in a joint's per-tick displacement, which separates a genuine discontinuity from a fast but smooth swing foot; at sprint the swing foot legitimately covers more than half a metre per tick, so the first difference alone is not a defect measure. Result: planted-foot slide is 0.0 cm at p95 and 0.0 cm mean for walk, jog, run and sprint on all three bodies. The only non-zero maximum is 1.4 cm, during a standing turn, where the feet deliberately step to new stance points.

Performance

Measured separately in the harness HUD and by the scaling probe: simulation step, animation and IK solve, skinning, character rendering, and compositing. The presentation stays capped at 60 fps and no GL buffer or canvas is recreated per frame: the character render target is grow-only and reused, and the region-of-interest pass reuses the same framebuffer across frames.

  • Simulation and animation are the meaningful CPU figures. Animation and IK cost about 0.10 ms per rig and scale linearly: 22 rigs solve in 2.3 ms, well inside the budget. A separate headless solver benchmark agrees at 0.16 ms per rig.
  • Character render and compositing here are measured under headless software OpenGL, which rasterises a 3000x1900 layer on the CPU. Those absolute numbers are not representative of a real GPU and should be read only for their structure, not their magnitude.
  • Draw calls equal the rig count — one merged draw per body, no per-bone draws.
  • No per-frame buffer or canvas recreation. The region-of-interest render target stays 3008x1920 across all three passes: it is allocated grow-only in multiples of 64 and reused. The frame cap held at 60 throughout.

Regression

Gate: sandbox/visual/tools/anim3d/of_loco_regress.js, 720 ticks covering every gait, both turns, acceleration and a stop.

  • Animation ON/OFF neutrality — authoritative x, y, vx, vy and facing are bit-identical with the presentation solve on and off, for all three bodies. Maximum absolute difference: 0.
  • Determinism — the same run twice gives bit-identical joint world positions. Maximum absolute difference: 0. No NaN.
  • No presentation RNG — no Math.random, Date.now or performance.now anywhere in the pose path. The harness calls performance.now only to fill its own timing panel.
  • Goalkeeper work — untouched. The locomotion lives in outfield files; the one shared file changed is the IK, where a per-chain knee-plane bound was added behind an opt-in field that only the outfield leg solve sets, so the goalkeeper path is byte-identical.
  • Camera — unchanged. The harness only sets the existing manual rail target, exactly as the goalkeeper playtest does.

Known limitations

  • Standing-turn foot drag. A planted foot is re-stepped when the stance point has moved 12 cm or the body has turned 28 degrees. Below those thresholds the foot is carried rather than stepped, so a large standing turn drags each foot up to about 2.3 cm before it replants. It is small and slow, but it is real sliding and it is the only place any is left.
  • Instant full reversal at speed. Holding right then pressing left in the same tick is the most extreme input the law allows. The body runs away from its planted foot faster than the leg can follow; the plant is released early and handed over, but the transition still shows up to about 50 cm of joint pop for a few ticks. Gradual and 90-degree turns are clean.
  • Sprint stride measurement is noisy. Contact is brief at 8.2 m/s and the fixture occasionally misses a plant, so the measured stride at sprint is less reliable than the configured value of 3.32 x leg length per cycle.
  • No upper-body detail yet. Arm swing is a counter-rotation with an elbow curve. There is no neck stabilisation, no shoulder girdle motion and no fatigue.
  • Contact is flat-ground only. The pitch is a plane; there is no terrain sampling.
  • No ball. Dribbling, carrying and any ball-aware footwork are explicitly out of scope for this pass.
