F — CPU profile and spike timeline

Where the time went: DRIBBLE-1.2 spent most of a cut's planning pass building poses (full RUN-1 + TURN-1.2 bodies, then full skeleton FK) and predicting branch motions, all inside one 240 Hz step (the 538 ms step). DRIBBLE-1.3: the same predictions are made once per motion and shared; a planner pose is TURN-1.2's pelvis and legs only (exact); the search is spread over ticks under the budget. TURN-1.2 prediction (t1Step, frozen) is now the largest cost (≈ 1,490 predicted ticks / s), mostly re-predicting the main motion after each pursuit re-aim — a DRIBBLE-1.2 behaviour. How DRIBBLE-1.3 works

  • Exact infrastructure (bit-identical to DRIBBLE-1.2 — the synchronous twin suite is 200 / 200 identical): each predicted tick keeps a state snapshot and evaluates its pose only when read; the planner's pose is TURN-1.2's pelvis and leg code transcribed onto its exports (217,961 / 217,961 poses bit-identical) with leg-chain-only FK; a time-warped swing is TURN-1.2's swing-leg branch alone (18,112 / 18,112 bit-identical); a checked pursuit correction's prediction becomes the main prediction at its switch tick (adoption) instead of being re-predicted; the rig's RUN-1 / TURN-1 speed tables are solved at load (they caused 50–100 ms first-use spikes).
  • Incremental planner: DRIBBLE-1.2's planning pass (candidates, ranking, versions, checks, choice) runs as a resumable search. Each tick it works until the tick's deterministic work units reach the budget (2 ms; 8 ms while no plan is in force or a meaningful cut is held), then pauses. It is abandoned only when what it reads changed — the input, the ball (a launch or contact), the last touch. The plan in force stays in force meanwhile. Release-now / staged options are evaluated for a release 24 ticks (100 ms) ahead, branched from the main prediction, so a search that spans ticks still releases exactly as evaluated. A found plan is installed when its deadline requires (anytime), else when the search ends; at installation its warp is re-derived from the swing's current state and its approach re-checked.
  • Broad / narrow: a release broad phase (if the free ball is beyond the body's reach from the release tick for the whole window, the turning motion is not predicted), distance and yaw gates, and cheap rejections before the exact approach check; only survivors get exact contact geometry, launch solving and path checks (exact contact-geometry evaluations 5.4k → 5.0k / s).
  • Set-up validation against BALL-PHYS: for a set-up, BALL-PHYS simulates the planned launch and its execution envelope — speed ±1.5σ × direction ±1.5σ (4 corners) and lift ±1.5σ — to the decisive tick; the decisive foot's exact nested search (DRIBBLE-1.2's, 8 tries over the full horizon) must find a contact for ≥ 5 of the 6 envelope balls. Calibrated on DRIBBLE-1.2's own set-ups (≥ 5 / 6: 12 taken, 1 lost; ≤ 4 / 6: 7 taken, 12 lost). Planning and execution stay separate: exec13.sigma / exec13.envelope is where player attributes will set the envelope (not implemented).
  • Prefetch: in a tick with budget left and no search running, the prediction the next search will read is extended ahead of time (the main prediction, or a pending pursuit correction's).
  • Determinism: the budget counts work, not time — the same inputs make the same decisions on any machine, in Chrome and in Node (20 / 20).

Defects and next step

Three biggest remaining V1 defects

  1. 90° cuts and 60° left are still near DRIBBLE-1.2's rates, not the targets: 90° right 5.1 / 8, 90° left 4.6 / 8, 60° left 5.4 / 8 over 96 seeds. DRIBBLE-1.3 made DRIBBLE-1.2 fast; it did not make it better. The lost cuts are the kinds DRIBBLE-1.2 also loses — e.g. 90° right seed 16: the same staged inside-of-left release one tick earlier, then a toe touch, a loose ball and a late laces contact off the line.
  2. Fewer clean cuts and a slower ball redirect: cleanly taken 114 vs 129 of 384, ball within 20° 1.62 vs 1.40 s (median). Two causes the twin suite logs: the 100 ms release lead (48 of 200 runs' first changed decision) and the stricter set-up check (set-ups 117 → 24 of 384 runs; the refused ones were often what made a cut clean).

CPU stretch and worst-case scenarios: 148 ms / s on average but 270–280 ms / s in CUT90_RUN / REV180_SPRINT, and a 22-player match with one dribbler is 842 ms / s on one core — 530 of it the other 21 players' plain TURN-1.2 locomotion.
