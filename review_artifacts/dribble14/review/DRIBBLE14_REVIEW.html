A — the known early-release failure: 90° right, seed 16

Reproduced first. DRIBBLE-1.3 (and DRIBBLE-1.4 before the fix) lose 90° right seed 16; DRIBBLE-1.2 takes it cleanly. The release-lead sweep below runs the release search to completion at every lead from 1 to 24 ticks. With contact ticks sampled every 3 ticks counted from the release tick, the outcome repeats with the lead modulo 3: a stage contact at tick 640 (speed-error draw +1.1σ → 6.87 m/s) gives the clean cut; at tick 639 (draw −1.9σ → 6.33 m/s) the slower ball meets the left foot's next swing 0.46 s later — a left-toe recovery, a loose ball, a late laces touch off the line; at tick 638 the cut is lost too. DRIBBLE-1.2's 1-tick release happened to land on 640; DRIBBLE-1.3's 24-tick lead on 639. The fix: the release search evaluates every tick of its contact window — the stage contact is tick 640 for all 24 leads — and the found release is re-evaluated at the earliest tick it can be decided (552 → 541). D1.4 takes this cut (the final release on the same tick as D1.2, 789); not cleanly — its cut touch comes at 815 rather than D1.2's 820 and turns the ball less, so the ball is inside 20° at 1.99 s.

B — best recovered 60° cut

60° left, seed 24. The planner proposes a set-up at 2.73 s (a left-foot nudge, then the outside of the left foot). Only 2 of its 6 execution variants keep a decisive contact, so DRIBBLE-1.3's rule (≥ 5) refuses it, plays a line touch and loses the cut. D — a rejected set-up

60° left, seed 4. D1.4's validator rejects a left-outside set-up at 2.77 s: only 1 of its 6 execution variants leaves the decisive foot a contact, robustness 0.17. With the check switched off (diagnostic override on the same page) that set-up is played at 6.1 m/s and the ball is lost at 3.53 s with no further touch. Nothing guarantees the dribble: the validator removes a set-up that would fail, it does not rescue the situation.

E — straight RUN

The set-up validator, audited

How the validator was audited. Every set-up the DRIBBLE-1.3 core makes with the set-up check off (so each one is executed and its outcome is known) was replayed to its commit and graded against the six BALL-PHYS execution variants the validator uses: for each, the number of slots (tick × held-back path phase) of the decisive window in which the decisive foot still meets that ball. Outcome: the planned decisive executed and the cut taken; catastrophic: the ball lost within 1 s. 89 set-ups from seeds 1–96 chose the rule, 79 from seeds 97–192 checked it. The 1.3 rule (≥ 5 of 6 variants reachable, one slot each suffices) keeps precision but rejects 59 % of the set-ups that work. The count of reachable variants hardly predicts the outcome; how much of the window survives does. DRIBBLE-1.4's rule: per variant, its coverage = slots / 4 (a comfortable window), at most 1; robustness R = the mean over the six variants; accepted if R ≥ 0.25 — four comfortable variants and two misses score 0.67, five barely-reachable variants and a miss 0.21. In the 384-run matrix it is the threshold that played best (0.5: 259 taken / 118 clean; 0.25: 265 / 123 before the release fix). About a quarter of accepted set-ups end as clean cuts whatever the threshold: the validator cannot pick clean ones, it removes the ones whose execution leaves no contact window.

Every run of the 384 whose outcome (lost / taken / clean) changed between D1.3 and D1.4 was re-run on both cores and its first changed decision classified from the plans and both decision logs (tools/d14_diff.mjs). 'Different contact opportunity' = a release-now / staged plan with another contact (the every-tick release window); 'planner-budget ordering' = another plan with no set-up or release change at its root (the validator's work shifts how far each tick's budget reaches); outcomes downstream of a changed decision are chaotic, which is why changes go both ways in every class.

What changed

  • Release contact window. The release search (release-now / staged options) evaluates every tick of its contact window instead of every third tick counted from the release tick. Which contacts exist no longer depends on when the search began. Work units rose 4 %.
  • Contact-timed release. When the release search has found a plan for its release tick, the same release is evaluated exactly at the earliest tick its evaluation can complete — now + the ticks the search just took (+ the search's own evalTicks margin) — branched from the main prediction there. Both plans stay in the found set and DRIBBLE-1.2's total cost chooses; if the earlier one is not complete in time or not as good, the found plan stands.
  • Graded set-up robustness. The set-up check still simulates the planned launch and its six execution variants with BALL-PHYS. For each variant it now counts how much of the decisive foot's contact window (tick × path phase) still meets that ball, instead of whether one slot does. The set-up is accepted on the mean coverage R ≥ 0.25 rather than ≥ 5 of 6 reachable.
  • Speed-error clearance. Every launch was already required to stay clear of the other leg for its +2σ lift and ±2σ direction errors. It is now also checked for its ±2σ speed error, the dimension DRIBBLE-1.2 left out.
  • Unchanged. TURN-1.2, the resolved touch, one authoritative impulse, the free physical ball, interruption, control loss, the contact geometry and its costs, the incremental planner and its budget. Randomness is keyed by (seed, player, tick, foot), so a changed decision changes only what it causes: 392 / 392 touches common to D1.3 and D1.4 draw identical errors.

Limitations and next step

Three limitations to leave to attributes / defenders

  1. 90° cuts redirect slower than DRIBBLE-1.2. Median ball-within-20° 2.14 s vs D1.2's 1.71 s for 90° right; taken more often (5.67 vs 5.33 / 8) but clean 16 vs 32 of 96. Ruled out tonight: planner latency (unbudgeted: 18 clean), the release lead (instant release: 13), release-first ordering (compare-all: 16). Present in every 1.3 / 1.4 configuration: it comes from DRIBBLE-1.3's search semantics (most likely the cap of 3 set-up validations per search or the release search stopping at its first valid plan). This one is a foundation defect, not an attribute matter.
  2. Weak foot and the preferred foot (out of scope here). Decisive cuts pick the foot the geometry favours; preferred-foot bias belongs to the attribute / utility layer.
  3. How tight and quick a 90° turn is, against pressure. Agility, ball control and a defender's pressure should decide how sharp a generic player's 90° turn can be. Do not tune the generic player toward 8 / 8.

Recommended next step

ONE MORE FOUNDATION FIX (DRIBBLE-1.5, narrow): find which of DRIBBLE-1.3's search caps slows the 90° redirect — set-up validations per search (maxSetups 3), the release search's first-valid stop (rStop1), the cut-at-contact version (off) — by restoring each to DRIBBLE-1.2's semantics under the budget on the 384-run matrix; adopt the one that brings clean 90° cuts back toward D1.2 (≈ 32 + 16 of 192) without losing the 60° and taken gains or the CPU guardrail. Then freeze DRIBBLE-V1 and move to Dribbling / Ball Control / Agility.
