Part A — execution variance around Alternative B

Every contact already had keyed per-dimension execution draws (RECEIVE-KILL-1 … 1.2): what the review tiles showed was the TYPICAL draw — each dimension at its median magnitude — and the 200-seed tables were distributions. Phase 1 keeps curve B and that structure and makes the draws bounded, re-keyed (seed | RECEIVE | receiver | receive event | contact index | receive-control-v2 | dimension | draw index) and named by physical dimension (damping, direction, vertical; the turn touch adds weight). σ = k · vRef · D · E(BC, D): the spread scales with the rating's error (BC 99 ≈ 1/24 of BC 10's) and with the ball's difficulty (D and its compression c(D) = D/(D+1)), so easy balls stay tight.

A1 · The bounded draw

d = N for |N| ≤ 1.5; otherwise ±(1.5 + 1 · tanh((|N| − 1.5) / 1)). The central 87 % of draws are unchanged, the median magnitude (0.674 — the TYPICAL draw) is exactly preserved, N = 2 → 1.96, 3 → 2.41, nothing beyond 2.5. Chosen over a truncated normal (same core, but a kink at the cut and an inverse-CDF to audit) and a triangular / Irwin–Hall draw (bounded, but it reshapes the whole distribution, so B would move): it is continuous, C¹, seeded, auditable in one line, and leaves B's centre where it was.

Part B — the stationary receive-and-turn library

A stationary receiver faces the passer and knows the space before the ball arrives: desiredTurnAngle θ = the exit relative to his initial facing (0 = straight back toward the passer, ±180 = directly away). One authored action — preparation during the ball's travel, the first touch, the rotation, the exit — with the ball the only authority. Research basis and what is our own design: research report.
