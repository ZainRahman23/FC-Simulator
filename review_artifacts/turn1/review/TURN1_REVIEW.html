The system

Base. The frozen RUN-1.3 straight-line run (unmodified) supplies the cyclic athletic motion at the current speed; straight running through TURN-1 stays RUN-1 (plant slip 0.02 cm, the same placement).
Dynamics. The COM's horizontal velocity changes only while a foot is on the turf — every stance carries a planned ΔV with a half-sine force profile, each bounded by what that stance can deliver (contact time, travel, ≤ 17.5 m/s² mean). The turn is therefore made by the plants, not by rotating a root.
Feet. Each plant is a fixed world point chosen at touchdown: RUN-1's own placement plus the inverted-pendulum offset of the stance's mean acceleration (outside the curve, ahead of a brake, wide of a cut), inside the leg's reachable window. The stance foot rolls heel → flat → toe pad (RUN-1's profile, heel-first for braking / plants, pivoting about the toe pad in reversals); the leg reaches it by analytic IK, with a predicted pelvis drop when a plant needs it. The swing foot follows RUN-1's own swing path warped onto the aimed plant.
Body. Pelvis heading is a state of its own (facing ≠ velocity); gaze and chest lead it; whole-body lean follows each step's planned acceleration; crouch, arm balance and a smoothed base phase complete the organisation.
Resolver. A request (incoming velocity + requested change + stride phase / foot availability) becomes an ARC, a CUT (side-step on the outside foot, crossover on the inside foot ≤ 70° when ≥ 0.2 s sooner) or a PIVOT (≥ 135°, or an inside-foot pivot ≥ 110° when sooner), with braking steps planned from what a braking stance can actually remove. Before the plant the request can be retargeted, abandoned or flipped; from the plant's touchdown it is committed (exit direction ±20°).
Cost. ≈ 0.012 ms per 240 Hz step and ≈ 0.065 ms per pose per character (warm), Node.

Minimum authored library (recommendation)

Recommended minimum set: 1 cyclic base + 3 plant bases + 2 step modifiers + 1 procedural layer.
  1. RUN-1.3 (existing): the cyclic run, 2.5–9.5 m/s, speed-blended. No new speed variants.
  2. ARC — procedural, no authored data: per-stance lateral impulses, bank, outside-foot placement, pelvis / gaze lead.
  3. SIDE-STEP PLANT (45–135°, the core cut): one base; entry speed, contact, crouch and redirect fraction interpolate continuously with angle and speed (TURN1_sweep.json).
  4. CROSSOVER PLANT (inside foot, ≤ 70°): a variant of the side-step base (narrow placement, foot turned); worth keeping because it saves up to a stride (≈ 0.25 s) when the outside foot is not next.
  5. PIVOT PLANT (≥ 135°; inside-foot fallback ≥ 110°): genuinely distinct biomechanics — stop on a plant ahead and rotate on the ball, reversal of the velocity along the incoming line.
  6. BRAKE step (modifier): shorter-travel heel-first contact, back-lean, moderate crouch.
  7. EXIT / ACCEL step (modifier): short flight, forward lean, the remaining turn.
Mirror: exact by construction (verified in 32 cases) — never author left and right. Interpolate: inside each plant base across angle, and every base across speed. Distinct: side-step vs pivot (different contact mechanics) and crossover vs side-step (different foot). Next authored content with the most visual value: upper-body key poses per plant base (arms / shoulders / head), a braking posture, and a below-2.5 m/s stop / walk base for standing turns (RUN-1 has none).

DRIBBLE-1 interface

TURN1.t1State(A) — at the current step, in the anim3d world frame: per foot the ankle, toe tip, heel and ball-of-foot positions and the full 4×4 transform; linear velocities of ankle / toe tip / ball and the foot's angular velocity (one-step forward difference of a cloned, identically advanced state); support (R / L / flight), stance or swing progress and step role; the plant (world point, yaw, time since touchdown) or, for a swinging foot, the aimed next touchdown (time, point, yaw, role); pelvis position / velocity / heading / yaw rate; COM position, velocity, speed, acceleration; desired vs actual direction, facing, gaze; stride segment / phase; the turn state (mode, technique, phase, requested change, plant foot).
TURN1.t1Predict(A, horizon, every) — a deterministic lookahead on a clone: where each foot's ankle, toe tip and ball will be, and which foot supports, over the next horizon seconds if the input does not change. This is the question DRIBBLE-1 asks: where will each foot actually be, and can it reach the ball?
TURN1.t1Clone(A) — an independent copy (advancing it with the same input reproduces the original exactly).

Simulation / presentation boundary

TURN-1 is presentation and runs on its own inputs (desired direction and speed); nothing in the match changes. Its stance-impulse COM model and its footstep plan, however, are exactly what decides where the feet are and when the velocity changes, which physics-based dribbling will make outcome-relevant. Recommendation: make the TURN-1 locomotion law (stance plans, ΔV per stance, footstep positions, pivot / plant timing, swing aims) the authoritative locomotion law of the simulation — it is cheap (≈ 0.012 ms per step) and deterministic — and let presentation add only the cosmetic layers (pose, lean, arms, gaze). Facts the simulation must then own and expose: input (desired direction, desired speed); COM position and velocity; support state; current stance plan (ΔV, contact, flight); plants (positions, yaw, timing); swing aims and progress; pelvis facing; turn / maneuver state; and, for DRIBBLE-1, the contact-capable foot trajectories. Today's authoritative law (match.js) is a direction-split velocity limiter (8.5 m/s² forward, 12 braking, 10 lateral) with facing at 4–7 rad/s — a presentation driven by it could not show plants that cause the turn. Animation ON / OFF neutrality holds as long as the pose only reads that state, as here.

Three biggest V1 defects

  1. Reversals / pivots (≥ 135°) are the weakest family: from a sprint they take five braking steps (new heading at +1.6 s), the pivot sits in a deep crouch, and a pivot on the inside foot slides 1.5–2 cm at the toe pad.
  2. The plant's body language is procedural, not authored: lean (≤ 28°), pelvis rotation, crouch and arm abduction; there are no plant key poses for the arms, shoulders and head, and braking steps have no strong dig-in back-lean — at the gameplay camera's scale the cut reads mainly through the trajectory, the speed change and the pelvis rotation.
  3. Momentum and timing are compromises: the plant's horizontal force is dramatised (≈ 2.8 g peak); latency depends on the stride phase (a 90° cut at a sprint plants 0.66–0.94 s after the request depending on which foot is next); below 2.5 m/s RUN-1 has no gait (walk / stop), so pivots near standstill reuse its slowest run timing; one retargeting case keeps a single-step foot slap (≈ 12 cm).

Measurements, every scenario

Determinism: every scenario replayed twice in one process with identical state hashes (240 Hz log + 60 Hz joint positions). Continuity: the largest 240 Hz residual |p(t+h) − 2p(t) + p(t−h)| of the pelvis and of any tracked joint (pelvis, ankles, toe tips, hands, head); RUN-1.3's own straight running shows 14–36 mm at foot contact events (the landing transient), < 1 mm at the pelvis. Slip: horizontal motion of the loaded contact point (heel, or toe pad once the heel is up / during a pivot) over 10–90 % of each stance.
