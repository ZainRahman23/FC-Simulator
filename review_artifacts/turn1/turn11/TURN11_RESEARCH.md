# TURN-1.1 — research notes behind the authored body language

These notes were read on 16 Oct 2026; TURN-1's own sources are in `../TURN1_RESEARCH.md`. They are laboratory findings, mostly about planned cuts. Touchline exaggerates some shapes on purpose so that they read at the gameplay camera's scale; those places are marked **(dramatised)**.

| Finding | Source | What TURN-1.1 does with it |
|---|---|---|
| A wide foot and a torso leaning **away** from the cut raise the knee valgus moments in a side-step; so does a torso rotating away. | Dempsey et al. 2007, *MSSE* 39:1765 | The side-step leans the trunk **into** the new direction (≤ 22° on top of TURN-1's acceleration lean), and the shoulders lead the hips toward it. |
| Training a foot closer to the midline with a more upright torso lowered the valgus loads. | Dempsey et al. 2009, *AJSM* 37:2194 | Not applied as a constraint: TURN-1's plant width is frozen. The cue is used the other way round: the pelvis is carried toward the wide plant, so the body loads over the support. |
| Constraining the arm on the plant side (holding a stick or ball) raised the knee abduction moment by 29–60 %. Free arms are part of the cutting technique. | Chaudhari et al. 2005, summarised in a 2020 systematic review (Sports Medicine Open) | The arms are an active part of every base. The outside arm is the balancing arm at the load; both arms then drive the first step. |
| Cut width, knee valgus, toe landing, approach speed and cutting angle predicted the knee abduction moment. | Kristianslund et al. 2012, *BJSM* | Intensity scales with angle and approach speed (a sprint cut leans further and spreads the arms wider). The plant geometry stays TURN-1's. |
| Side-step, crossover and split-step are distinct techniques with kinetic and kinematic differences. | Review, "Biomechanical comparison of cutting techniques" | Two of them are bases. The crossover is authored as hips opening early, a tall trunk and the free leg crossing in front, not as the side-step's pose on the other foot. |
| Faster 180° turners show more hip, knee and ankle flexion at the penultimate contact, more medial trunk lean, and more pelvis and foot rotation. Coaches are advised to "align the body toward the exit". | Dos'Santos et al., the determinants of the modified and traditional 505 | The pivot turns the hips side-on before the plant (≤ 35°) and leads the rotation with the shoulders and the outside arm. The penultimate braking stance lowers and braces. |
| COM lowering in the plant: 0.26–0.29 m at 90°, 0.33–0.35 m at 180°. | Falch et al. 2020 (TURN-1's table) | Depth at the plant is real and is kept: it is reach-driven and unchanged. What changed is that the body is never low **and** upright (the "sitting" look). |

**Readability at the Touchline camera:** the player is about 31 px tall in a 720p frame and 46 px at 1080p (CAMERA_V1 at about 83 m). These shapes are therefore deliberately larger than the laboratory means **(dramatised)**:
- the trunk lean into the cut (≈ 37° total at the load);
- the braking back-lean (≈ 17°);
- the outside arm held nearly straight and near horizontal at the load (arm span 0.85 → 1.06–1.10 m);
- the free knee carried in front.
