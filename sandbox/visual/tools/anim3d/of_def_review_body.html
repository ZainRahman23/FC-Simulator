Touchline — Defending V1

branch prototype/3d-animation-pipeline · built on baseline/possession-v1.1-handoff (a30ddab) · frozen controls baseline/outfield-runtime-v1, baseline/shooting-v1, baseline/receiving-passing-v1.1, baseline/possession-v1.1-handoff untouched · local only, nothing pushed

A carrier dribbles at you. You jockey (face him, side-step, back off) and pick your moment. A standing tackle is a short lunge with a real contact tick: the tackling boot either reaches the ball or it doesn't. When it does, the recovered match law decides clean win, 50-50 poke or beaten. A slide is a genuine commitment. The body launches at its own pace and decelerates along the pitch; the leg sweeps ahead and meets the ball, or the attacker's legs, or nothing. Then the player is on the ground and has to get up. The simulation decides all of it. The skeleton shows the body doing what was decided, and the rendered boot or leg is measured against the ball at the contact tick.

2 · Scope

Included:

  • jockey (manual and AI);
  • standing tackle (WON / POKE / BEATEN / MISS);
  • slide tackle (WON / POKE / GLANCE / MISS), with ground slide, get-up and recovery;
  • body occupancy;
  • slide-into-body contact;
  • foul facts;
  • interceptions through the existing reception law;
  • the loose-ball aftermath through the existing reception and carry laws;
  • attribute architecture;
  • live harness, auto demos, and the 1v1 / 2v2 / 3v3 small-sided drills.

Deferred:

  • team tactics and pressing schemes, offside;
  • a referee (calls, cards, advantage);
  • injury, ragdoll, trips and falls for the tackled player;
  • shoulder-to-shoulder duels and shielding as an action;
  • aerial duels;
  • any goalkeeper change;
  • attribute tuning;
  • a tackle-avoidance AI for carriers.

3 · Architecture

  • pt_defend.js (SIMULATION) is active only when the drill is flagged defending. Every other squad fixture, and all single-player play, runs byte-identically (section 12).
    • Hooks: one movement override in ptPlayerStep (a jockey speed cap and facing, the lunge's desired velocity, the slide's own velocity, input locked during actions).
    • After every player has moved: occupancy, then all defensive contacts due this tick, then receptions and the ball.
  • anim3d/of_defend.js (PRESENTATION) reads each player's action, result and plan, and never writes the simulation.
    • Its link is only called for defending drills.
    • With the animation OFF the simulation runs on bit-identically (section 12).
  • anim3d/of_defdemo.js is the auto defending demo: inputs only.
  • of_squad.js holds the drills, keys, HUD and overlay; of_player.js the key legend and demo hooks.

4 · Jockey

Hold Z without the ball. The facing is held on the carrier (or the loose ball) at every speed, and the desired speed is capped at 2.6 m/s. The ordinary locomotion limiter and gait do the rest: side-steps, and the back-pedal when moving away from the facing. The posture is a presentation delta over the locomotion (lower hips, wider base, trunk over the ball, arms out), slewed in over 0.18 s. It is not magnetic: you move him. The AI press contains goal-side at 1.7 m and steps in to 0.95 m the longer it is engaged. When beaten it turns and runs (no jockey) and may slide.

5 · Standing tackle

Space without the ball. The simulation decides everything below; the rendered boot then meets the ball within a couple of centimetres when it reached, and falls visibly short when it did not.

  • Tackling foot. The one on the ball's side of his body line (rig-correct right = (−dirY, dirX)). There is no preferred-foot rule.
  • Wind-up. A 0.20 s lunge: the desired velocity goes toward the ball at up to 1.6 m/s, through the ordinary limiter.
  • Contact tick. The ball is measured from the tackling-side hip on the ground.
    • In reach (≤ 0.72 × leg, not behind him, ≤ 0.45 m high) → the tackle quality law.
    • Out of reach → MISS, with the reason recorded (OUT_OF_REACH / BEHIND_HIM / TOO_HIGH).
  • Contact-geometry terms. Stretch beyond 60 % of reach costs up to −0.25. Tackling from behind the carrier costs −0.20.
  • Ball result. WON: blocked and dragged toward the tackler's feet, and he collects it through the reception law. POKE: knocked away at 4 m/s, and the carrier is excluded 0.35 s. BEATEN: the carrier keeps it.
  • Recovery. 0.30 s after a contact, 0.55 s after a miss or a beaten tackle, at reduced pace.
{{STAND_TABLE}}

"inside-face–ball" is the boot's medial surface point against the ball's surface at the contact tick, after the complete solve (the receiving metric). "leg–ball" is the rendered shin and boot capsules against the ball. The reach is a tracking reach with the soft extension limit, so a miss shows the leg short of the ball, never a hyperextended knee.

6 · Slide tackle

F without the ball. The slide's travel is not animated: it emerges from launch speed and deceleration. The ball's result comes from the same quality law as the standing tackle, reached only through the leg meeting the ball.

  • Movement (authoritative).
    • Wind-up 0.10 s: momentum carries, input is locked.
    • Launch at v0 = clamp(own speed + 1, 4.2, 7.5) m/s along the aimed direction.
    • Uniform deceleration of 5.5 m/s², so the distance follows from his pace (about 1.6–5 m).
    • Then 0.25 s on the ground, a 0.85 s get-up, and 0.35 s at reduced pace. No receptions while on the ground.
  • Contact.
    • The tackling leg is a capsule from 0.46 to 1.12 × leg ahead of the root, 0.11 × leg to its side. These dimensions are taken from the rendered slide body.
    • It extends over the first 0.12 s after launch.
    • It is swept against the moving ball in 4 sub-steps per tick.
  • Result. The quality law (+0.10 for a slide, −0.30 from behind, −0.15 if the ball meets the shin rather than the boot) → WON (cleared along the slide at 0.6 × slide speed + 1.5 m/s) / POKE (deflected) / GLANCE (the carrier rides it).
  • Body contact. If the leg meets the carrier's body, that is recorded with its tick and whether the ball came first, and the slide stops there: a leg cannot slide through a standing player.
  • Plan. A read-only look-ahead of the same sweep, like the reception plan. The presentation uses it to track the boot onto the coming contact.

Slide biomechanics (V1.1, see section 6b)

The ground key poses are solved, not hand-set: target knee, ankle, hand and head positions, fitted through the rig's own forward kinematics with anatomical bounds (hip rotation ≤ 50°, abduction ≤ 50°, knee 0–140°) and "nothing through the pitch". The normal slide lies side-on on the tucked-leg hip: one tackling leg along the pitch, the other folded under it, the tucked-side hand braced, the chest up and the head watching the ball. Every body is lowered by exactly the amount that puts its lowest point on the pitch. The body's yaw turns onto the slide through the wind-up, and a per-body presentation offset lays the rendered tackling leg on the simulation's leg line. The get-up rolls onto the tucked knee with the tackling foot planted (kneel), then crouch, then locomotion, timed to the simulation's ground and get-up windows.

6b · Slide-tackle biomechanics review (V1.1)

What the research says

Sources: coaching manuals and technique guides (Human Kinetics Soccer three basic tackles; Soccer Coach Weekly; Coaching American Soccer; Holliston SC Teaching the Slide Tackle; Online Soccer Academy); encyclopedic and technical summaries (Encyclopedia.com Soccer Tackling Mechanics; Wikipedia Sliding tackle); and IFAB Law 12 for the reckless and two-footed boundary. They agree on one shape, with small variations by purpose.

aspectordinary controlled slide tackle
approachfrom the side or diagonally, across the carrier's path, never from behind; running alongside until the lead foot can overtake the ball, ideally when the ball is a touch away from him
which body side is on the groundone hip / the outer thigh of the tucked leg (the leg nearest the carrier "sinks down and curls beneath the seat"). The slider is side-on, not sitting on both buttocks.
hips / torsohips rolled onto the tucked side and twisted, "drawing up the far side"; torso bent at the waist and turned to the ball, not lying flat on the back; eyes on the ball
tackling legONE leg, the far one, extended but "slightly bent", swept across the ball's path (the "sweep"); timing: horizontal when the foot reaches the ball
tackling footankle locked, toes forward; laces / instep to clear it or the inside, hooked (curled back) to trap it; contact at the ball's centre to upper half; never studs up
trailing legknee bent ≈ 90°, tucked under the extended leg's thigh ("the lower part of the tucked leg under the upper thigh of the extended leg"); the two knees stay close; used afterwards to push back up
armsout for balance; the tucked-side hand goes to the pitch for support
after contact / recoveryroll toward the stomach or tackling side with the momentum and back onto the tackling foot, pushing with the tucked leg: up at once
purpose variantsclearance / poke: laces, the ball kicked away. Hook: inside of a hooked foot, a wider sweep. Block (a shot or cross coming across): the same one-leg-out / one-leg-under shape, more front-on, the blocking leg held at ball height ("like sliding into second base"). Last-ditch: the same shape at full stretch and flatter. All of them are one extended leg plus one tucked leg.
two-footed / studs exposedboth legs thrust at the opponent with the soles forward is a lunge. Law 12: a two-footed lunge that endangers an opponent is serious foul play (a sending-off) even if the ball is won. It is not a technique variant.

What was wrong with the V1 pose, measured at the authoritative contact tick

{{SLIDE_DIAG}}

The cause is the pose data, in combination:

  • a square, supine pelvis (sitting on both buttocks facing the attacker);
  • a knee-up trailing leg whose foot is planted ahead beside the tackling leg (a second foot forward, knees close together);
  • a tackling foot whose sole faces the ball (studs forward).

It is not caused by mirroring (both sides read the same), IK, or contact targeting (the reach correction is a few centimetres). The V1 fit kept the pelvis square: with a square pelvis the researched tuck needed more than 50° of hip rotation, so the solver fell back to a knee-up brace. That braced leg is what reads as "both feet forward".

What changed

  • New normal slide key pose. Solved with the pelvis free, targeting the researched posture:
    • tucked-side hip 16 cm lower than the other (lying on it);
    • tackling leg along the pitch, knee ≈ 160°, sole facing down and sideways (laces / instep toward the ball);
    • trailing leg folded under the tackling thigh;
    • tucked-side hand on the pitch, free arm out, chest up, head up.
  • Leg alignment. A per-body presentation offset (yaw plus pelvis shift, measured once per body and side) lays the rendered knee→toe line on the simulation's leg line. The root is untouched.
  • Get-up. The "sit up" step is dropped: slide → kneel on the tucked knee with the tackling foot planted → crouch → up.
  • V1 pose retained as the review-only presentation variant OF_DEF.slide.variant = "reckless_v1". It is never selected in play and never mixed in. Because it is presentation only, it cannot change reach, timing or success.
  • What a future foul system needs. A reckless two-footed challenge would have to be a simulation decision (a challenge intent), recorded as a fact alongside the existing ones: from behind, closing speed, ball-first or man-first, tackle quality. That is not built now.

Vocabulary. One parameterised normal slide covers the researched purposes, because all of them share the one-leg-out, one-leg-under shape. The simulation already varies the direction, speed, distance and contact point, and the tracking reach adapts the boot. A separate block or last-ditch pose would need a simulation reason (a different contact geometry), which V1 does not have. So no more variants were made.

7 · Bodies and contact

  • Occupancy. world.py PLAYER PHYSICAL OCCUPANCY V1 is ported for defending drills: 0.32 m discs, closing velocity removed, positional Jacobi mop-up (8 iterations, 5 mm tolerance). A jockeying or lunging defender cannot walk into the carrier, and a defender behind the carrier cannot get at a ball the carrier's body shields. That is the retention fixtures' out-of-reach result.
  • Not modelled in V1 (deferred). No ragdoll, no trip or fall, no push-off. A slide that reaches the carrier's body stops. Upper bodies can still brush visually in close contests (limbs are not collided).

8 · Foul facts (recorded, never called)

The playtest has no referee, so nothing is decided from these. Every challenge records what continuous.py _adjudicate_foul consumes, plus what a future referee would need.

  • Recorded, matching the logit's inputs. tq (the tackle quality), from_behind, the contact kind (standing / slide tackle vs body contact), and the closing speed that feeds the card severity.
  • Recorded for location. The carrier's position (penalty box or not), with every player's position in the trace (the goal-side count).
  • Engine-owned. Aggression, standing tackle and awareness are the profile's. pressure and desperation are the engine's own.
  • Beyond the Python model. The ball contact tick against the opponent contact tick (ball first / man first), the tackling foot, the contact point and the relative velocity.

9 · Interceptions and the loose ball

  • A defender in a passing lane receives through the V1.1 boot-realised reception, with its hostile outcome branch. There is no radius, no teleport and no new law. The auto demo's "intercept" pattern and the small-sided games show interceptions and loose touches.
  • After a WON or POKE the ball is simply free. The nearest player of each team goes for it (AI), and whoever's boot meets it first takes it through the reception law.
  • The dispossessed carrier is excluded for continuous.py's windows (0.5 s after a clean win, 0.35 s after a poke). Otherwise possession contests flow with no reset.

10 · Attributes (architecture only)

  • Every attribute enters through ptDefAttrs (the reference profile = 60 everywhere) and ptDefK, a decision factor that is exactly 1 at the reference. Nothing is tuned; no OVR.
  • Standing Tackle / Sliding Tackle / Reactions. The tackle quality law, as continuous.py.
  • Strength / Balance. The carrier's resistance in the same law.
  • Defensive Awareness. The AI containment distance.
  • Aggression. The AI's exposure threshold for challenging.
  • Interceptions. Reserved in the profile; the reception law's hostile branch is where it will enter.
  • Agility / Acceleration. Through the existing locomotion limiter, unchanged.
  • The retention fixture's carrier uses strength and balance of 80–90, which is why it takes a tight ball from behind as BEATEN.

14 · Limitations and next

  • No trip or fall for the tackled player (deferred with ragdolls). A slide that meets his legs stops; he carries on.
  • Limb-to-limb contact is not collided: arms and upper bodies can brush through each other in close contests. Body discs cannot overlap.
  • Slide leg residual is 1–8 cm at the contact tick. At ≈6 m/s the leg moves 10 cm per tick, so sub-tick contacts are rendered at the tick's final state. The standing tackle's inside face is within ≈3 cm.
  • AI. The AI is deliberately simple:
    • no tackle-avoidance by carriers;
    • the presser tackles on a fixed exposure threshold;
    • the slide rule fires when beaten and 1.2–2.6 m from the ball.
  • Recorded for later, not fixed. Dribbling V1's shared boot plan (ofBootPlan) is laterally mirrored relative to the rig. The known receive → shot hold residual from V1.1 also remains.
