Touchline — Receiving + Passing V1

branch prototype/3d-animation-pipeline · control baseline/outfield-runtime-v1 (23b7af4, untouched) · local only, nothing pushed

A controls → passes → the authoritative ball travels freely → B's boot meets it at the tick the simulation chose → possession and your control move to B → B walks, jogs, dribbles, passes or shoots straight out of the touch. The simulation decides the pass, the reception, the foot and the outcome; the skeleton only explains them. Animation ON and OFF give bit-identical outcomes, and every pre-existing scenario is identical to the frozen baseline.

2 · Receiving architecture

  • Plan (simulation, every tick while the ball is free). The ball path is integrated forward with the real free-ball law. For each candidate receiver, both boots are placed per look-ahead tick: a standing receiver (≤ 1.2 m/s) at his idle stance, a moving one by the stride-clock boot track restricted to its contact window (late swing → early stance). The first tick the ball enters a boot's comfort zone (0.20 leg + ball radius) is the contact; otherwise the closest approach inside reach (0.36 leg + radius) is a stretch. Balls behind the body line or above 0.45 m are not foot receptions.
  • Receiving-foot policy (a score, geometry dominant): sooner, less stretch, a boot not bearing weight, the ball's side, a directional touch across the body with the far foot's inside, preferred foot +0.15 (a bias), hysteresis so a plan does not flip-flop. No alternation rule, no forced preferred foot.
  • Resolve. On the contact tick: touch_model outcome from the relative speed (reference profile), world.py's velocity law, possession + control transfer on CLEAN. The receiver faces the ball's incoming line while it comes, and the push direction just after control.
  • Presentation. One parameterised action: prepare (support knee gives, trunk over the ball, receiving hip opens and foot turns out), meet with the inside face of the boot on the ball's leading side at the authoritative tick (bounded reach), stay with the ball briefly, then a standing receiver sets the boot down where it is and steps home; a moving one hands the boot back to the stride. Measured at the final solved state (after every FK rebuild, ground clamp and reach).

11 · Findings and known limitations

  • Pre-existing: Dribbling V1's shared boot plan is laterally mirrored. ofBootPlan puts the "R" boot where the rig renders the left foot (measured facing east / west / south: longitudinal match, lateral side swapped). world.py's own convention matches the rig. Changing it would move Dribbling V1's authoritative foot choice and timing, so it is untouched; the reception planner and the pass approach use the rig-correct side. Worth a decision in the dribbling polish pass.
  • Moving receptions still add pops just before contact (a receiver braking into the ball, a running take) on top of the locomotion's own pops in the same fixtures — see §8. Standing receptions and stretches are close to the no-receiving control.
  • After the touch a standing receiver takes one recovery step home (the idle stance logic); readable, but it is a step, not yet a weight shift.
  • The outcome model uses a neutral reference profile with no noise: failures come from speed (HEAVY / DEFLECT) and from geometry (out of reach, behind), not yet from pressure or attributes. The inputs are named (PT_RECV.ref, c.attrs, the noise slot) for Ball Control, Technique, Composure, Reactions, standing tackle; Short Passing, Vision, Agility and weak-foot quality are identified but not wired.
  • Passive defenders (drill 9) only shadow lanes and intercept what reaches their boots; no tackling, pressing or team AI.
  • No aerial passing, chest / thigh control, headers or volleys (V1 scope). A ball above 0.45 m is not received with the foot.
  • The hard-reversal "roulette" is untouched.
