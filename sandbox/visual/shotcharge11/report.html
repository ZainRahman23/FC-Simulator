One charge-defined apex request, solved through the frozen physical world before contact. The 30-case matrix uses provisional centre-apex anchors of 0.32 m at minimum / 3.00 m at maximum. All three maximum anchors (2.7 / 3.0 / 3.3 m) and three tap anchors (0.22 / 0.32 / 0.45 m) have also been tested at every rating. No winner is selected.

Matched apex is not an identical path. The report exposes early heights, apex location, descent and landing. At full charge, first-flight time remains about 1.49–1.50 s under the same gravity; the stronger shot covers more distance in that time and reaches the 19 m plane sooner. There is no time scaling or post-launch assistance.

What changed

q = clamp((holdMs − 80) / 520, 0, 1)
f = 0.40 + 0.60 × [1 − (1 − q)²]
speed = frozen BALL-SHOT-1.2 Laces capability × f

g = q³ × (4 − 3q)
desired centre apex = A_min + (A_max − A_min) × g
provisional A_min = 0.32 m; A_max = 3.00 m

Solve θ before contact using unmodified physical previews:
v_forward = speed × cos θ
v_vertical = speed × sin θ
spin = unchanged [0, 0, −4] rad/s

One launch → frozen BALL-PHYS → flight / landing / bounce / roll

The exact previous charge and speed functions are imported unchanged. The new solver does not use the previous charge angle. It holds total launch speed fixed while reconciling its forward and vertical components. A deterministic 0–45° physical search uses 30 bisections; unreachable objectives fail explicitly rather than changing an anchor or the speed. The largest measured apex error across all 50 unique review cases is below 0.000000006 m at the native 240 Hz measurement resolution.

One apex objective was sufficient here: full-charge angles remain 13.56–20.79° and normal-charge angles 9.32–14.44°, with ordinary smooth rise/descent and positive forward motion. A second target would introduce a tradeoff without another free launch degree of freedom at fixed speed. No landing or goal distance is solved. This is a finding for this fixed clean Laces setup, not a claim about every future shooting context.
