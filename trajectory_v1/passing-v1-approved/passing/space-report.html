Through Ball · depth, then a dying ball

q = clamp(hold / 400 ms, 0, 1)
requestedDepth = 12 + 24 × q metres
requestedEndpointSpeed = 1.2 m/s
backspin = +0.6 × launchSpeed / 0.11 rad/s (canonical forward +x)

Depth is measured horizontally from the passer to the intended space. Human aim supplies a ray; the selected runner’s velocity adds a 0.55 s path projection before that ray is normalized to the requested depth. Equivalently, aim is an offset from the selected runner’s projected path. The same runner stays selected regardless of Long Passing. Charge controls distance; the physical solve determines launch pace.

The default runner starts 6 m ahead. Aim freely to change the space. If a requested endpoint is behind a moving runner, the lab reports it; it does not secretly extend the depth. A stationary runner falls back to the aim direction. No arrival synchronization or reception is forced.

A bounded secant search jointly determines launch velocity and backspin under the stated technique constraint. Every candidate is simulated in the existing BALL-PHYS world until it slows to 1.2 m/s. Its distance is matched to the requested depth. The maximum is 17 candidate simulations, with a 32 m/s search guard. There is zero authored vertical velocity and no post-launch change, braking zone or attraction to the endpoint.

What spin actually does on this turf: backspin starts the ball sliding and the contact torque consumes it, eventually producing forward rolling spin. The frozen turf’s rolling resistance is stronger than its sliding friction, so the transition into rolling provides the sharper late slowdown. Backspin extends the sliding phase; it does not create an extra late braking force. The same-speed control below makes this limitation explicit.

Measured target-plane speeds are approximately 1.15–1.21 m/s, not exact coordinate stops. Tiny solve residuals reflect discrete turf transitions. The ball continues physically beyond the endpoint and comes to rest naturally.

Long Passing · execution only

x = ((99 − LongPassing) / 89)^2.1
σyaw = (0.015 + 5.5 × x) × D degrees
σweight = (0.0007 + 0.25 × x) × D
actualSpeed = clamp(idealSpeed × (1 + σweight × Zweight), 0.5, 32)

Initial backspin magnitude stays at the solved ideal value; it is rotated to the executed direction. This allows weight error to change where the ball dies and its remaining pace, without a separate spin-error model. No random elevation. Long Passing is the only accuracy attribute. Vision, Shot Power, Short Passing, Curve and Crossing do not influence this execution.

Fixed 200 ms / 24 m intent and one execution draw. Actual endpoint = where the real ball first slows to 1.2 m/s; actual target speed is separately measured at the intended plane. A missing crossing means the ball died short.

Cross · an inside-foot whipped delivery

q = clamp(hold / 550 ms, 0, 1)
requestedRange = 20 + 50 × q metres
sideSpin = footSign × 56 × (Curve / 75)^2.7 rad/s
canonical spin = [0, sideSpin, +8] rad/s
requestedApex = 3 + 5 × (1 − exp(−range / 35)) metres
arrivalHeight = 1.2 m, descending

Human aim and the selected target region define direction. Charge places the receiving point at the requested range along that direction. There is no automatic near-post, penalty-spot or far-post snap, and no automatic moving-receiver lead for this space-delivery action.

Each candidate physically flies to descending 1.2 m height. A bounded two-variable Newton solve matches horizontal range and apex with speed and elevation. Its sideways endpoint offset determines the outside launch yaw before contact. Velocity and spin are then rotated into the action’s local geometry. This produces outside initial aim → continuous inward bend → intended region. The simulation knows no target after launch.

The Curve mapping is reused exactly, including zero intentional sidespin at Curve 0. The 8 rad/s backspin is a separate fixed aerial-contact component, so total spin magnitude differs from sidespin. Crossing does not change the intended spin magnitude. The approved continuous Magnus world is reused byte-for-byte: constant gain 1, early factor 1, quench 0. No inherited late-bend shaping.

Apex approaches 8 m rather than rising without bound. The tested 20–70 m range gives roughly 5.18–7.32 m apex. The solver search guard is 65 m/s and 36 candidates. Shot Power is not a speed clamp in this provisional range-first Cross experiment.

Open capability question: at 70 m, the frozen drag and this bounded arc require approximately 50.17 m/s at Curve 40, 52.42 at Curve 80, and 57.81 at Curve 99. These are strong, potentially unsuitable game launch speeds. The solver succeeds without reversal, but this is not a claim that the long-range pace is approved or football-realistic. Range, arc or eventual physical capability limits need owner review; shooting capability has not been increased.

Crossing · placement independently of whip

x = ((99 − Crossing) / 89)^2.1
σyaw = (0.018 + 6 × x) × D degrees
σweight = (0.0008 + 0.20 × x) × D
σelevation = (0.012 + 3.5 × x) × D degrees

D = 0.12 + 0.85 × (requestedDistance / 30)^2
         + 0.25 × (contactRedirection / 180°)^2
         + 0.20 × (runnerSpeed / 8.2)^2

The same contact-geometry difficulty formula is used for Through Ball. Contact redirection uses the direct target direction, not the spin-dependent outside launch angle. Curve, Shot Power and solved launch speed never feed back into accuracy. Geometry is a provisional task difficulty factor, not a blend of player attributes. Stronger Curve can change the physical response to a fixed error; it cannot shrink sigma.

Actual speed and elevation receive the scaled locked draws before contact; yaw error rotates both velocity and spin. Cross spin magnitude is preserved. All these actual launches then fly freely. 99 retains a small nonzero error floor. The 65 m/s numerical guard can clip extreme positive weight errors; applied and requested error are both recorded.

Inside foot × pitch side

Attack direction is +x. Right-foot inside contact bends toward the action’s local left; left-foot contact mirrors it. For an opposite-side mirrored scene, the entire trajectory mirrors, including its outside aim. Same-side deliveries read as outswingers; opposite-side deliveries as inswingers. There is no single fixed horizontal world-space bend direction.
