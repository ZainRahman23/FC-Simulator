Direction chooses the teammate. Charge chooses delivery pace. Shot Power scales the extra pace available. Short Passing controls execution. This update changes Short Pass only.

Directional receiver selection

For Short Pass, both teammate markers are candidates. The edit-receiver menu configures their position and motion; the pass itself selects from the aim direction. Candidates must be 0.5–60 m away and within 30° of aim. The lowest score wins:

score = (angleFromAim / 30°)² + 0.15 × (distance / 30 m)

Direction has the stronger influence; equal-angle receivers prefer the nearer player. An exact tie uses stable receiver ID. There is no tactical intelligence. No candidate in the cone means no launch and a clear message. Attributes, hold duration and RNG cannot change selection. The aim ray, all teammate markers, highlighted receiver and selection audit are visible.

Stationary target = selected teammate’s position. Moving target = selected teammate position + velocity × solved travel time. Short Pass has zero extra run-ahead allowance. It meets the player’s natural receiving point; it does not add Through Ball’s deliberate space lead.

The provisional control curve

t = clamp(holdMilliseconds / 350, 0, 1)
q = 1 − (1 − t)²

extraPaceScale(SP) = 0.55 + 0.45 × (SP / 99)^1.5
maximumArrival(SP) = 8 + (14 − 8) × extraPaceScale(SP)
desiredArrival = 8 + q × (maximumArrival(SP) − 8) m/s

There is no minimum-charge dead zone. Even 0 ms requests a complete 8 m/s arrival. Charge begins rising immediately; at 100 ms q is about 0.490, and at 200 ms it is about 0.816. 350 ms is full charge; 400 ms produces the same request. Through Ball and Cross retain their existing 40–500 ms charge model.

All four tested Shot Power ratings produce the exact same ideal tap. Shot Power changes only the available additional pace. These are receiving-point pace ceilings, not fixed launch speeds. A longer pass needs a faster launch to compensate for the frozen turf.

Backward solve and ground contact

For each launch candidate, the real physical ball travels until its horizontal speed reaches the requested arrival pace. Its travelled distance and elapsed time are measured. A safeguarded bracketed secant solve finds:

physicalRange(launchSpeed, desiredArrivalSpeed)
  = distance(origin, receiverPosition + receiverVelocity × physicalTravelTime)

The solver also resolves the player’s maximum-arrival request for the same receiver; this gives a distance-specific maximum launch speed used to cap execution weight. A 32 m/s search envelope prevents unbounded attempts at impossible requests. It is an explicit provisional solver limit, not an invisible increase in Shot Power. In the tested 5–30 m grid, both the requested and maximum pace solve within this envelope.

Short Pass starts with zero vertical velocity and natural rolling spin (ω = horizontal speed / ball radius). Direction and weight errors retain that grounded launch. The frozen physics still owns gravity, drag, Magnus, turf and sleep. There is one physical launch and no post-launch correction, target-dependent friction or receiving integration.

Pace limitation: unchanged turf causes continuous deceleration before and after the receiver. This update ensures useful arrival pace; it does not make the ball constant-speed or delay friction until reception. A 30 m tap drops from about 22.93 m/s at launch to 8.00 m/s at the receiver. Only Short Passing is an accuracy attribute. Long Passing, Vision, Curve, Composure and Shot Power do not enter sigma. The old Short Pass speed-difficulty term is removed. All difficulty geometry is measured at contact, so changing pace cannot feed a different prediction distance into sigma. No charge or firmness penalty is added.

Physical miss can still respond to flight duration, speed and ceiling clipping; that is measured physics, not an accuracy-rating blend. At full charge, positive weight error may be capped. The requested and applied errors are both audited. A nonzero 99 floor remains; no elevation error is introduced.

Determinism, performance and protected systems

Execution keys remain [pass-execution-v1, seed, passerId, eventId, dimension, drawIndex]. SHA-256 supplies the same bounded standardized direction/weight draws. Rating, receiver choice, input source, Shot Power and hold do not reroll the draw. Locking a draw and repeating the same input scales its execution tendency.

At most 28 physical candidate simulations per Short Pass (14 when maximum charge reuses the same solve); early convergence usually uses fewer. The ideal replay and candidate solves run in a worker. Actual play advances one fixed-step 240 Hz physical ball. CPU values are observed local measurements, not frame-budget guarantees.
