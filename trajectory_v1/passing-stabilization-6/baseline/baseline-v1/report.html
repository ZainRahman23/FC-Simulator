Gold is ideal; mint is actual. A receiver is a moving reference, with no receiving animation or possession success rule. Ground, drag, spin and bounce come from the frozen BALL-PHYS / BALL-FEEL-1.4 world.

Architecture & isolation

Human intent → family → bounded ideal physical solve → relevant attribute execution → one scheduled contact → authoritative ball world → measured arrival. The worker prepares the ideal launch; the main thread advances the actual world at a fixed 240 Hz. Rendering reads snapshots. Neither physics world reads a target after contact.

All new work is in this standalone passing folder. The 24-module frozen dependency closure is copied, not edited. No integration with DRIBBLE-V1, RECEIVE-CONTROL, goalkeepers, match AI or production animation. The earlier shooting sandbox and accuracy lab are protected by file hashes.

Exact charge & capability

q = clamp((holdMilliseconds − 40) / 460, 0, 1)
Short arrival speed = 2 + 6 q^1.2 m/s
Through arrival speed = max(3, runnerSpeed + 0.8 + 3q) m/s
Through early-arrival allowance = 0.15 + 0.35q seconds
Ground launch lift = 0.35 + 0.25q m/s
Ground contact spin = 0.96 × rolling spin for actual horizontal velocity

Cross requested apex = max(2.2, 1.2 + 0.09 × targetDistance) × (1.2 − 0.35q) m
Cross arrival height = 1.2 m, descending
Cross backspin = 8 rad/s; zero intentional sidespin

capability(SP) = 0.65 + 0.65 × (SP / 99)²
familyCeiling = 34 × capability(SP) / capability(90) × techniqueFraction
techniqueFraction: Short 0.85; Through 0.90; Cross 0.80

Holds beyond 500 ms retain maximum requested weight. Passing has no shooting overcharge region. Charge never fixes a pass distance: the solver combines it with the intended destination. Crosses become firmer and lower as charge rises; a light cross is more floated. Short and Through use the same turf, with different desired arrival states.

Capability decision: the player schema has Shot Power but no separate outfield kicking-power attribute. The older match passing system combines skill, spatial errors and receipt probabilities; it does not establish a suitable physical passing ceiling. This isolated candidate explicitly reuses the BALL-SHOT-1.2 capability ratio as a provisional shared kicking-strength proxy, with separate pass technique fractions. Passing attributes never change that ceiling or the ideal solve. The shooting implementation is unchanged.

Target and trajectory solving

R = receiver position at contact; V = receiver velocity
A = user aim point; offset = A − R
T(t) = R + offset + V × (t + earlyArrivalAllowance)
Early allowance = 0 for Short/Cross; 0.15 + 0.35q for Through

Ground candidate: simulate a canonical launch until horizontal speed = requested arrival speed.
Measure its range D(s), arrival time t(s) using the actual physical world.
Solve D(s) − |T(t(s)) − origin| = 0 over the allowed launch-speed bracket.
Rotate the final velocity AND spin into the target direction.

Cross candidate: simulate until the descending ball crosses 1.2 m.
Solve [measured range − target distance, measured apex − requested apex] = [0, 0].
Use bounded finite-difference Newton updates of speed and launch elevation.
Ballistic equations provide an initial guess only. Every candidate and final flight is BALL-PHYS.

Ground solving uses at most 15 candidates, including the upper-bound feasibility check. Cross uses at most 27 candidates. No thousands-of-trajectories search. Target range tolerance is 4 cm for ground, 8 cm for cross; cross apex tolerance is 6 cm. A turf contact transition can leave a bounded weight residual; this is labelled approximate rather than silently reported exact.

The through ball arrives a small time ahead of the runner’s projected arrival while still moving. This is a deliberate run-on candidate, not a guaranteed meeting or first touch. A stationary receiver has no lead direction, so Through degenerates to the selected ground point until a run mode is supplied. The runner is never slowed, steered or teleported. Its faster motion generally creates more lead. If the requested weight/range exceeds capability, the closest bounded launch is shown and marked unresolved.

Cross reports receiving-plane state separately from first physical turf landing. The landing marker comes from the world’s first post-flight turf-contact event, not an extrapolated arc. No target attraction, spline, post-launch force or mid-flight spin correction is used. Zero sidespin keeps this candidate independent of the shooting curve calibration; backspin and rolling spin use the existing Magnus/contact implementation.

baseSigma = floor + range × x
finalSigma = baseSigma × difficulty
error = finalSigma × standardizedDraw

Actual speed = clamp(ideal speed × (1 + weight error), 0.5, family ceiling). Cross pitch is clamped to 1–85°. Both requested and applied errors are recorded. Ground vertical launch speed is retained, so poor passing does not introduce a lob. Ground rolling spin follows the executed velocity at contact; cross backspin follows the executed heading. Subsequent spin changes belong to physics.

The easy-pass floor compresses, but does not erase, rating differences. At 10, a clean 5 m ball remains a plausible professional pass; demanding diagonal passes expose much greater error. At 99, each relevant error dimension has a tiny nonzero floor. Distance influences this provisional passing execution-difficulty model and geometric projection; this is separate from, and does not modify, the approved shooting accuracy model.

“Redirection” here means the difference between the requested launch direction and the explicit clean contact-facing direction. It is a geometric demand term, not a weak-foot, body-quality, pressure or fatigue modifier. All constants are available in config.mjs.

Measurement definitions
  • Physical miss: distance between actual and ideal ball centres at the ideal target-plane arrival time. This exposes underhit/overhit depth, including a ball that never reaches the target plane.
  • Lateral / depth: signed components in the ideal pass frame at that same time. The displayed “actual lead point” uses this definition. A separate actual target-plane marker shows where the ball eventually crosses the perpendicular receiving plane.
  • Plane error: actual minus ideal crossing height, lateral position and time. Missing crossing is explicitly null; rest is never presented as a crossing.
  • Ball/runner timing: actual ball plane time minus the runner’s time at the target’s projection onto its path. Negative means ball early. Runner-path offset is shown separately; with off-path human aim, this is not a claimed interception.
  • Cross landing: first physical turf contact after genuine airtime. Receiving height, velocity and ascending/descending state are reported separately.
  • Closest approach: minimum physical ball/constant-velocity receiver separation over the observation window, in the full audit. It does not award possession.


Determinism, telemetry and performance
key = JSON(["pass-execution-v1", matchSeed, passerId, passEventId, dimension, drawIndex])
uniform = (first 52 SHA-256 bits + 0.5) / 2^52
standardizedDraw = √6 × (uniform₀ − uniform₁)
dimension ∈ {direction, weight, elevation}
Independent bounded triangular draws have mean zero and variance one. Rating, family, position and human/AI source do not enter the key. Locking the event holds standardized draws fixed; normal passes advance it. Changing ratings scales the same draw. Repeating an event with identical inputs reproduces launch and the full physical trace. Speed/pitch clamps and turf transitions mean physical miss is not guaranteed strictly monotonic for every individual draw, even though sigma decreases strictly.
Audit records include input family, passer/contact pose, selected receiver position and velocity, aim offset, measured hold, charge, target, capability, ideal launch and arrival, primary attribute, every difficulty term, sigmas, keyed uniforms/hashes, standardized draws, requested/applied errors, actual launch/arrival/landing, same-time errors, runner timing, solver residuals, CPU and candidate count. The 12-entry browser history is session-local.
Times are measured on this local machine; they are observations, not a frame-budget guarantee. Resolver CPU includes the ideal physical replay; trajectory CPU covers candidate simulations. They run in a worker. Actual gameplay advances one physical ball, with no Monte Carlo sweep. Calibration reuses an ideal solve across its ten ratings.

Controls and review questions
WASD moves the passer. Click the pitch to aim; the Pitch click menu also moves passer/receiver. Hold J = Short, K = Through, L = Cross, or use the three buttons. Release to launch. R resets. Select Receiver A/B, choose stationary/forward/diagonal/across movement and jog/run/sprint. Training and Top-down support pointer placement; Cross trajectory shows the vertical flight with separately labelled height and distance scales. Lock a draw, change the relevant rating, then Repeat same input.
  • Is the 2–8 m/s Short arrival-weight range controllable and responsive?
  • Does the Through early-arrival allowance of 0.15–0.50 s give the right run-on feel, especially for sprinting runners?
  • Are the floated-to-firm cross apex and 1.2 m receiving-height profiles useful?
  • Are the provisional technique speed fractions too restrictive for deep/far crosses?
  • Do easy-pass compression and 60/80/99 differences look right with a locked draw?
Known limits: constant-velocity receivers, no acceleration or first touch, clean stationary contact despite simplified setup movement, no defenders/keeper/offside/out-of-play referee, no tactical selection, no pressure/weak-foot/fatigue/Vision execution effects. The ball may leave the pitch; it is not clamped. Preview is a 12-second observation maximum. Difficult or infeasible targets are reported rather than corrected. All passing formula choices await visual review.
